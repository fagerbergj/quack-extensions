# Trade Evaluation in 10-Team Redraft PPR — Research Report

This report covers trade evaluation for a 10-team redraft full-PPR league on Sleeper: rolling waivers, 100 FAAB, 6 of 10 teams make the playoffs, lineup QB/RB/RB/WR/WR/TE/FLEX/K/DEF + 5 bench. All sources were retrieved 2026-09-18 (2026 season, weeks 3–4) and scoped to 2024–2026; foundational methodology predating 2024 is cited with its real publication date and explicitly flagged out-of-window. A recurring structural fact: **nearly every published trade-value chart is calibrated to 12-team, 1QB, full-PPR** — every number below carries the league size/format it came from.

## 1. Decision rules & numeric thresholds

### 1.1 Chart construction & positional scarcity

**How mainstream charts are built (the standard pipeline: projected rest-of-season points → single index → additive sum test).**

- The implied fairness test on every major chart is additive: "just add the value of each player involved in a trade on both sides to see who wins… these are not salary-cap values or FAB dollars" ([FantasyPros, Week 1 2021 chart explainer, Sept 2021 — out-of-window](https://www.fantasypros.com/2021/09/week-1-fantasy-football-trade-value-chart-2021/)). The same additive framing persists in the [2026 Week 2 chart (Sept 2026)](https://www.fantasypros.com/2026/09/fantasy-football-trade-value-chart-week-2-2026/), where values come from analysts' combined rankings — **black-box** (no published formula; page JS-gated, lede snippet-verified).
- [Roto Street Journal, Week 2 2026 chart (Chase Marquette-Gaines, Sep 15, 2026)](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2026-09-15/2026-fantasy-football-week-2-trade-value-chart/): values "built from The Wolf's Rest Of Season 2026 Rankings" (2nd of 172 experts in FantasyPros' Multi-Year Draft Accuracy, 2022–2024) and "tailored to a **12-team, full-PPR, 1QB** league" — the 12-team tag matters for this league.
- [CBS Sports, Dave Richards' chart (2024-08, 2026 edition live)](https://www.cbssports.com/fantasy/football/news/fantasy-football-2024-week-1-trade-chart-and-rest-of-season-rankings-help-you-win-now-before-the-games-begin/): "Values are determined by expected future performance, future schedule and, most importantly, **public sentiment**. Past performance isn't a major factor." **Black-box.** CBS added half-PPR and 4-point-pass-TD columns only in [2025](https://www.cbssports.com/fantasy/football/news/fantasy-football-2025-week-10-trade-chart-rest-of-season-rankings-will-help-at-your-trade-deadline/).
- [PFF trade value chart (Tyler Loechner, Sept 6, 2017 — out-of-window, still the template)](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-trade-value-chart-what-are-the-fantasy-values-heading-into-week-1): values from rest-of-season projections, "assume a 12-team league and a starting lineup of 1 QB, 2 RB, 2 WR, 1 TE, and 1 Flex"; publishes both standard and PPR columns.
- [FanDuel Research / numberFire (Week 4, 2025)](https://www.fanduel.com/research/fantasy-football-trade-value-chart-week-4-2025): values "come straight from the models of numberFire's rest-of-season… projections (and account for positional scarcity)… entirely based on rest-of-season, game-by-game projections rather than gut feel or optimistic upside."

**The market-derived alternative (real trades, not projections).**

- [FantasyCalc](https://fantasycalc.com/about): "algorithmically generated rankings and trade values calculated from **7,211,577 real trades**… run… through an optimization algorithm… Automatically updated multiple times per day." Its [API docs](https://fantasycalc.com/api-docs) expose `numTeams` as a first-class parameter — i.e., **market values are computed separately per league size**; this is the only major source showing size-adjusted values rather than one 12-team table.
- [Stats Guy Fantasy methodology](https://statsguyfantasy.com/methodology/how-values-work): "treat every completed trade as an approximate equation… when you solve all of those equations **simultaneously** (least-squares fitting), the noise cancels out," with trade-age weighting; explicit cost: "after major breaking news it takes **a day or two** of post-news trading before values fully adjust."
- [4for4, Alex Korff (Aug 23, 2022 — out-of-window, most detailed published human formula)](https://www.4for4.com/2022/preseason/fantasy-football-trade-value-models-what-why-how-and-who-cares): uses **RB as the currency** ("running backs are by far the rarest and greatest positional need"), standard scoring as base with a "PPR correction database… a function of their targets, catches, team pass attempts"; assumes 12-team, 1QB/2RB/2WR/1TE/1Flex. He also states the family's known failure: "most trade value charts have a hard time with cross-positional values and with quantifying value at the top and bottom."

**The VOR / replacement-value family (explicit points-above-replacement math).**

- [PFF, Mike Clay (May 27, 2015 — out-of-window, framework still PFF's stated one)](https://www.pff.com/news/the-value-of-replacement-level): replacement level = "a player who is on your bench or near the top of the waiver wire"; 12-team **non-PPR** baselines: QB18 / RB30 / WR42 / TE18 ≈ 16.0 / 8.5 / 7.0 / 5.5 points/week.
- [LineupLab VOR guide (updated Sept 3, 2026)](https://www.lineuplab.ai/guides/value-over-replacement-fantasy-football): "VOR = player projected points − replacement player projected points… so two sites can calculate different values **without using different player projections**." Public board (12-team, 1QB, full PPR): **QB12 / RB30 / WR42 / TE12**; warns larger rosters "can remove otherwise useful backups from waivers."
- [SportsMonkie trade calculator (undated, small independent site — flagged, but formula fully published)](https://sportsmonkie.com/tools/fantasy-football-trade-calculator/): surplus = raw − replacement (the last player rostered at that position in your league size, flex filled by best remaining); package discount `value(i) × λ^(i−1)` with **λ = 0.66 (8-team), 0.75 (12-team), 0.85 (16-team)** → in a 12-team league the 2nd asset counts at **75%**, 3rd at **56%**, 4th at **42%**; 1QB replacement ≈ the **23rd-best QB** vs superflex ≈ **45th**.

**Positional scarcity in this league.**

- [Footballguys, Adam Harstad (Aug 27, 2020 — out-of-window, the foundational arithmetic, method transparent)](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2020-positional-scarcity): weekly NFL supply ≈ **32 QB, 40 RB, 80 WR, 40 TE**; "most leagues start… **60–70% of available running backs, but under 50% of available players at every other position**"; "on average, **14 of the first 24** players selected… are running backs"; league-size sensitivity stated directly — in an 8-team league only 40% of the RB pool is used and "8-team leagues tend to increase the value of top performers regardless of position."
- **Applying that arithmetic to this league (derived, inputs from Harstad's supply figures):** 10 QB / 32 (≈31%), **20 RB / 40 (50%)** before flex, 20 WR / 80 (25%), 10 TE / 40 (25%). RB is still the scarcest starter position, less extreme than 12-team (24 RBs started, 60%).
- [PFF, Nathan Jahnke (Aug 17, 2026 — exact format match: 10-team, 1QB, PPR redraft)](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-2026-perfect-draft-strategy-round-by-round-for-10-team-leagues): "**Roughly 12 or 13 of the first 22 picks should be running backs**" → only ~12–13 viable RBs in a 10-team PPR league (vs 14–18 typically in 12-team); "a 10-team league has room for three set-and-forget wide receivers… build that core in Rounds 3 through 6." Built from consensus ADPs (ESPN/Sleeper/Yahoo); some detail paywalled.
- [Draft Sharks, Matt Schauf (Sept 2, 2026 — 10-team PPR)](https://www.draftsharks.com/article/fantasy-football-draft-strategy-guide/10-team-ppr): "easier in a 10-team league for every manager to start out with a team he/she likes… so you can afford to go after a top QB and/or TE… chase ultimate upside"; "This format favors **opportunity volume**." (Caveat: that guide assumes a 3-WR lineup; this league is 2-WR+flex — treat directionally.) The companion [standard-scorer 10-team guide (Shane Hallam, Sep 2, 2026)](https://www.draftsharks.com/article/fantasy-football-draft-strategy-guide/10-team-standard) makes the same point: "safe picks leave you with the same solid lineup as everyone else, so **the edge comes from chasing optimal performers**."
- Scarcity-ratio metric: [Draft Value Analytics (undated, no byline — indicative only)](https://draftvalueanalytics.com/positional-scarcity-metrics): Nth starter's points ÷ marginal starter at 1.5× starting spots; "**above 1.25** signals premium territory, **below 1.10** suggests depth to defer"; "a 16-team league exhausts positional depth 60% faster than a 10-team league."
- **Format consequence (synthesis of the above, each input sourced):** elite RB value runs *above* 12-team charts and mid-tier RB *below* them (better 10-team waiver wire); WR is the deepest, most liquid pool (80/week supply); TE replacement at ~TE12 in PPR means mid-tier TEs ≈ replacement and only elite TEs are trade-worthy ([LineupLab, Sept 3, 2026](https://www.lineuplab.ai/guides/value-over-replacement-fantasy-football)); QBs are the *least* scarce position despite high points (32/week supply, still-starting free replacement — [SportsMonkie](https://sportsmonkie.com/tools/fantasy-football-trade-calculator/)).
- **Gap (honest):** no source publishes a replacement-level baseline or trade-value chart specific to 10-team, 2-RB/2-WR+flex PPR; the 10-team picture is derived from Harstad's parametric arithmetic + PFF's 10-team tier count, which is how PFF itself derives it.

### 1.2 Consolidation: the 2-for-1 rules

- **1.3×→1.0× stud sliding scale.** [PeakedinHighSkool (Jul 27, 2022 — out-of-window, dynasty 1-QB polls, model Patreon-gated — flagged)](https://peakedinhighskool.com/2022-07-27/analyzing-2-for-1-dynasty-trades-in-terms-of-trade-values/): for 2-for-1 trades the overpay "is a function of the stud value" — "a sliding scale starting at a **1.3 overpay rate for the tippy top** that gradually drops to **1.0 for lower players or non-studs**." Worked example: a player charted at 83 requires two outgoing players totaling ≈108. Validated against crowd-sourced poll data with [FantasyPros' June 2021 trade values](https://www.fantasypros.com/2021/06/fantasy-football-rankings-dynasty-trade-value-chart-june-2021-update/).
- **82% second-asset weight (format-scaled).** [Fantasy Trade Pilot 2-for-1 calculator (values updated Aug 7, 2026 — values themselves black-box, "directional estimates")](https://fantasytradepilot.com/2-for-1-fantasy-football-trade-calculator): "The highest-value asset receives full weight. **The second asset receives 82% by default.** Deeper formats may justify a smaller discount; **shallow starting lineups may require a larger one**" — i.e., a 10-team start-9 discounts the second piece at least ~18%. Two further rules: **price the forced cut** ("A two-for-one deal also forces a cut… Include the value of the dropped player") and "**measure the second upgrade, not the second player**" (compare the incoming player against whoever currently occupies that lineup spot).
- **λ decay (fully published formula).** [SportsMonkie (undated — flagged)](https://sportsmonkie.com/tools/fantasy-football-trade-calculator/): in 12-team the 2nd asset counts at 75%, 3rd at 56%, 4th at 42%; λ drops to 0.66 at 8 teams and rises to 0.85 at 16 — the *shallower* the league, the *stronger* the consolidation discount.
- **Why the premium is largest at RB.** [FFTradeAnalyzer position-scarcity guide (undated — flagged)](https://www.fftradeanalyzer.com/guides/position-scarcity): "The RB12 might score 15 PPG while RB24 scores 8 PPG — a 7-point gap. The WR12 scores 14 PPG while WR24 scores 11 PPG — only 3 points," and "a WR2 for RB2 swap typically favors whoever gets the RB"; "Only **8–12 RBs consistently get 15+ touches per game**."
- **The flaw in the mainstream math, stated by its builder.** [4for4/Korff (Aug 23, 2022)](https://www.4for4.com/2022/preseason/fantasy-football-trade-value-models-what-why-how-and-who-cares): lopsided 3-for-1 trades break naive additive math (excluded as "much more difficult"), which is why cross-positional correction is needed.
- **When taking two *is* the right move** ([Fantasy Trade Pilot](https://fantasytradepilot.com/2-for-1-fantasy-football-trade-calculator)): you have two genuine weaknesses; both incoming roles are startable; replacement options are poor (shallow league); you're rebuilding and get a liquid asset; or "the package includes enough premium to compensate for consolidation."

### 1.3 Roster fit & win-now vs depth

**Fit adjustments.**

- **15–25% roster-need band.** [Draft Value Charts Explained (undated — flagged, indicative)](https://draftvalueanalytics.com/trade-value-charts-explained): chart value "doesn't capture roster-need premium, which can legitimately shift the fair trade price by **15 to 25 percent**" on an imbalanced roster.
- **The reversal rule.** [FantasyStrategyGuide trade strategy guide (undated — flagged)](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/trade-strategy-guide): "a team already carrying 4 solid running backs gets no marginal benefit from adding a 5th. **Roster context can reverse the scarcity premium entirely**." Its execution rule: "If the offer is **within 10–15% of fair value** by the best available metric and addresses a real need, **execution is generally superior to prolonged negotiation**."
- **The mutual-need rule (most-cited mainstream version).** [RSJ, Sep 15, 2026](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2026-09-15/2026-fantasy-football-week-2-trade-value-chart/): "trade offers that clearly benefit both teams' overall value, not just your own, will make a trade partner more cooperative… **Be mindful not just of weak positional depth, but also of surplus positional depth across your league's rosters**… if what you have to offer isn't what your trade partner needs, your offers will likely fall on deaf ears."
- **Don't over-trade RB insurance.** [FantasyStrategyGuide (undated — flagged, qualitative claim, no study cited)](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/trade-strategy-guide): "Trading all RB depth before week 10 is a well-documented path to roster crisis" (RB carries the highest in-season injury frequency among skill positions).
- **Duplicate-position anti-pattern.** [FantasyPros, Trading 101 (2026-07 — JS-gated, snippet-verified)](https://www.fantasypros.com/2026/07/fantasy-football-trading-101-how-to-make-a-trade-2026/): "trading for Lamar Jackson when you already have Josh Allen probably isn't a good idea. Nor is giving up Jahmyr Gibbs if it will leave you without a viable RB2."

**Win-now vs depth — the two positions, both sourced.**

- **Win-now / consolidate:** [FFTradeAnalyzer, Playoff Trading Strategy (undated — flagged)](https://www.fftradeanalyzer.com/guides/playoff-trades): "If you're a contender in **Weeks 10–14, you're no longer building for the season — you're optimizing for Weeks 15–17 only**. Future value means nothing if you don't make playoffs. **Trade depth for stars**… **Prioritize ceiling over floor. Reduce bench depth**… **Handcuff your stars**." Also: trade eliminated teams for their studs; "a 2-10 team doesn't need Ja'Marr Chase for one more week. Package depth players… they'll appreciate the rebuild pieces."
- **Data-backed concentration:** [PFF-derived "What 5 Years of Winners Tell Us" (Aug 17, 2026 — snippet-verified)](https://profootballmania.com/2026-08-17/fantasy-football-draft-strategy-championship-data/): the ~50 players most commonly on championship rosters across 2022–2025 (200 player-seasons) show winning rosters concentrate elite assets rather than spread middling ones.
- **Deadline week-14 playbook:** [DraftSharks Week 10 targets (Matt Schauf, Nov 6, 2025)](https://www.draftsharks.com/article/fantasy-football-trade-targets-sells-and-holds-week-10): "Every move has to be focused on getting your team into the postseason and then winning that league title" — including selling a hot star to buy the piece that actually wins; [USA TODAY, Joe Bond (Dec 1, 2025)](https://www.usatoday.com/story/sports/fantasy/football/2025-12-01/fantasy-football-week-14-buy-low-sell-high/87547470007/): must-win teams trade players on bye for active pieces "even if that means losing the trade on paper."
- **Depth / hold-the-star:** [RSJ, "Anatomy of a Tier Down" (Tyler Sutphen, Aug 5, 2026)](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2026-08-05/anatomy-tier-down/): "**In shallow formats like a 10-team, start-9 league, weekly ceiling is everything. Stars win those titles. If you trade a blue-chip superstar for three average starting options, you just traded a dollar for four quarters**… Flip that script in a 14-team, start-11 format." Quantified inefficiency behind it: the WR5-to-WR10 gap is "often less than one full point per week" yet WR5 market-costs ~three first-round picks vs one for WR10. [The Tennessee Trader (Steve Margolis, Sep 11, 2026 — practitioner opinion, not data-backed — flagged)](https://thetennesseetrader.substack.com/p/stop-trading-dollars-for-quarters): "In shallow leagues, **elite studs are the only currency that matters**."
- **Published crossover threshold (unverified at source — flagged):** [FantasyStrategyGuide, early vs late season (undated)](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/early-season-vs-late-season-strategy/): "when a team's projected win probability for the week falls below 60%, adding a high-variance player rather than a high-floor one typically improves expected wins" — attributed to "research… documented in FiveThirtyEight's sports analytics coverage"; the underlying FiveThirtyEight piece could not be retrieved, so treat the 60% figure as a secondary attribution.

### 1.4 Standings & playoff-position modifiers

**The quantified win-now premium (methodology transparent, model linked).**

- [Footballguys, Adam Harstad, "Dynasty, in Theory: Let's Talk About Win-Now Trades" (09/21/2024)](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2024-dynasty-in-theory-lets-talk-about-win-now-trades): in a typical 12-team league, a **guaranteed regular-season win lifts title odds by ~2%**; top weekly score in **Week 16/17** lifts title odds **>8%**, top score in **Week 15** lifts them **>4%** — playoff-week points are worth roughly **4–8× a regular-season week's** for title purposes (12-team with bye; the direction "scales to 6-of-10, which makes playoffs *harder* to reach, not easier," but the exact multipliers are 12-team-tagged). **Marginal wins are worth more approaching the bye line**: 1st guaranteed win +2.01%, 2nd +2.21%, 3rd +2.31% title odds — "a team one win from a bye should pay more per win than a team four wins from it." For a strong team, a guaranteed win in the **finals is worth 17.88% title odds vs ~1.7% in the regular season** (~10×).
- [Footballguys, Buzzard14, Monte Carlo payout weighting (08/11/2014 — out-of-window, the only published week-weighting by record strength)](https://www.footballguys.com/article/buzzard14_Strategies_to_win_your_league): for a 12-team, 6-make league, playoff weeks worth **1.8× / 4.0× / 1.6×** a regular week (Weeks 14/15/16) for an average team, rising to **2.7× / 7.4× / 3.2×** for a 6-2 team; printed rule of thumb: "**value playoff weeks at ~3× regular-season points**" and re-derive as your record improves.
- **The counterweight (published data):** [South Harmon FF, T-Rock, "Playoff Odds & Strategy — Part II: Trades" (Jan 22, 2025 cycle; 25 seasons of dynasty data, 56 analyzed trades)](https://www.southharmonff.com/articles/playoff-odds-amp-strategy-1): "**82.14%** of analyzed playoff-window trades had **ZERO impact** on the outcome"; only **3.58% (2 of 56) directly affected a title**; 2 trades cost their owners the championship by moving players away.

**The target format's actual rules (official Sleeper docs).**

- [Sleeper Support, "How do playoff teams get determined?" (Dec 7, 2020)](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/2203518-how-do-playoff-teams-get-determined): in division-less leagues, top N by **Overall Record > Points For > Points Against**; in 6-team playoffs **the bye goes to the top-2 seeds**. [Sleeper blog, "How Do Fantasy Football Playoffs Work?" (Avi Creditor, Dec 9, 2024)](https://sleeper.com/blog/how-do-fantasy-playoffs-work/): 6-team playoffs start Week 15, top-2 seeds get byes. [Sleeper blog (Josh Larky, Aug 30, 2024)](https://sleeper.com/blog/what-happens-if-you-tie-fantasy-football/): tiebreaks are **total points scored, then points against** — not head-to-head; in playoffs the higher seed advances a tied matchup.
- **Consequence for 6-of-10 (derived from the Sleeper rules above):** the seed-1/seed-2 bye is the single biggest cliff in this format; a team on the 2–3 seed line is buying "skip the Week 15 game," while a team on the 5–6 line is buying "one more week of life"; a 7th-position team in redraft has **zero remaining season value** — its only leverage is losing the trade and feeling good.
- **Published seed-outcome data (12-team dynasty sample — flagged as extrapolation):** [South Harmon FF, T-Rock, Part I (Jan 21, 2025 cycle; 25 seasons)](https://www.southharmonff.com/articles/playoff-odds-amp-strategy): 1st seed reaches the final 75% of the time (avg finish 2.17); the 3rd seed had the **highest championship odds in the sample (33.33%)**; 4th place finished bottom-half **72%** of the time; **no 6th seed ever won the title in the sample**; "Not much differentiates 4th–6th seeded teams" — past the bye line, scoring strength (PF) matters more than seed number, which is exactly what the Sleeper PF tiebreaker rewards.
- **Playoff-odds, not seed, is the right object:** [ffwrapped, Kevin Tian, "Fantasy Football Playoff Odds" (undated — method on-page, 5,000 Monte Carlo sims)](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/fantasy-football-playoff-odds): "standings mislead; a 6-5 team with strong scoring + easy schedule can outrank a 7-4 team." Worked example: one win moved playoff odds **33.6% → 48.4% (+14.8pp)**; the corresponding loss cost 9.5pp. Its companion piece ([ffwrapped, "Why Your League Settings Matter," undated](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/fantasy-football-trade-values)) gives the 10-team adaptation anchor: trade value = projected points − replacement points, **with replacement derived from your league's size** — replacement level is *higher* in a 10-team league, so chart points are compressed; K/DEF discounted as streamable.
- **The rebuild pivot line:** [CalculatorCollection playoff odds calculator (last updated Sept 2026 — formula on-page)](https://www.calculatorcollection.org/en/calculators/fantasy-sports/playoff-odds-calculator/): base probability = playoff spots / teams (6-of-10 = 60%); "**if playoff probability is below 15–20% with fewer than 5 weeks remaining, the math generally suggests pivoting to a rebuild** rather than an all-in push."
- **Position → posture (dated rules):** [fftradeanalyzer.com, 5-step evaluation checklist (2026-05-20)](https://www.fftradeanalyzer.com/news/how-to-use-trade-analyzer-tools-effectively): "Contender → prioritize points in the starting lineup and playoff-week availability. Rebuilder → prioritize durable value, youth, and future picks. Middle roster → 'avoid paying contender prices before deciding whether the team can realistically compete.'" [MFL Fantasy trade strategy (undated — flagged)](https://mflfantasyfootball.com/trade-strategy): "Above .500 is a win-now buyer. Below is a rebuild seller… Locked in = focused on Weeks 15–17. Fighting for a spot = focused now. **Eliminated = open to anything**."

**Season stage — how value shifts across the calendar.**

- [FantasyPros, Trade Advice series (2026-09 — lede snippet-verified)](https://www.fantasypros.com/2026-09/fantasy-football-trade-advice-players-to-buy-sell-or-hold-2026/): react only to things "at least hopefully likely to be sticky"; the first 16 games are "**nowhere near a large enough sample** to confidently draw conclusions" — in weeks 2–4 (where this league currently is), discount single-week swings heavily. Same rule in operation at [Footballguys buy-low/sell-high (Sigmund Bloom, 2026-09-17)](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2026-buy-low-sell-high-week02).
- [Matchup Analytics (undated — flagged)](https://matchupanalytics.com/trade-value-and-matchup-context): "In redraft, matchup context operates on a **2–4 week window**… the buy-low window opens when a skilled player has endured 2–3 rough weeks" attributable to schedule, not role.
- **Chart mechanics:** every 2026 chart is a rest-of-season chart, so values *mechanically shrink* as the season ends ([RSJ, Sep 15, 2026](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2026-09-15/2026-fantasy-football-week-2-trade-value-chart/); [CBS Week 2 2026 (Sep 15, 2026 — lede snippet-verified)](https://www.cbssports.com/fantasy/football/news/dave-richards-week-2-trade-chart-and-rest-of-season-fantasy-football-rankings-help-you-win-now/); [FanDuel/numberFire Week 1 2025](https://www.fanduel.com/research/fantasy-football-trade-value-chart-week-1-2025)) — all 12-team-calibrated (flag).

(Apr 22, 2026)](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/2435411-when-is-my-trade-deadline): the deadline is tied to the **final game of the selected NFL week** — trading stays open through all games that week; commissioners can change the week or **turn the deadline off entirely**, and accepted trades still process their review period. [Sleeper Support, "How to Trade" (Mar 19, 2024)](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3188802-how-to-trade): trades may include players, draft picks, **and FAAB dollars**. ### 1.5 League fairness & collusion norms

**What counts as collusion (convergent across platform policies and case law).**

- [ESPN Fair Play/Conduct policy (undated platform doc, current as of 2026-09-18)](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003903111-Fair-Play-Conduct-Collusive-Transaction): "Collusive Transactions: When one team makes moves to benefit another team **without trying to improve its own position**" (named examples: one-sided trades; dropping a player so a partner can claim it); "Standard League members are expected to **veto/protest all unfair/collusive trades**. **Four votes** are required in a Standard League for a trade to be denied."
- [NFL.com Fair Play and User Conduct Policy (undated)](https://support.nfl.com/hc/en-us/articles/4989074619804-Fair-Play-and-User-Conduct-Policy): collusion "strictly prohibited and grounds for immediate dismissal"; dropping all/most players near-simultaneously = dismissal; multiple-team management prohibited; the NFL is "100% hands-off" in custom leagues — commissioning is delegated.
- [Yahoo Fantasy fair play rules (undated)](https://help.yahoo.com/kb/fantasy-football/fair-play-rules-penalties-yahoo-fantasy-public-leagues-sln6215.html): four rules — no collusive action; no malicious drops/dumps; no moves whose purpose is to hinder fair play; no moves that intentionally hamper other managers; "Yahoo won't interfere in league disputes" in private leagues — all norms fall to the commissioner.
- **Canonical waiver-collusion case:** [PFF reprint, "Supreme Court of Fantasy Judgment," *Steel Curtain v. Rusty Trombones* (Nov 12, 2011)](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-fantasy-judgment-decision-in-fantasy-football-collusion-case): a top waiver-priority team claimed a player solely to trade it to a partner who couldn't claim it; the trade itself was fair, but the **prearranged waiver-order exploitation was collusion**; remedies: player frozen while held, and the two teams **barred from trading with each other for the remainder of the season**. Same page states the industry norm: trade review "based purely on its fairness, free from collusion, and in the best interests of the league" — **freedom of team management is the default; intervention is the exception.**
- **Evidence threshold:** [SportsJudge Index No. 3184-T, Judge Marc Edelman (June 2, 2009)](https://sportsjudge.blogspot.com/2010/07/sportsjudgecom-indec-no-3184-t-court.html): a coordinated add→trade was *not* collusion because the players were near-even in value, each team had a self-interested motive, and league impact was *de minimis*; working definition: collusion is where "the underlying motivation of at least one team owner is to not act in his own best interest." The page documents a sample league clause with a **quantified veto threshold: 4 non-involved teams objecting within 24 hours nullifies the trade.**
- **Prevention norms (commissioner advice):** [FanDraft (May 1, 2020)](https://fandraft.com/blog/preventingcollusion): don't set next year's draft order by end-of-year standings (tank incentive — use a loser bracket); stack **double-headers in the final two weeks** so more teams keep playoff hope; **early trade deadline, weeks 8–10**. [RotoPicks (undated, site © 2013 — flagged)](http://www.rotopicks.com/nfl/collusion.php): a **don't-drop list** (top 75–100 can't be waived absent a league-approved reason) and a **tiered trade deadline** — late in the season, "teams can only make trades with teams one or two places above or below them." [Fantasy Strategy Guide, collusion & ethics (undated — flagged)](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/collusion-and-ethics-in-fantasy/): the informal **8-of-10 rule** ("if a trade would be universally rejected by 8 out of 10 experienced managers in the same league context, it warrants review") and the **competitive-intent test**: "was this trade made with genuine intent to improve the manager's competitive standing?"
- **Definition that matters for an AI assistant:** [RandomDraftOrder, Ryan Glab (Nov 25, 2025)](https://www.randomdraftorder.com/articles/fantasy-football-disputes/): collusion = "any agreement **outside the trade itself** (future draft picks, cash, non-fantasy favors, trade-back promises)"; investigation = privately request both managers' chat logs ("if they refuse, it's a major red flag") and test for rational self-interest; the named red-flag pattern: "**teams mathematically eliminated trading away best assets** for injuries/bench fillers to a contender." [ESPN Commissioner Court, Randy Scott (Nov 13, 2015)](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/14119741/collusion-quitters-more-fantasy-football): coined "**fairness isn't the same as legality**" — a laughably one-sided trade stands (the "that's what you get" precedent).
- **Sleeper mechanics (official):** [Sleeper Support, "How to Trade" (Mar 19, 2024)](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3188802-how-to-trade) — accepted trades are **approved by default** through a configurable review period; the commissioner can veto, force through, or **reverse a completed trade**. [Sleeper Support, "Can I veto a trade?" (Jul 9, 2022)](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3200544-can-i-veto-a-trade) — two supported models (commissioner cancel and/or league-wide vote); "The commissioner still has ultimate control… at any time." [Sleeper Support, "Can I disable trading?" (Mar 20, 2024)](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/4258022-can-i-disable-trading) — no kill switch; the sanctioned workaround is a Week 9 deadline + max **3-day (72-hour) review** + vetoes.
- **The classic 10-team veto threshold:** [ESPN RULES: TRADES, Free Standard Leagues (page dated 2009-03-10, still published)](https://www.espn.com.sg/fantasy/football/ffl/story?page=fflrulestradesstandard2009): **48-hour review window, 4 of 10 managers vote to veto** (40%); a trade accepted Friday afternoon processes Sunday evening — players switch owners for the next scoring week, not mid-week.
- No authoritative "no trading with eliminated teams after week N" rule exists in any retrieved source; the published norm is incentive design + commissioner scrutiny of eliminated-team transactions late in the season ([WeekOneFantasy (undated — flagged)](https://www.weekonefantasy.com/what-is-collusion-in-fantasy-football-4142967/); [Glab, Nov 25, 2025](https://www.randomdraftorder.com/articles/fantasy-football-disputes/)).

### 1.6 The persuasive, honest write-up & when to counter

- **Price both sides on one named, consistent metric** — the de-facto public price is a consensus anchor such as FantasyPros ECR; [FantasyStrategyGuide trade strategy (undated — flagged)](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/trade-strategy): identify roster holes → surplus audit → partner's holes → "price both sides against consensus" → **adjust for context** (scoring format, remaining schedule, injury risk, redraft vs dynasty) → "Trade value charts… reflect consensus, not context."
- **State the net-value band explicitly.** [Draft Value Analytics (undated — flagged)](https://draftvalueanalytics.com/trade-value-charts-explained): "**trades within 10 to 15 percent of chart parity are functionally equivalent**, meaning the chart can't distinguish between them reliably." Competing band: [SportsMonkie (undated)](https://sportsmonkie.com/tools/fantasy-football-trade-calculator/): "A gap under **5% is noise**… Under 10%, it is close enough that **your own roster should break the tie**. Anything past **30%** is the kind of offer you send once and do not mention again."
- **Write the lineup before and after.** [SharkSnip trade-analyzer math (undated blog — flagged)](https://sharksnip.com/blog/fantasy-trade-analyzer-math): "write the lineup before and after the trade. Name every starter, the first replacement, and the roster spot you would cut." The commercial direction agrees: [RotoWire trade analyzer (Aug 7, 2026 — JS-gated, snippet-verified, black-box "adjusted value")](https://www.rotowire.com/football/article/fantasy-football-trade-analyzer-calculator-august-7-2026-126731) "weighs one high-end player above several lower-value players with the same point total, since a true difference-maker is harder to replace than roster depth."
- **Price the overpay explicitly for consolidation, and name the forced cut** — 1.3×→1.0× stud scale ([PeakedinHighSkool, Jul 27, 2022](https://peakedinhighskool.com/2022-07-27/analyzing-2-for-1-dynasty-trades-in-terms-of-trade-values/)) or 82% second-asset weight ([Fantasy Trade Pilot, values Aug 7, 2026](https://fantasytradepilot.com/2-for-1-fantasy-football-trade-calculator/)).
- **Quantify floor/ceiling and risk, not just projection.** [NovaPredict (Jun 30, 2026)](https://novapredict.com/blog/fantasy-football-trade-value-chart): "A player is not a single value. He is a range of outcomes, a floor, a median, a ceiling, and real odds of a boom or a bust… Trade the range, not the name."
- **Cite numbers, never as gospel.** [RSJ, Sep 15, 2026](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2026-09-15/2026-fantasy-football-week-2-trade-value-chart/): charts "are a reference… **not gospel**, and trying to make your potential trade partner think otherwise could shut the door on negotiations real quick."
- **Anchor above target and concede; let it sit.** [RSJ, Aug 5, 2026](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2026-08-05/anatomy-tier-down/): "Always start with an anchor offer… Make them talk you down to the deal you actually wanted… **never start with your best offer**," plus a "silence strategy" (let the offer sit; urgency shifts to the side needing the player).
- **Let the other side feel like it won a little.** [The Devy Royale (Jay Stein, Jul 27, 2026 — paywalled past §2)](https://www.thedevyroyale.com/p/the-art-of-the-trade-a-handbook-for): endowment effect (Kahneman/Knetsch/Thaler mug experiment — owners priced ~2× buyers) and loss aversion (~2×: "a perfectly fair 1-for-1 offer dies"), so "the other manager usually needs to feel like he's winning by a little"; find deals "where the piece you're giving up matters less to your roster construction than the piece you're getting matters to yours."
- **Accept vs counter vs walk.** Accept when "within **10–15%** of fair value… and addresses a real need" ([FantasyStrategyGuide, undated](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/trade-strategy)); the only published numeric counter rule found: [Footballguys "Gut Check No. 278" (Waldman, circa 2013 — out-of-window, the fullest published negotiation framework)](https://www.footballguys.com/article/gutcheck278tradeadvice?article=gutcheck278tradeadvice): open with a **15–20% discount** off the offer's value, "know your boundaries before you enter any negotiation and… be committed to sticking to them," and decline on good terms. [StrategyFantasyFootball (Feb 9, 2025)](https://www.strategyfantasyfootball.com/articles/fantasy-football-trade-etiquette-tips/): respond within a day or two, even to say no; "If your first offer is rejected… use counter-offers to keep the conversation going." [FantasyStrategyGuide](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/trade-strategy): "Reassess in **72 hours** — a rejected trade on Monday may make sense by Thursday after a new injury report."
- **When NOT to deal.** [RSJ, Sep 15, 2026](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2026-09-15/2026-fantasy-football-week-2-trade-value-chart/): offers "so clearly in your favor" that they'd pass only ~1% of the time "likely aren't worth hitting the send button" (relationship capital). [ESPN 101 etiquette (Jun 30, 2022)](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/19577495/fantasy-football-101-fantasy-football-etiquette): lowballing is fine "if you intend to up the ante to actually pull off a deal"; "people who send gobs of horrible trade offers typically get shunned… and sometimes get booted."

## 2. Where experts disagree

**2.1 What the numbers are grounded in — projections, the market, or explicit VOR math.**
- **Consensus expert rankings / ROS projections:** [FantasyPros, Sept 2026](https://www.fantasypros.com/2026/09/fantasy-football-trade-value-chart-week-1-2026/) (analysts' combined rankings); [RSJ, Sept 2025](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2025-09-16/2025-fantasy-football-trade-value-chart-week-2-quarterbacks-not-ready-to-publish/) (VBD tool off FantasyPros ECR + Wolf ROS); [CBS, 2024-08](https://www.cbssports.com/fantasy/football/news/fantasy-football-2024-week-1-trade-chart-and-rest-of-season-rankings-help-you-win-now-before-the-games-begin/) (future performance + schedule + sentiment); [FanDuel/numberFire, 2025](https://www.fanduel.com/research/fantasy-football-trade-value-chart-week-4-2025) (game-by-game models). RSJ itself concedes its chart "should closely reflect consensus rankings" — rankings-in-disguise.
- **Completed real trades (market):** [FantasyCalc](https://fantasycalc.com/about) (7.2M trades, per-league-size); [Stats Guy methodology](https://statsguyfantasy.com/methodology/how-values-work) (least-squares on trades, self-corrects to what managers actually paid but lags news by a day or two); [4for4/Korff, Aug 23, 2022](https://www.4for4.com/2022/preseason/fantasy-football-trade-value-models-what-why-how-and-who-cares) (RB-currency calibrated from hundreds of 1-for-1 swaps).
- **Explicit VOR over replacement level:** [PFF/Clay, 2015-05-27](https://www.pff.com/news/the-value-of-replacement-level); [LineupLab, Sept 3, 2026](https://www.lineuplab.ai/guides/value-over-replacement-fantasy-football) (same projections, different baselines → different values); [SportsMonkie](https://sportsmonkie.com/tools/fantasy-football-trade-calculator/) (published formula).
- Conditions: projection charts lead the market on fresh news; market values self-correct but lag; VOR is the only family where the league-size/roster-size dependence is explicit math.

**2.2 How much recent performance should count.**
- **Past performance isn't a major factor** — future performance + schedule + sentiment: [CBS, 2024-08](https://www.cbssports.com/fantasy/football/news/fantasy-football-2024-week-1-trade-chart-and-rest-of-season-rankings-help-you-win-now-before-the-games-begin/).
- **Form-weighted weekly moves** — "Justin Fields leads all QBs in fantasy points over the past five weeks… he moves up," with explicit "Be cautious" flags on 4–5-week-sample rookies: [ESPN/Eric Karabell, Week 11, 2022 (out-of-window, flagged)](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/34980305/fantasy-football-trade-value-2022-updated-rankings).
- **Pure model, no sentiment** — "entirely based on rest-of-season, game-by-game projections rather than gut feel": [FanDuel/numberFire, 2025](https://www.fanduel.com/research/fantasy-football-trade-value-chart-week-4-2025). A prompt must pick a default philosophy and say so rather than letting "the chart says X" stand for all three.

**2.3 Consolidate or hold the star in a 10-team start-9.**
- **Hold / trade-down:** "In shallow formats like a 10-team, start-9 league, weekly ceiling is everything. Stars win those titles… you just traded a dollar for four quarters"; tier-down is cheapest in weeks 6–10: [RSJ, Aug 5, 2026](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2026-08-05/anatomy-tier-down/); "In shallow leagues, elite studs are the only currency that matters" (practitioner opinion, not data-backed): [Tennessee Trader, Sep 11, 2026](https://thetennesseetrader.substack.com/p/stop-trading-dollars-for-quarters).
- **Convert depth into elite starters before the deadline:** "contender in Weeks 10–14… optimizing for Weeks 15–17 only… Trade depth for stars": [FFTradeAnalyzer playoff guide (undated)](https://www.fftradeanalyzer.com/guides/playoff-trades); [DraftSharks Week 10 targets, Nov 6, 2025](https://www.draftsharks.com/article/fantasy-football-trade-targets-sells-and-holds-week-10).
- The published reconciliation is mode-by-record: consolidation for teams at/inside the playoff cutline; trade-down for teams past their window. The *premium to pay* when consolidating is where the numbers diverge: 1.3× (2022, stud-only) vs 82%-weight (2026 tool) vs "never equal, full stop" (Tennessee Trader).

**2.4 The consolidation discount: fixed, player-scaled, format-scaled, or roster-scaled.**
- **Scales with player stud-ness:** 1.3× at the top → 1.0× at the bottom: [PeakedinHighSkool, Jul 27, 2022](https://peakedinhighskool.com/2022-07-27/analyzing-2-for-1-dynasty-trades-in-terms-of-trade-values/).
- **Scales with league size:** 82% default second-asset weight, "shallower starting lineups may require a larger one": [Fantasy Trade Pilot, values Aug 7, 2026](https://fantasytradepilot.com/2-for-1-fantasy-football-trade-calculator/); same axis as SportsMonkie's λ (0.66/0.75/0.85): [SportsMonkie](https://sportsmonkie.com/tools/fantasy-football-trade-calculator/).
- **Scales with roster asymmetry:** 15–25% fair-price shift: [Draft Value Analytics (undated)](https://draftvalueanalytics.com/trade-value-charts-explained).
- These are three different axes (asset tier, league size, roster shape) — treat as compounding, not interchangeable.

**2.5 How big should the win-now premium actually be.**
- **The premium is real and quantified:** ~2pp title odds per guaranteed win; playoff weeks worth 4–8× (12-team): [Harstad, Footballguys, 09/21/2024](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2024-dynasty-in-theory-lets-talk-about-win-now-trades/); ~3× week-weight rule of thumb: [Buzzard14, Footballguys, 08/11/2014](https://www.footballguys.com/article/buzzard14_Strategies_to_win_your_league).
- **Executed playoff trades mostly don't matter:** 82.14% of analyzed window trades had zero impact; only 2 of 56 directly affected a title: [T-Rock, South Harmon FF, Jan 22, 2025 cycle](https://www.southharmonff.com/articles/playoff-odds-amp-strategy-1).
- Both are methodology-transparent with limited samples. Reconcilable framing: Harstad measures *win probability per dollar of roster improvement* (premium is single-digit to low-double-digit %, not "give anything"); South Harmon's failures are mostly *panic trades*, evidence about what managers do, not about what is worth paying.

**2.6 The fairness band: 5–10% vs 10–15%.**
- "Under 5% is noise; under 10% your roster breaks the tie; past 30% you send it once and never mention it again": [SportsMonkie (undated)](https://sportsmonkie.com/tools/fantasy-football-trade-calculator/).
- "Within 10 to 15 percent of chart parity, trades are functionally equivalent": [Draft Value Analytics (undated)](https://draftvalueanalytics.com/trade-value-charts-explained); the same 10–15% appears as the execution threshold in [FantasyStrategyGuide (undated)](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/trade-strategy). A judge can check an answer against either band; an honest answer names the band it is using.

**2.7 League-wide veto vs commissioner-only review.**
- **Abolish the vote:** "a bad trade is not cheating"; the league vote is "tyranny of the majority" where bubble teams veto rivals' deals out of self-interest — "let the Commish rule with a swift, firm, and focused hand": [RandomDraftOrder, Nov 25, 2025](https://www.randomdraftorder.com/articles/fantasy-trades-vetoes-collusion/). Same skeptical view, snippet-only: [The Athletic "Judge Gene" (Nov 8, 2023, paywalled)](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/5042699/2023-11-08/fantasy-football-judge-trade-vetoes/).
- **Vetoes can work:** "in highly experienced leagues… with clear veto thresholds (e.g., supermajority)": [FantasyOwner (undated)](https://www.fantasyowner.com/articles/vetoes-vs-commissioner-approval-fantasy-football/); the canonical architecture is ESPN's 4-of-10 member vote with 48-hour review: [ESPN rules page, 2009-03-10](https://www.espn.com.sg/fantasy/football/ffl/story?page=fflrulestradesstandard2009); Sleeper supports both models: [Sleeper Support, Jul 9, 2022](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3200544-can-i-veto-a-trade).

**2.8 Is a lopsided (non-collusive) trade veto-able at all.**
- **Collusion-only standard:** "A bad trade is not collusion. It's an owner making a miscalculation" — fleecing (a novice accepting a lopsided deal, stash-vs-win-now swaps) stays legal: [Glab, Nov 25, 2025](https://www.randomdraftorder.com/articles/fantasy-trades-vetoes-collusion/); "fairness isn't the same as legality — the 'that's what you get' precedent": [Randy Scott, ESPN, Nov 13, 2015](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/14119741/collusion-quitters-more-fantasy-football).
- **"Unfair" alone suffices:** ESPN's policy tells members to "veto/protest all **unfair**/collusive trades" — treating unfairness and collusion as co-equal grounds: [ESPN support page (undated)](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003903111-Fair-Play-Conduct-Collusive-Transaction).

**2.9 Where to set the trade deadline.**
- **Weeks 8–10 (early, anti-collusion):** [FanDraft, May 1, 2020](https://fandraft.com/blog/preventingcollusion); Glab's playbook: [Nov 25, 2025](https://www.randomdraftorder.com/articles/fantasy-football-disputes/).
- **Weeks 10–13 (default-ish):** third-party summaries of platform defaults cluster there ([sportDA guide, undated — snippet-verified](https://www.sportda.com/fantasy-football-trade-deadline/); [Fantasy Trade Analyzer late-season guide, 2026](https://fantasytradeanalyzers.com/late-season-fantasy-football-trade-advice/)).
- **Sleeper makes it a league setting** — final game of the chosen week, changeable, or off entirely: [Sleeper Support, Apr 22, 2026](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/2435411-when-is-my-trade-deadline). The assistant must read the actual league setting, not assume.

**2.10 Static weekly charts vs live/daily values.**
- **Weekly static charts dominate actual league usage** (RSJ, CBS, FantasyPros — consensus base + editorial scarcity adjustment): [RSJ, Sep 15, 2026](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2026-09-15/2026-fantasy-football-week-2-trade-value-chart/); [CBS, Sep 15, 2026](https://www.cbssports.com/fantasy/football/news/dave-richards-week-2-trade-chart-and-rest-of-season-fantasy-football-rankings-help-you-win-now/).
- **They are structurally stale:** "values move daily, charts update weekly" and one number hides a range of outcomes: [NovaPredict, Jun 30, 2026](https://novapredict.com/blog/fantasy-football-trade-value-chart); live alternatives update "multiple times per day" ([FantasyCalc](https://fantasycalc.com/)) or lag news "a day or two" ([Stats Guy](https://statsguyfantasy.com/methodology/how-values-work)). Honest framing per the sources: "chart = negotiation anchor, not verdict."

**2.11 When is the "strategic window" for trading.**
- **Weeks 5–10:** "the strategic window — roles are clearer, breakouts have emerged, injuries have hit. Trade for your playoff lineup now, not in Week 13": [MFL Fantasy (undated)](https://mflfantasyfootball.com/trade-strategy).
- **Weeks 10–14:** "If you're a contender in Weeks 10–14… optimizing for Weeks 15–17 only": [FFTradeAnalyzer (undated)](https://www.fftradeanalyzer.com/guides/playoff-trades).
- Published resolution: must-win/bubble status overrides the calendar: [USA TODAY, Dec 1, 2025](https://www.usatoday.com/story/sports/fantasy/football/2025-12-01/fantasy-football-week-14-buy-low-sell-high/87547470007/).

## 3. What bad advice looks like

Checkable failure patterns for an AI answer about a fantasy trade in this league:

1. **Transplanting a 12-team chart into a 10-team league.** Quoting a chart number for this league without noting the 12-team, 1QB, full-PPR calibration (all of [RSJ, Sep 15, 2026](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2026-09-15/2026-fantasy-football-week-2-trade-value-chart/), [FantasyPros, Sept 2026](https://www.fantasypros.com/2026/09/fantasy-football-trade-value-chart-week-1-2026/), [CBS, Sep 15, 2026](https://www.cbssports.com/fantasy/football/news/dave-richards-week-2-trade-chart-and-rest-of-season-fantasy-football-rankings-help-you-win-now/) are 12-team) and not adjusting for scarcity — where [PFF's 10-team PPR tier count (Aug 17, 2026)](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-2026-perfect-draft-strategy-round-by-round-for-10-team-leagues) (~12–13 viable RBs) says elite RB runs above and mid-tier RB below 12-team values.
2. **Pricing by raw points, ignoring positional scarcity.** Ranking or comparing players on PPG alone (e.g., offering an "efficient" WR for a top RB) — violating the 60–70% RB vs under-50% elsewhere start rate ([Harstad, Footballguys, 2020-08-27](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2020-positional-scarcity)) and the 7-point RB12/RB24 gap vs 3-point WR12/WR24 gap ([FFTradeAnalyzer (undated)](https://www.fftradeanalyzer.com/guides/position-scarcity)).
3. **Naive additive sum on a 2-for-1.** Summing chart values on both sides with no second-asset discount (75%/56%/42% per [SportsMonkie](https://sportsmonkie.com/tools/fantasy-football-trade-calculator/); 82% per [Fantasy Trade Pilot](https://fantasytradepilot.com/2-for-1-fantasy-football-trade-calculator/)) and no forced cut priced — the hidden 14th asset is simply missing.
4. **Overpaying on a stocked position.** Paying a scarcity premium for a 5th RB on a roster already carrying 4, or buying a duplicate elite at a held position — violating the reversal rule ([FantasyStrategyGuide (undated)](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/trade-strategy-guide)) and the "Lamar when you already have Allen" anti-pattern ([FantasyPros Trading 101, 2026-07](https://www.fantasypros.com/2026/07/fantasy-football-trading-101-how-to-make-a-trade-2026/)).
5. **Ignoring standings at the wrong end.** Pricing a Week 13+ deal at full-season value while fighting for the bracket (ignoring the 4–8× playoff-week weight per [Harstad, 09/21/2024](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2024-dynasty-in-theory-lets-talk-about-win-now-trades/) and the weeks 10–14 rule per [FFTradeAnalyzer](https://www.fftradeanalyzer.com/guides/playoff-trades)); or demanding full-season value from a partner with <15–20% playoff odds and <5 weeks left, when the math says rebuild ([CalculatorCollection, Sept 2026](https://www.calculatorcollection.org/en/calculators/fantasy-sports/playoff-odds-calculator/)).
6. **Small-sample and staleness blindness.** Trading on 2–4 weeks of data as if it were signal (the first 16 games are "nowhere near a large enough sample" — [FantasyPros, 2026-09](https://www.fantasypros.com/2026-09/fantasy-football-trade-advice-players-to-buy-sell-or-hold-2026/)), or treating a chart as current after breaking news when values lag "a day or two" ([Stats Guy](https://statsguyfantasy.com/methodology/how-values-work)) and charts carry "no injury data" ([SportsMonkie](https://sportsmonkie.com/tools/fantasy-football-trade-calculator/)).
7. **"NFL trade ⇒ fantasy breakout."** Buying a player whose touch surge came from an NFL transaction without flagging that only 3 of 9 non-QB players in the study saw more touches post-trade and the group fell 7.5 → 4.2 touches/game: [PFF, Ryan Conway, Nov 7, 2025](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-midseason-trades-production-role).
8. **Collusion.** Recommending any deal that benefits a rival or tanks a third team's playoff hopes — engineered waiver-order claims ([PFF, *Steel Curtain v. Rusty Trombones*, 2011-11-12](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-fantasy-judgment-decision-in-fantasy-football-collusion-case)), drops timed to a partner's waiver rank ([ESPN policy](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003903111-Fair-Play-Conduct-Collusive-Transaction)), or an eliminated team dumping best assets to a contender (the named red flag in [Glab, Nov 25, 2025](https://www.randomdraftorder.com/articles/fantasy-football-disputes/)). A one-sided but voluntary trade (fleecing) is *not* in this category.
9. **Quoting the chart as the verdict to the partner.** Leading a negotiation with "the chart says…" instead of context-adjusted needs — RSJ: charts "are a reference… not gospel," and asserting chart-parity as final "could shut the door on negotiations real quick" ([RSJ, Sep 15, 2026](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2026-09-15/2026-fantasy-football-week-2-trade-value-chart/)); inside the 10–15% parity band, the chart cannot distinguish deals at all ([Draft Value Analytics](https://draftvalueanalytics.com/trade-value-charts-explained)).
10. **Counter-spam with no concrete reason.** Countering a good-faith fair offer without naming a deficiency, or lowballing repeatedly without intent to up the ante — "people who send gobs of horrible trade offers typically get shunned… and sometimes get booted" ([ESPN 101 etiquette, Jun 30, 2022](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/19577495/fantasy-football-101-fantasy-football-etiquette)); a counter should anchor high but not insultingly ([FantasyStrategyGuide (undated)](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/trade-strategy); [Gut Check 278, ~2013](https://www.footballguys.com/article/gutcheck278tradeadvice?article=gutcheck278tradeadvice)).

## 4. Glossary

One reusable vocabulary for the assistant's trade-evaluation outputs:

- **Trade value** — the chart/consensus price of a player for the rest of the season, on a named metric and a named league size (state the size tag; most published charts are 12-team). Not a salary cap; not FAB dollars.
- **Replacement value** — the production of the player on your bench or at the top of the simulated waiver wire *in a league of your size*; the baseline subtracted in VOR math ([LineupLab, Sept 3, 2026](https://www.lineuplab.ai/guides/value-over-replacement-fantasy-football)). **VOR (value over replacement)** = projected points − replacement points; the scarcity gap between usable starters and replacement rank is where trade value actually lives.
- **Net value / differential** — the signed gap between what you give up and what you receive, after any consolidation discount and the forced cut; judged against a stated fairness band (5–10% or 10–15% — name which).
- **Additive sum test** — the chart's implied fairness test: sum both sides on one consistent metric; known to fail on packages (no discount for the 2nd asset landing on an already-built roster).
- **Positional scarcity** — the ratio of starters a league runs at a position to the available supply of players there (RB ≈ 50% of supply in this league; WR/TE ≈ 25%); the reason equal-point players at different positions have different trade values.
- **Consolidation / 2-for-1** — giving up two assets for one; the incoming star must clear a discount on the second outgoing asset (82% weight / λ-decay) plus the value of the forced cut. "Dollar vs quarters" is the shorthand for the risk: matching paper value while losing field value.
- **Forced cut** — the roster spot a 2-for-1 (or any net-gainer) makes redundant; must be priced as a hidden 14th asset.
- **Roster fit / position of need** — the adjustment (up to ~15–25% in the published band) for how the incoming player maps against your current lineup; includes the reversal case (a 5th RB on a 4-deep roster earns no premium) and the mutual-need rule (your offer must hit a hole on the partner's roster).
- **Win-now premium** — the extra value of a player whose production lands in playoff weeks or who settles a bye-line/bracket race; derived from ~2pp title odds per guaranteed win and ~3× to 4–8× playoff-week weights (12-team-tagged inputs); the mirror image is the **cellar discount** — a team below the ~15–20% playoff-odds line with <5 weeks left should not pay contender prices.
- **Floor and ceiling** — the downside (floor) and upside (ceiling) of a player's weekly range; trades should price the range and the odds of boom/bust, not the median projection; "prioritize ceiling over floor" is the bracket-week convention.
- **Season stage** — classification used to set posture: early (weeks 1–4, small-sample, buy-low plentiful), strategic window (weeks 5–10 per one camp; 10–14 per the other), deadline/bracket (weeks 13–17). Contender / middle / rebuilder determines who pays and who gets value.
- **FAAB** — the auction-bidding budget in this league (100 FAAB), a tradable asset in its own right — on Sleeper, trades can move FAAB dollars between teams ([Sleeper Support, Mar 19, 2024](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3188802-how-to-trade)).
- **Trade window** — the open period for deals; on Sleeper it runs to the **final game of the commissioner's selected deadline week** (or all season if the deadline is off), with a max 72-hour review period ([Sleeper Support, Apr 22, 2026](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/2435411-when-is-my-trade-deadline); [Sleeper Support, Mar 20, 2024](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/4258022-can-i-disable-trading)).
- **Counter** — a responsive revised offer; convention: anchor above target and concede toward a pre-set walk-away threshold; an open 15–20% below the ask is the only published numeric counter rule ([Gut Check 278, ~2013](https://www.footballguys.com/article/gutcheck278tradeadvice?article=gutcheck278tradeadvice)).
- **Collusion** — any agreement *outside the trade itself*, or any move whose purpose is to benefit another team / harm a third without improving your own position (side deals, engineered drops, waiver-order schemes, friendly dumps to a contender). Distinguished from **fleecing** — a one-sided but voluntary trade, which stays legal and cannot be vetoed under the collusion-only standard.
- **Veto** — league members' (or the commissioner's) power to cancel a trade in its review window; the classic 10-team threshold is 4 of 10 votes in 48 hours ([ESPN rules, 2009-03-10](https://www.espn.com.sg/fantasy/football/ffl/story?page=fflrulestradesstandard2009)); on Sleeper the commissioner retains ultimate control at any time ([Sleeper Support, Jul 9, 2022](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3200544-can-i-veto-a-trade)).
- **Bye / seed cliff** — in this 6-of-10 format the top-2 seeds skip Week 15 ([Sleeper Support, Dec 7, 2020](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/2203518-how-do-playoff-teams-get-determined)); the single largest jump in bracket value, and the reference point for "what exactly is this deal buying."
