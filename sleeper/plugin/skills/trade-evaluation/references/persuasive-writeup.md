# The persuasive, honest write-up and when to counter

Source: `report.md` (R3 v2) section 1.6, plus a write-up template and
counter-etiquette rules from `research-r3-trades.md` (R3 v1) marked below.

## Price both sides on one named, consistent metric

[FantasyStrategyGuide (undated)](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/trade-strategy):
identify roster holes, audit your own surplus, map the partner's holes,
price both sides against a named consensus anchor, then adjust for context
(scoring format, remaining schedule, injury risk). "Trade value charts...
reflect consensus, not context."

## State the fairness band you're using

Two bands are published, and they disagree — name which one is in use:

- [Draft Value Analytics (undated)](https://draftvalueanalytics.com/trade-value-charts-explained):
  "trades within 10 to 15 percent of chart parity are functionally
  equivalent."
- [SportsMonkie (undated)](https://sportsmonkie.com/tools/fantasy-football-trade-calculator/):
  "a gap under 5% is noise... under 10%, your own roster should break the
  tie... anything past 30% is the kind of offer you send once and do not
  mention again."

## Write the lineup before and after

[SharkSnip (undated)](https://sharksnip.com/blog/fantasy-trade-analyzer-math):
"write the lineup before and after the trade. Name every starter, the first
replacement, and the roster spot you would cut." A trade that only improves
one side on paper, or lands the incoming player on the bench, is not a real
upgrade — see `consolidation-rules.md` on pricing the forced cut.

## Quantify range, not just the median

[NovaPredict (Jun 30, 2026)](https://novapredict.com/blog/fantasy-football-trade-value-chart):
"a player is not a single value. He is a range of outcomes, a floor, a
median, a ceiling, and real odds of a boom or a bust. Trade the range, not
the name."

## Accept, counter, or walk

Accept when the offer is "within 10-15% of fair value... and addresses a
real need" ([FantasyStrategyGuide](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/trade-strategy)).
The only published numeric counter rule: open a counter at a **15-20%
discount** off the offer's stated value, know your walk-away boundary before
negotiating, and decline on good terms if it isn't met ([Footballguys,
Waldman, "Gut Check No. 278," circa 2013, pre-2024 — the fullest published
negotiation framework](https://www.footballguys.com/article/gutcheck278tradeadvice?article=gutcheck278tradeadvice)).
Reassess a rejected offer in about 72 hours — new information (an injury
report) can flip a Monday "no" into a Thursday "yes"
([FantasyStrategyGuide](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/trade-strategy)).

## When NOT to send an offer

[RSJ (Sep 15, 2026)](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2026-09-15/2026-fantasy-football-week-2-trade-value-chart/):
an offer so clearly one-sided it would only be accepted about 1% of the time
"likely isn't worth hitting the send button" — it burns relationship
capital. Repeated lowball spam gets managers shunned
([ESPN 101 etiquette, Jun 30, 2022](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/19577495/fantasy-football-101-fantasy-football-etiquette)).

## The behavioral half: let the other side feel like it won a little

[The Devy Royale, Jay Stein (Jul 27, 2026)](https://www.thedevyroyale.com/p/the-art-of-the-trade-a-handbook-for):
loss aversion means a dead-even 1-for-1 offer often dies anyway; find deals
where "the piece you're giving up matters less to your roster construction
than the piece you're getting matters to yours," so the partner feels like
they won a little.

## Supplemental: a concrete write-up template (R3 v1)

[Dynasty Nerds, "Grade the Trade," John Hammersmith (Aug 30, 2025)](https://www.dynastynerds.com/trades/grade-the-trade-advice-august-week-4/)
publishes a five-part per-deal structure (dynasty-format source; structure
transfers to redraft, the grade letters do not need to be reused verbatim):
restate both sides' packages, name the specific question asked, list
pros/cons for each side, give a one-line verdict naming which side wins and
why, and grade on the source's own scale (A = great for both, B = leans one
side but fair, C = questionable but defensible, D = lopsided, F = the
source's own label for a borderline-collusion deal). Use this as a
write-up *shape*, not as a scale this agent must reproduce.

## Counter etiquette (R3 v1)

[GoingFor2 (Jun 3, 2021, pre-2024 — the definitive published counter-offer
etiquette source, still cited as the standard)](https://goingfor2.com/trade-better-a-guide-to-fantasy-football-trade-strategy/):
"a good counter should keep the spirit of the original offer" — don't
counter with an unrelated trade shape, and don't over-counter ("endless
counters could end in no trade at all"). If the original offer is fair and
helps your team, accept it rather than counter reflexively; you don't know
whether the same offer is out to other teams too.

## Cite numbers, never as gospel

[RSJ (Sep 15, 2026)](https://www.rotostreetjournal.com/2026-09-15/2026-fantasy-football-week-2-trade-value-chart/):
charts "are a reference... not gospel, and trying to make your potential
trade partner think otherwise could shut the door on negotiations real
quick."
