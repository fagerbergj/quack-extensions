# Luck vs. process

Source: `research-r8-history-grading-v2.md` §1(d).

## Four channels

Decompose every season into: (1) lineup execution (controllable — see
`lineup-efficiency.md`); (2) player-outcome luck (actual vs. projected
points, partly controllable); (3) schedule luck (uncontrollable); (4) a
pre-kickoff test before calling any specific week a skill error — was the
starter already ruled out, was a bye/empty slot/role change missed
(fixable process), or was the bench boom unpredicted (not a real
mistake).

## PF vs. PA

PF measures roster quality; PA is the sum of your *opponents'* scores —
entirely uncontrollable, a schedule-luck measure, never a skill credit or
debit ([RotoWire, Dec 2024](https://www.rotowire.com/football/article/points-for-points-against-explained-95311); [Sleeper blog, 2025](https://sleeper.com/blog/what-is-pf-and-pa-in-fantasy-football/)).

## Expected wins / luck gap

All-play record = the record if the manager played every team every
week. Expected wins ≈ all-play win % × 14; luck gap = expected wins −
actual wins, with PF/PA as tiebreakers. The "unlucky team profile": high
PF + high PA + strong all-play + wins below expected + multiple close
losses ([ffwrapped, undated](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/unluckiest-team)).

## Schedule luck does not fully wash out

Extremes of ~±22% (≈±3 wins in a 14-game season) were measured in a
12-team, 2018-2023 sample; **even six seasons did not fully even it out —
report the five-season cumulative luck gap, do not zero it**
([Elhabr, undated](https://tonyelhabr.rbind.io/posts/fantasy-football-performance/)).

## Close losses are timing, not a clutch trait

Count losses by <5 and <10 points separately. The NFL analogue (829
season-pairs since 1999): one-score records correlate +0.08 with the
next season's close-game record (statistical zero) while point margin
holds at +0.40 ([NFL Analytic, 2026](https://nflanalytic.com/explainer-close-game-luck.html)).
Never read a close-loss rate as clutch/anti-clutch evidence.

## Actual − projected: mostly luck, with a management overlay

Elhabr treats the gap as misfortune (injury, lost playing time) because
projections are supposed to absorb matchup difficulty; ffwrapped's
pre-kickoff checklist carves some of it back out as process error. This
is a genuine disagreement — present it as "mostly luck, with a
management overlay," and always run the pre-kickoff test before calling
a process error.

## The five-season skill test = persistence

Skill implies first-half and second-half win fractions correlate;
chance implies a round cloud at .500. Fantasy football landed clearly on
the skill side ([MIT News, Nov 7, 2018](https://news.mit.edu/2018/hosoi-study-skill-fantasy-sports-1107); [SIAM Review 60(4), 2018](https://doi.org/10.1137/16m1102094)).
A manager consistently below average across multiple seasons carries a
genuine skill signal; a single-season outlier washes out.

## Gap

No published fantasy-football Pythagorean exponent or mid-season
regression-to-.500 fraction exists in the open literature; for this
league, per-league fitting is the honest approach.
