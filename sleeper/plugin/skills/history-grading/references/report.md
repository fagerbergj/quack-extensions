# R8 Research Report: Evaluating a Manager's Past Drafts and Seasons in Hindsight

**League scope.** 10-team PPR redraft on Sleeper: 14-round snake, 16-man roster (QB/RB/RB/WR/WR/TE/FLEX/K/DEF + 5 bench), rolling waivers, $100 FAAB, 6 playoff teams from Week 15. Five seasons of history to grade.

**Method note.** This report consolidates four specialist research passes (draft-grading metrics; positional allocation; lineup efficiency / luck-vs-process; waiver-trade activity and recurring mistakes), completed 2026-09-18. All URLs were retrieved in that session unless marked *snippet-verified* (JS-gated page; figure taken from the search-indexed summary) or marked **no date** (undated page; retrieval date given). Pages from the templated **Authority Network America (ANA)** network — fantasystrategyguide.com, draftvalueanalytics.com, draftdayauthority.com, fantasyprojectionlab.com — are flagged wherever used; each self-identifies as ANA on its homepage ([ANA About page](https://authoritynetworkamerica.com/about/)). Its numbers are carried only as unvalidated single-site examples, never as load-bearing methodology.

## 1. Decision rules and numeric thresholds

### 1(a) Grading a single draft pick

**R-a1 — Freeze the price, then compare to end-of-season positional finish using PPG, not total points.**
Price each pick at its ADP slot at draft time; grade it against the player's end-of-season *positional* finish, using fantasy PPG because "injuries aren't very predictable"; a pick where your rank matched ADP counts as neither right nor wrong.
→ [PFF, "2025 Fantasy Football Season In Review" (Nathan Jahnke), Jan 13, 2026](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-season-in-review-what-nathan-jahnke-got-right-and-wrong-2025)

**R-a2 — Build an "ADP rewind" table (draft position vs. finish position) per position.**
"We do not dislike players; we dislike their draft cost." Qualitative baseline: about half of the top-12 at a position providing solid returns is normal; a **bust = large adverse gap** (2024 examples: McCaffrey RB1 draft → RB67 finish; Pacheco RB12 → RB62).
→ [The Fantasy Footballers, "ADP Rewind: RB Takeaways" (Joe Beldner), Jul 25, 2025](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/analysis/adp-rewind-rb-takeaways-for-2025-fantasy-football/)

**R-a3 — Early-round landmine = a Round 1–2 pick whose positional finish dropped materially.**
PFF's 2025 list (Weeks 1–17, half-PPR): Barkley RB3 (ADP 1.03) → RB13; Jefferson WR2 (1.05) → WR28; Jeanty RB6 (1.10) → RB14; Thomas Jr. WR8 (2.03) → WR43 — drops of 8–35 positional spots on first/second-round prices. The 10-spot line is a researcher characterization of this list, not a rule PFF states; treat "≈10+ spots on a round 1–2 pick" as the working flag.
→ [PFF, "Early-Round Landmines From 2025 Drafts" (Nic Bodiford), Jan 7, 2026](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-early-round-landmines-from-2025-drafts)

**R-a4 — Apply a healthy-play filter before calling a bust a draft error.**
A player who "played in the large majority of games" and still underperformed is the grader's case (2025: Jefferson, consensus top-3 WR → PPR WR25); injury-driven misses are excluded. The same article defines the ROI frame: Harold Fannin Jr., largely undrafted at ADP TE34, finished TE4.
→ [PFF, "PFF's 2025 Fantasy Football Awards" (Jonathon Macri), Dec 30, 2025](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-pff-2025-fantasy-football-awards)

**R-a5 — Opportunity cost (Draft Leverage Differential): grade the pick against what would have been available at your *next* pick, not against one other player.**
DLD = historical value of the positional slot you took minus historical value of the slot expected at your next pick, from 16 years of normalized data (RB1 = 290.8, RB14 = 158.3, WR1 = 209.5, WR7 = 163.0 in their 2018 set). Worked example (a 10-person snake — the closest published per-slot proxy for a 10-team league): "RB1 historical Leverage Score = 290.8, RB14 historical Leverage Score = 158.3, **Draft Leverage Differential = 142.5**"; in the RB1-vs-WR1 comparison, "RB1 wins by a landslide, with a **Draft Leverage Differential 96 points greater**." **Flag: evergreen 2018 methodology; the slot values predate 2024–26 boards.**
→ [FantasyLabs, "Draft Leverage Differential" (Ryan Collinsworth), Jun 28, 2018](https://www.fantasylabs.com/articles/fantasy-football-rankings-draft-leverage-differential-historical-data/)

**R-a6 — Slot-expectation check: "the Nth pick should return the Nth-best player taken."**
The Nth pick should land the Nth-most-valuable player taken; per-pick results average into a grade that is zero-sum across the draft room — "a chalk draft grades around a B for everyone." Useful to stop a manager (or an AI) from claiming a B+ draft in a room where everyone was chalk.
→ [LeagueLogs, draft-grades methodology (no date; retrieved 2026-09-18)](https://leaguelogs.com/fantasy-football-draft-grades)

**R-a7 — PPG above replacement (VOR): points above a replacement-level player at the same position. The replacement baseline is the #1 disagreement — three published conventions:**

| Convention | Baseline (12-team as published) | Weekly/season baselines | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bench / 12th man | QB18, RB30, WR42, TE18 | 16.0 / 8.5 / 7.0 / 5.5 PPG | [PFF, Mike Clay, May 27, 2015](https://www.pff.com/news/the-value-of-replacement-level) |
| Last starter | QB12, RB36, WR48, TE12 | 2026 PPR season points: 289.7 / 132.3 / 156.7 / 151.9 | [YAFSB, Mar 11, 2026](https://yafsb.com/content/vor-value-over-replacement-fantasy-football/) |
| Post-draft waiver wire | "the player you could easily replace him with on the waiver wire"; VOR floored at 0 | — | [FantasyPros, updated Mar 30, 2020](https://support.fantasypros.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005868747-What-is-value-based-drafting-What-do-player-draft-values-mean-VORP-VONA-VOLS-VBD) |

**10-team adaptation (researcher-derived, not a published table):** all three sources state replacement level is format-specific; for this league the last-starter convention maps to roughly QB10 / ~RB30 / ~WR30 / TE10. The analyzer must recompute per season and print which convention it used.

**Good/bad thresholds:**
- **"Average pick" line = median VOR by position.** For a 10-team-ish PPR format (1QB/2RB/3WR/1TE/FLEX, 2024 projections — note: 3 WR, not this league's 2): QB median ≈ 25, RB just under 50, WR just above 50, TE ≈ 38. A great pick is well above the positional median; at/below is a bad pick. → [Fantasy Football Analytics, Aug 2024](https://fantasyfootballanalytics.net/2024/08/winning-fantasy-football-with-projections-value-over-replacement-and-value-based-drafting.html)
- **Elite-value band (12-team PPR, 2026):** Bijan Robinson VOR 192.6; top-20 VOR range 84–193; the best QB scores only 71.8 (1QB format) — the metric's purpose is exposing QB value compression. → [YAFSB, Mar 11, 2026](https://yafsb.com/content/vor-value-over-replacement-fantasy-football/)
- **Letter bands on PPG-based value (single site — flag as one data point):** A > 8.0, B 5.0–7.9, C 3.0–4.9, D < 3.0, F negative, where value = (projected PPG − replacement PPG) ÷ normalized slot cost. → [Calculatrex (no date; retrieved 2026-09-18)](https://calculatrex.com/fantasy-draft-value-calculator)
- **Cliff rule:** a steep drop between adjacent players (e.g., TE2 89.0 → TE3 42.2 VOR) is a signal to take the last cliff-top player even slightly ahead of ADP. → [YAFSB, Mar 11, 2026](https://yafsb.com/content/vor-value-over-replacement-fantasy-football/)

**R-a8 — Reach: declare your band before grading. Best-established hindsight bands:**
PFF's own season review treats **20+ spots** rank-vs-ADP as "notable" (2025: "Olave was notably one of two players that I ranked over 20 spots higher than ADP among the top-75 players") and **100+ overall spots** for late-round values (Rodriguez Jr. RB52→RB77; Isaiah Davis RB61→RB118). These are the only reach-magnitude numbers a named analyst applied to a full season of real results.
→ [PFF, Jahnke, Jan 13, 2026](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-season-in-review-what-nathan-jahnke-got-right-and-wrong-2025)

**R-a9 — Steal / value pick: price fell past ADP by a material margin.**
Tool definitions: "Steals are players who fell past their market value; reaches are players taken well ahead of it"; a consensus #1 taken at 1.01 "grades as a fair pick, not a steal"; consistent reaching "earns a D or F." Tier rule: value = market price later than your independent rank (e.g., "ranked 18th but goes 31st"); reach = "goes 15th but ranks 34th — paying a second-round pick for a third-round player." → [LeagueLogs (no date; retrieved 2026-09-18)](https://leaguelogs.com/fantasy-football-draft-grades) and [NovaPredict, Jun 13, 2026](https://novapredict.com/blog/fantasy-football-adp-values-and-reaches). ffwrapped's worked examples imply ~14–17 spots of gap as the visible line (pick 38/ADP 52 = value; pick 95/ADP 78 = reach). → [ffwrapped, draft grades (no date; retrieved 2026-09-18)](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/fantasy-football-draft-grades)

**R-a10 — An off-market pick is defensible only with a stated structural reason; the decision test is next-pick availability.**
"Can I get the player I want at my next pick? If the answer isn't confidently 'yes,' then it's not a reach." *(JS-gated; snippet-verified.)* → [FantasyPros, Mario Mergola, May 2019](https://www.fantasypros.com/2019/05/when-is-it-okay-to-reach-on-draft-day-fantasy-football/)

**R-a11 — Slot-quality check, 10-team PPR: compare your slot's total value to the best slot's.**
3D Value-by-draft-position for a 10-team PPR league: by ADP, Pick 3 (331.3) > Pick 1 (323.3) … > Pick 9 (284.7); optimally, Pick 4 (340.3) leads, and **the No. 8 spot captures 14.1% fewer 3D Value points than the No. 3 pick through six rounds.** Use this to frame "your slot cost you X% of the draft" before blaming individual picks.
→ [DraftSharks, "Best Draft Position, 10-team" (Jared Smola), Aug 26, 2026](https://www.draftsharks.com/article/best-draft-position-fantasy-football-10-team)

**⚠️ Unvalidated ANA-network bands (carry only as flagged examples):** "reach = 15+ picks before ADP; 5–15 = normal reach zone; ±4 = neutral; value = 8+ picks after ADP; 15+ late = investigate" ([Fantasy Strategy Guide, no date](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/adp-strategy)) and "act only on a surplus of 2+ rounds of ADP gap, or 15%+ below projected auction value; below that the gap is more likely noise" ([Draft Value Analytics, no date](https://draftvalueanalytics.com/surplus-value-drafting/)). Both sites self-identify as ANA ([About](https://authoritynetworkamerica.com/about/)); neither is dated or bylined. **If the analyzer quotes these numbers, it must carry the same caveat.**

**Explicit gap (draft grading):** no peer-reviewed study of fantasy draft grading exists in the 2024–2026 literature found. The strongest dated, named-analyst anchors are PFF (Jan 2026 / Dec 2025 / Jan 2026 / May 2015), FantasyPros (Mar 2020 / May 2019 / Jul 2025–26), DraftSharks (Aug 26, 2026), and The Fantasy Footballers (Jul 25, 2025).

### 1(b) Positional allocation (10-team PPR norms)

**R-b1 — Target roster shape: ~4–5 RB, 4–5 WR, 1–2 QB, 1–2 TE, K/DEF in the final rounds.**
→ [DraftSharks 10-team PPR guide (Matt Schauf), Aug 22, 2026](https://www.draftsharks.com/article/fantasy-football-draft-strategy-guide/10-team-ppr); [FantasySixPack (Joe Bond), Aug 5, 2026](https://fantasysixpack.net/fantasy-football-draft-strategies-from-pick-10/) (flagged: written for 12-team half-PPR 3-WR — directionally the same shape). Board mechanics: on the live 2026 10-team PPR ADP (143 human drafts, Sept 11–18, 2026), 14 RBs and 14 WRs are gone by pick 30; 26 RBs and 31 WRs by pick 76 — the first ~100 picks of a 140-pick draft are RB/WR depth. → [Fantasy Football Calculator PPR ADP, 10-team](https://fantasyfootballcalculator.com/adp/ppr/10-team/all) (methodology: human picks only, at [help.fantasyfootballcalculator.com/article/34](https://help.fantasyfootballcalculator.com/article/34-average-draft-position-adp-data))

**R-b2 — Round map (PFF 10-team playbook):** Round 1 = RB or WR; Round 2 = WR; Round 3 = QB/RB/TE; Round 4 = WR/RB/top TE. Rationale for 10-team: "It is much easier to find two running backs you're comfortable starting on a weekly basis in 10-team leagues compared to 12," while "at quarterback and tight end, there is a clear difference between the top few players at the position and the bottom of the top 10." → [PFF, Jun 30, 2025](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-perfect-10-team-2025-draft-strategy-round-by-round)

**R-b3 — QB timing, from realized 10-team PPR boards: QB1 in Round 3; QB2–3 at the Round 4/5 turn; QB4+ in Rounds 8+; a starter-grade QB is always reachable (QB10 ≈ Round 13), but the top 3–5 are gone by the end of Round 5.**
2026 board: Allen 25.5 (QB1), Burrow 48.2 / Lamar Jackson 49.4 (R4/5 turn), Williams 76.8, Hurts 78.0, Daniels 80.6, Lawrence 82.1, Mahomes 99.5; QB10 at 128.7. 2025 archive (8,470 human drafts, Aug 25–Sept 1, 2025) shows the same shape a touch earlier: Lamar 22.9 → Allen 24.7 → Burrow 30.3 → Daniels 31.4 → Hurts 38.5.
→ [FFC 10-team 2026 ADP](https://fantasyfootballcalculator.com/adp/ppr/10-team/all), [10-team 2025 archive](https://fantasyfootballcalculator.com/adp/ppr/10-team/all/2025)

**R-b4 — Never take a QB6–10.** The single most over-projected tier in fantasy: ~42-point average miss vs. preseason projections over six seasons (2019–2024), ballooning to ~80 in the last three.
→ [Fantasy Football Analytics positional-bias study, Jul 2025](https://fantasyfootballanalytics.net/2025/07/fantasy-football-projections-exploring-positional-bias-in-projections.html)

**R-b5 — 10-team makes waiting on QB cheaper: quantified two ways.** QB1→QB12 gap was 6.8 PPG in 2024 vs. 9.5 (RB1→RB24) and 11.3 (WR1→WR36) — "Taking a quarterback that early is usually a mistake," and the first QB drafted is "almost never" the season's top scorer (2024: Allen first drafted, fourth in QB FPPG). → [Footballguys, Gary Davenport, Jun 6, 2025](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2025-fantasy-draft-strategy-guide-quarterbacks). Stathole's WAR study: "Josh Allen's WAR drops an entire expected win for 10-team leagues vs just 12-team" — "Less scarcity. So waiting isn't as big of a deal" (verbatim from the free lede; full charts paywalled). → [Stathole, Jeff Henderson, Aug 3, 2026](https://statholesports.substack.com/p/fantasy-football-war-alternative-d2a)

**R-b6 — TE: the cliff is flatter than its reputation, so a top-3 TE is pay-up-optional and a late TE is free.** TE1→TE12 gap: 5.6 PPR PPG in 2024, lowest of any position; top-3 within 30 points total. 2026 10-team board: only five TEs drafted at all (McBride 28.9, Loveland 54.1, Warren 63.4, Kraft 107.1, Likely 136.2); DraftSharks calls Rounds 8–12 safe ("only 10 TEs start in your league"), and FLEX makes a second TE optional, not required. The conditional data say *either* take elite *or* punt: TE1–3 picks had a 10% bust rate and +8.6 value vs. 26.7% and −38.8 for TE4–12 (12-team PPR 2015–2024; *indexed summary*).
→ [Footballguys, Davenport, Jun 27, 2025](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2025-fantasy-draft-strategy-guide-tight-ends); [FFC 10-team TE ADP, live 2026](https://fantasyfootballcalculator.com/adp/ppr/10-team/tight-end); [DraftSharks, Aug 22, 2026](https://www.draftsharks.com/article/fantasy-football-draft-strategy-guide/10-team-ppr); [Fantasy Football Blueprint, Jul 28, 2026](https://www.fantasyfootballblueprint.com/2026/07/28/the-elite-te-conditional/)

**R-b7 — The RB axis (disagreement — present both; let the manager choose, then hold them to their own declared strategy):**
- **Hero RB / RB-heavy:** 2024's top-4 RBs all scored 336+ PPR (Gibbs 362.9, Barkley 355.3, Bijan 341.7, Henry 336.4) while RB5–21 sat in a 200–300 band — a real positional cliff, "particularly effective in PPR." → [FantasyLabs (Ben Strunk), Aug 18, 2025](https://www.fantasylabs.com/articles/hero-rb-explained-top-fantasy-football-targets-for-2025/). The risk window is the "RB Dead Zone," ~Rounds 4–8, where RB bust rates are worse than WR's. → [Footballguys, Jun 12, 2025](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2025-fantasy-draft-strategy-guide-running-backs) (also: in 2024 only one RB — Barkley — averaged 20+ carries/game; only six hit 275 carries).
- **Zero RB / WR-heavy:** DraftSharks' 2026 data study says the *optimal* 10-team PPR build is Zero RB — "four high-end WRs paired with a high-end TE and QB" — 340.3 vs. 319.9 by ADP (+6.4%). → [DraftSharks, Aug 26, 2026](https://www.draftsharks.com/article/best-draft-position-fantasy-football-10-team). Freedman's viability test: Zero RB can win **as long as RBs going after round 4 significantly outperform ADP**. → [Fantasy Life (Matt Freedman), Jul 4, 2025](https://www.fantasylife.com/articles/fantasy/zero-rb-strategy-for-fantasy-football-2025-alvin-kamara-and-more-league-winning-targets). Standing counterargument: the usable-RB pool is *smaller* than the usable-WR pool — "24 RBs averaged 12+ fantasy PPG, while 38 WRs did" (JS-gated; indexed summary). → [Sporting News 10-team guide, 2025](https://www.sportingnews.com/us/fantasy/news/fantasy-football-10-team-draft-strategy-2025/89641aec2e5552e3f3bad15c)
- **The bridge the analyzer should quote:** elite RBs are the most *over-projected* tier (RB1–5 miss projections by ~55 points, worst of any tier; RB6–10 by only ~14; WR1–5 by ~31) → [FFA, Jul 2025](https://fantasyfootballanalytics.net/2025/07/fantasy-football-projections-exploring-positional-bias-in-projections.html) — while they are also the *most durable* tier (85.0% of available games over five seasons vs. TE 84.5, QB 83.9, WR 83.6; 400 player-seasons) → [Fantasy Index (Ian Allan), Aug 24, 2026](https://fantasyindex.com/2026/08/24/factoid/risky-business). Honest synthesis: **RB bust risk is projection/volume risk more than injury risk**, which is why the middle path — one top-12 RB in Rounds 1–2, a second by Round 5–6 — satisfies both camps. *(Synthesis of the researchers' findings, not a single cited claim.)*

**R-b8 — 2-QB evidence is context only for this 1-QB league.** 13-year simulation (2012–2024): QB scoring SD jumps from 39 (1-QB) to 66 (2-QB); the winning 2-QB draft was QB in Round 1 then best-available RB/WR in Round 2 (61.5% of simulated wins) while QB-QB first two picks won only 7.7% → [Teams In A Vacuum, 2025](https://teamsinavacuum.com/2-qb-or-not-2-qb/). Opposite named-analyst rule: "walk away from the first four rounds of the draft with two quarterbacks" (top-5 QB in R1 if available, else R2, third QB by R3–4) → [Fantasy Life (Gene Clemons), Aug 7, 2025](https://www.fantasylife.com/articles/fantasy/two-qb-fantasy-football-league-strategy-for-2025-tips-and-tricks). Its only use here: a mandatory second QB would reprice the position; it tells the analyzer how much headroom exists.

**R-b9 — Kicker (disagreement) and DEF (near-consensus).**
- **Orthodox:** exactly one K, final round only; where allowed, "not to draft one" is the perfect strategy. → [FantasyPros, Jun 2026](https://www.fantasypros.com/2026/06/fantasy-football-draft-strategy-kickers-2026/) and [FantasyPros, Aug 2026](https://www.fantasypros.com/2026/08/fantasy-football-draft-strategy-how-to-draft-kickers-2026-advice/) (both snippet-verified).
- **Revisionist (2024–25 data):** Jason Myers scored 195 pts (more than all but two TEs) at ADP 286.3 — unpicked in most drafts; two of the four best K PPG seasons ever occurred in 2024 (Fairbairn 12.7, Myers 11.5). Target elite booters in Rounds 9–10; keep the K as the penultimate-round target while streaming D/ST. → [SI, Michael Fabiano, Jan 19, 2026](https://www.si.com/fantasy/five-biggest-fantasy-football-hits-misses-2025-lessons-learned); [ESPN, Jul 24, 2026](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/49426681/fantasy-football-strategy-2026-nine-mistakes-avoid).
- **DEF:** stream it. "Investing heavily in defense in fantasy football drafts is a lousy allocation of resources" → [FantasyPros, Jun 2026](https://www.fantasypros.com/2026/06/fantasy-football-draft-strategy-streaming-defenses-2026/); weekly matchup > team identity (even the most-common championship-roster D in 2025 had its roughest September) → [ESPN D/ST Road Map, Sep 2, 2026](https://www.espn.com.au/fantasy/football/story/_/id/49798496/2026-fantasy-football-d-st-road-map-early-season); operational streamer line: a defense is a legit weekly stream below **50% rostership** (rolling-waiver leagues: above that you can't grab it cheaply) → [4for4, Sep 11, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/w2/fantasy-football-defense-streaming-week-2-potential-gold-waiver-wire).

### 1(c) Lineup efficiency

**R-c1 — Definition (consistent across sources): lineup efficiency = actual starter points ÷ points of the best possible *legal* lineup from the same roster that week (final scores, slot-legal, each player once) × 100. Season figure = total-points-weighted ratio — never a simple average of weekly percentages.**
→ [ffwrapped lineup-efficiency explainer (Kevin Tian; no date, current 2026)](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/fantasy-football-lineup-efficiency); identical definition in [League History glossary (no date, current 2026)](https://www.leaguehistory.app/research/glossary). **No published distribution of lineup efficiency exists for a 10-team PPR redraft league — say so explicitly; do not invent one.** The closest published number, N(77.5%, 5%), is a *simulation parameter* for generating fake seasons, not a measurement of real managers. → [ffsimulator R package docs, current 2025–2026](https://ffsimulator.ffverse.com/articles/custom)

**R-c2 — Benchmarks to anchor against, all with stated formats (and none is this league's format):**
- 12-team keeper/dynasty, three seasons: **85.9%, 86.8%, 83.7%** (finished 2nd, 4th, 7th of 12). The author explicitly argues there is **no universal good/bad threshold** and that a 10-team league with only 5 bench slots "should trend higher" (fewer realistic coin flips) — and that a *deep* roster can produce *lower* efficiency because there are more reasonable ways to be wrong. → [Advanced Sports Logic, L. LaPadula, Sep 12, 2026](https://advancedsportslogic.com/nfl/6416-set-better-fantasy-football-lineups)
- 12-team ESPN league, 2018–2023: expect **~20 points/week left on the bench from roughly 2 sub-optimal starts** (post undated; covers through the 2023 season). → [Tony Elhabr](https://tonyelhabr.rbind.io/posts/fantasy-football-performance/)

**R-c3 — Red flag = persistence, not a single week.** Over six seasons, each team's lost-opportunity vs. league average regressed toward zero with extreme deviations of only **about ±4 points/week** → [Elhabr](https://tonyelhabr.rbind.io/posts/fantasy-football-performance/). Decision-error metrics are only meaningful **aggregated over weeks**: Fantasy Genius computed optimal lineups for 20,000+ platform teams over 7 weeks and defines *incorrect start* (a started player outside the optimal lineup), *incorrect bench* (a benched player who would have been in it), and **Correct Decision Rate (CDR) = correct decisions ÷ rostered weeks**, warning that many "incorrect" calls are defensible before kickoff. → [Fantasy Genius (Mike Barton), Oct 27, 2023](https://fantasygenius.substack.com/p/decision-disasters-a-statistical)

**R-c4 — Efficiency is separable from wins and roster quality.** The league winner in the canonical "actual vs. best possible" study had *bad* lineup-picking and won anyway — high efficiency does not visibly correlate with record in a single season. → [Steven Morse, "Measuring Roster Skill in Fantasy Football," Sep 15, 2019](https://stmorse.github.io/journal/fantasy-bench.html). A team that scores 80/80 is perfectly efficient and can still lose badly. → [ffwrapped](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/fantasy-football-lineup-efficiency)

**R-c5 — Context for "what's on the bench worth":** a first backup is worth ~**50.4% of a starter's points at RB, 44.3% at WR, 28.7% at TE, 23.8% at QB**; with flex, the backup at the position *not* filling flex is the most valuable (RB backup ≈ 70.8–72.6% of starter value when the flex is a WR). Monte Carlo, 1M reps per point, error bars stated. → [4for4 (Kevin Zatloukal), Jul 23, 2015](https://www.4for4.com/2015/preseason/what-value-bench-player-fantasy-football). **No source found publishes a clean "bench depth → wins/playoff berth" regression for redraft H2H — flag as unresolved, don't invent a correlation.**

### 1(d) Luck vs. process

**R-d1 — Decompose every season into four channels** (synthesis of the two practitioner sources below): (1) lineup execution (controllable) — §1(c); (2) player-outcome luck: actual vs. *projected* points (partly controllable); (3) schedule luck (uncontrollable) — below; (4) a pre-kickoff test before any specific week is called a skill error: was the starter already ruled out, did you miss a bye/empty slot/clear role change (→ fixable process) or was the bench boom unpredicted (→ not a real mistake). → [ffwrapped](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/fantasy-football-lineup-efficiency); [Elhabr](https://tonyelhabr.rbind.io/posts/fantasy-football-performance/)

**R-d2 — PF vs. PA: PF measures roster quality; PA measures schedule luck** (it is the sum of your *opponents'* scores — entirely uncontrollable). → [RotoWire, Dec 2024](https://www.rotowire.com/football/article/points-for-points-against-explained-95311); [Sleeper blog, 2025](https://sleeper.com/blog/what-is-pf-and-pa-in-fantasy-football/); [ffwrapped schedule-luck guide (no date, current 2026)](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/unluckiest-team)

**R-d3 — Expected wins / luck gap: all-play record** (the record if you played every team every week — in a 10-team league the week's top scorer goes 9-0); **expected wins ≈ all-play win % × 14; luck gap = expected wins − actual wins**, with PF/PA as tiebreakers. The "unlucky team profile": high PF + high PA + strong all-play + wins below expected + multiple close losses ("a 6-8 team ranking 3rd in total points probably had a competitive roster and caught opponents on their best weeks"). PA alone is insufficient — it doesn't show whether points arrived in close losses or blowouts. → [ffwrapped](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/unluckiest-team)

**R-d4 — Quantified bounds on schedule luck (12-team sample, 2018–2023):** actual win % minus all-play win % ranged with **extremes of ~±22% ≈ ±3 wins in a 14-game season**; largest single-season deviation −29%; and **even six seasons did not fully even it out — report the five-season cumulative luck gap, do not zero it.** Weekly matchup-difference SD was ~32.5 points in that league. → [Elhabr](https://tonyelhabr.rbind.io/posts/fantasy-football-performance/)

**R-d5 — Close losses are a timing story, not a skill trait.** Operationalization: count losses by <5 and <10 points separately, plus "a bench decision that would have changed the result" — and note lineup efficiency and schedule luck "measure different things." The NFL analogue (829 season-pairs since 1999): one-score records correlate **+0.08** with the next season's close-game record (statistical zero) while point margin holds at **+0.40** — code published. → [ffwrapped](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/unluckiest-team); [NFL Analytic close-game-luck explainer, 2026](https://nflanalytic.com/explainer-close-game-luck.html) (see also [Pythagorean wins explainer](https://nflanalytic.com/explainer-pythagorean-wins.html))

**R-d6 — Actual−projected points: "mostly luck, with a management overlay."** Elhabr treats it as misfortune (injury, lost playing time) because projections are supposed to absorb matchup difficulty; ffwrapped's pre-kickoff checklist carves some of it back out as process error. *(Disagreement — see §2.)* → [Elhabr](https://tonyelhabr.rbind.io/posts/fantasy-football-performance/); [ffwrapped](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/fantasy-football-lineup-efficiency)

**R-d7 — The five-season skill test = persistence, with three published methods.** Skill implies first-half and second-half win fractions correlate (diagonal scatter); chance implies a round cloud at .500; heavy players win more than light players; humans beat thousands of randomly generated teams. Fantasy football landed clearly on the skill side. → [MIT News, Nov 7, 2018](https://news.mit.edu/2018/hosoi-study-skill-fantasy-sports-1107); paper: [Getty, Hosoi et al., SIAM Review 60(4), 2018](https://doi.org/10.1137/16m1102094). Cross-sport backing: manager ranks across seasons are significantly correlated in ~1M Fantasy Premier League managers. → [PLOS ONE, 2020](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0246698)

**R-d8 — Season-level luck estimate (single source, snippet-verified — flag as such):** luck accounts for roughly **2 wins/losses per team per year** at the season level, from week-by-week matchup analysis. → [Alex Cates (undated; page unfetchable)](https://www.alexcates.com/post/luck-vs-skill-how-much-does-luck-matter-in-season-long-fantasy-football)

**Explicit gap (luck):** there is **no published fantasy-football Pythagorean exponent or mid-season regression-to-.500 fraction in the open literature** found; for a 10-team 14-game + 6-playoff-team league, per-league fitting is the honest approach. Elhabr's ±3 wins is the only quantified bound, in a 12-team sample.

### 1(e) Waiver and trade activity

**R-e1 — Grade waiver/FAAB moves by *quality*, not count: rank every move by rest-of-season points-above-replacement gained, and compare a manager's realized total to the league-best moves available that week.**
→ [League History glossary (no date, current 2026)](https://www.leaguehistory.app/research/glossary)

**R-e2 — The "league-winning pickup" check: was the manager positioned to claim the player who won his league?**
2025 examples of championship-deciding claims: QB Brock Purdy (outscored all QBs by 12.7 pts in Weeks 16–17 (69.8 pts in those two weeks)), WR Christian Watson (Wks 8–17 overall WR9), TE Jake Tonges (TE3 in Week 17), RB Kimani Vidal (Wks 6–14 RB17), WR Wan'Dale Robinson (92 catches). → [PFF (Nic Bodiford), Jan 14, 2026](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-waiver-wire-pickups-that-won-2025-leagues); PFF's season deciders list puts waiver additions alongside injuries and late-season QB play. → [PFF, Jan 7, 2026](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-biggest-deciders-of-the-2025-season)

**R-e3 — FAAB spend benchmarks (most numeric public treatment found — undated practitioner guide, flag as such):** spend **30–50% of FAAB in Weeks 2–5**, ~30–40% in Weeks 5–10, aggressive push Weeks 11+; bid sizing: **season-changing pickup = 25–40% of remaining FAAB, mid-tier flex = 5–15%, lottery ticket = 1–3%**; documented failure patterns (in ~half of losing waiver claims): round-number bidding ($25 vs. $27 — ties break by waiver priority, so the better team usually loses the tie), late-season FAAB spent on players whose value doesn't reach the playoff weeks, and hoarding (unspent $80 in Week 14 "is worth exactly what you paid for it: nothing"). In this league (6 playoff teams from W15), Weeks 12–14 spending must map to playoff matchups. → [Calculator Collection, undated 2026-era](https://www.calculatorcollection.org/en/articles/faab-waiver-wire-strategy/); [ESPN, Jul 24, 2026](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/49426681/fantasy-football-strategy-2026-nine-mistakes-avoid). Most leagues run $100 FAAB — matches this format; the dollar amount is relative, what matters is spend discipline and timing. → [FantasyPros, Sep 2025, snippet-verified](https://www.fantasypros.com/2025/09/fantasy-football-faab-waiver-wire-strategy-advice/). Demand/market signal for sizing: %-rostered ownership from a large national contest (400+ leagues) is the replicable methodology. → [FTN Fantasy FAAB primer, 2024, snippet-verified](https://ftnfantasy.com/nfl/2024-fantasy-football-faab-primer)

**R-e4 — Raw transaction count is a weak diagnostic: grade timing and sizing instead.** 167,265 trades from 6,976 completed Sleeper leagues: champions averaged **3.07 trades vs. 2.76** for everyone else — "Trading more helps a little. But it's not how much champions trade that sets them apart. It's **when**." (Dynasty sample; JS-gated, snippet-verified; extrapolate to redraft cautiously.) → [Dynasty Trade Maker](https://dynastytrademaker.com/research/do-teams-that-trade-more-win). Skeptic's counterweight on churn: → [Alex Cates (undated, snippet-verified)](https://www.alexcates.com/post/is-activity-always-good-or-are-we-just-spinning-our-wheels). Volume context: 600,000+ player adds across 2024; redraft volume swells midseason (peak Week 6, 18,092 adds). → [FantasyPros, Sep 3, 2025, snippet-verified](https://www.fantasypros.com/2025/09/fantasy-football-waiver-wire-pickups-win-championships/)

**R-e5 — Flag trades executed 24–48h after a close loss as highest-risk.** Regression-discontinuity analysis of Sleeper data: managers trade significantly more after close losses than after close wins (prospect-theory loss aversion) — the data-based explanation for "panic trades." → [dkro23/fantasy-football-trades (GitHub; undated, snippet-verified)](https://github.com/dkro23/fantasy-football-trades)

**R-e6 — Value a trade by improvement to *your* projected starting lineup, not head-to-head "fairness."** Formal model: trades as mean-variance optimization over weekly lineups. → [Haugh & Singal, SSRN, posted May 31, 2019 / revised Sep 16, 2019](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3393127). The trade market is inefficient enough that +EV trades upgrading both sides ~3 pts/week are systematically findable (real 12-team Week-8 2025 league). → [arXiv 2511.17535, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17535v1). In-season norm: "championship managers are focused on improving their starting lineup, not 'winning' every trade." → [FantraxHQ, 2026, snippet-verified](https://fantraxhq.com/in-season-fantasy-football-trade-strategy-for-2026/)

**R-e7 — Tanking: treat as an unproven myth in this format.** The most complete treatment says tanking pays off **only** where losing is structurally rewarded (inverse-standings order, worst-record rolling waiver priority); in a redraft with lottery/standard order the only real currency is waiver priority, and "the cost is real; the benefit disappears." **No published dataset shows redraft tanking improves next-season outcomes.** ⚠️ The framework source is ANA-network (undated); the commissioner-side view is secondary. → [Fantasy Strategy Guide (undated, ANA)](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/tanking-strategy-fantasy); [Week One (undated)](https://www.weekonefantasy.com/how-can-leagues-prevent-tanking-in-fantasy-football-2857147/)

**R-e8 — ⚠️ Weak single-source number to treat as directional only:** "championship managers made 8.2 waiver pickups in Weeks 1–4 vs. 6.1 for average managers; early waiver activity adds 18% to team PPG" — the source's own disclosures page states its figures come from "publicly available data and proprietary algorithms" with no published dataset or methodology. → [Fantasy Hype, 2026](https://fantasyhype.com/insights/how-to-win-fantasy-football-2026); [disclosures](https://fantasyhype.com/disclosures)

| 11 | **Activity volume: does trading/adding more win?** | More transactions ≈ better (common belief) | Volume barely moves outcomes: champions averaged 3.07 trades vs. 2.76 — "It's **when**" — [Dynasty Trade Maker, undated (JS-gated, snippet-verified)](https://dynastytrademaker.com/research/do-teams-that-trade-more-win); churn may be "spinning our wheels" — [Alex Cates, undated (snippet-verified)](https://www.alexcates.com/post/is-activity-always-good-or-are-we-just-spinning-our-wheels) | Grade timing and sizing; treat raw transaction count as a weak diagnostic. |
| 12 | **Tanking for next year's position** | Rational where future draft value > playoff EV — [Fantasy Strategy Guide, undated (⚠️ ANA)](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/tanking-strategy-fantasy) | Same source's redraft conclusion: with lottery/standard order "the cost is real; the benefit disappears"; **no published dataset shows tanking works in PPR redraft**; commissioner literature treats it as an integrity problem — [Week One, undated](https://www.weekonefantasy.com/how-can-leagues-prevent-tanking-in-fantasy-football-2857147/) | Treat tanking as an unproven myth in this format. If a five-season stretch shows deliberate tanking, describe the behavior and the waiver-priority gain; do not grade it as a rational strategy choice. |

## 3. What bad advice looks like

Ten concrete, checkable failure patterns. A judge can verify any single sentence of the assistant's output against each. (Pattern set synthesized from the four research passes; every pattern is anchored to the cited rule it violates.)

1. **12-team numbers used in this 10-team league.** The sentence cites a 12-team ADP, a 12-man replacement baseline (QB18/RB30/WR42/TE18), or a 12-team lineup-efficiency norm (83.7–86.8%, ~20 pts/week) as the threshold for this league, without a 10-team adaptation or an explicit flag. Check: does the sentence name the league size and format of the cited benchmark? (Grounded in: PFF May 27, 2015; ASL Sep 12, 2026; Elhabr.)
2. **"Reach" without a declared band or a realized-result comparison.** Calls a pick a reach (or a steal) without (a) stating which band it applies (default: 20+ spots among top-75 / 100+ overall) and (b) comparing to the player's end-of-season positional finish or PPG above replacement. ADP gap alone is not a grade. (Grounded in: PFF Jan 13, 2026; PFF Dec 30, 2025.)
3. **Scolding a single week.** Points at one low-efficiency week, one bust, or one bad trade as evidence of a bad manager. The data say single-week signals regress (6-season lost-opportunity deviations of ±4 pts/week; decision errors are only meaningful aggregated over weeks). Check: does the sentence aggregate over weeks/seasons before drawing a conclusion? (Grounded in: Elhabr; Fantasy Genius Oct 27, 2023.)
4. **One-cause season verdicts.** Attributes a losing season purely to draft quality while ignoring points-against / all-play / luck gap — or, mirror image, waves off a lost season as "just bad schedule" without checking lineup efficiency. Both skill channels must appear in the diagnosis. (Grounded in: ffwrapped "unluckiest team"; RotoWire Dec 2024; Morse Sep 15, 2019.)
5. **Bench points = "points left on the bench."** Uses total bench production as the missed-points figure. Only the best *legal* substitution counts: optimal − actual. 52 bench points ≠ 52 missed points. (Grounded in: ffwrapped.)
6. **Efficiency ↔ wins conflation, either direction.** Calls 100% efficiency "great" as if it implies winning, or a losing record as evidence of poor lineup skill. A perfect-efficiency 80-point team loses; the canonically documented champion had *bad* lineup-picking and won. Check: does the sentence equate a rate with a record? (Grounded in: ffwrapped; Morse.)
7. **Close losses read as clutch/anti-clutch evidence.** Uses a close-loss rate to claim a team "doesn't finish" or "plays better in close games." The NFL analogue regresses to noise (+0.08 next-season correlation vs. +0.40 for margin). (Grounded in: NFL Analytic, 2026; ffwrapped.)
8. **Assuming schedule luck averages out.** States or implies that over five seasons the luck gap "evened out." The six-season data show cumulative deviations persisting; the five-season cumulative luck gap must be *reported*, not zeroed. (Grounded in: Elhabr.)
9. **Grading with information the manager didn't have (outcome bias).** Calls a pre-kickoff lineup decision a mistake without the pre-kickoff test (was the starter already ruled out? bye missed? clear role change? — or was the bench boom unpredicted?), or penalizes/praises a roster for points-against (opponent scoring, entirely uncontrollable). (Grounded in: ffwrapped; RotoWire Dec 2024.)
10. **Quoting unsourced "data" as fact, or simple-averaging weekly percentages.** Presents a figure from a no-methodology source (e.g., Fantasy Hype's 8.2-vs-6.1 early pickups — disclosures say "proprietary algorithms"; ANA-network reach bands; Calculatrex letter bands) without a weak-source flag, or computes season efficiency as the simple mean of weekly percentages instead of the total-points-weighted ratio. (Grounded in: Fantasy Hype disclosures; ffwrapped; ANA About page.)

## 4. Glossary — terms the prompt must use consistently

One canonical definition per term. The prompt should enforce these so the assistant never uses a word two ways. Where a definition rests on a source, it is cited.

- **ADP (average draft position).** The average pick number at which a player is taken across tracked *human* drafts of the same format, as of a stated date. Always cite with format + league size + date (10-team PPR); methodology: human picks only, computer picks filtered — [FFC methodology](https://help.fantasyfootballcalculator.com/article/34-average-draft-position-adp-data). Never use a 12-team or half-PPR ADP as the price in this league.
- **Replacement level.** The production of the player you could replace this player with on the waiver wire without giving up value — format-specific. Three published conventions exist (12th man; last starter; post-draft waiver wire); the output must print which one was used — [PFF, May 27, 2015](https://www.pff.com/news/the-value-of-replacement-level); [YAFSB, Mar 11, 2026](https://yafsb.com/content/vor-value-over-replacement-fantasy-football/); [FantasyPros, updated Mar 30, 2020](https://support.fantasypros.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005868747-What-is-value-based-drafting-What-do-player-draft-values-mean-VORP-VONA-VOLS-VBD).
- **PPG above replacement (VOR).** A player's fantasy points per game minus the replacement-level PPG at that position. The grading currency for picks, trades, and waiver moves. Season-total variant (season points above replacement) may be used when stated.
- **12th man.** The 12th-ranked player at each position in a 12-team league — the classic bench-based replacement level (PFF: QB18, RB30, WR42, TE18) — [PFF, May 27, 2015](https://www.pff.com/news/the-value-of-replacement-level). In this 10-team league the analogous slot is the bench/waiver boundary, recomputed per season.
- **Reach.** Taking a player at a pick number materially earlier than his market value, judged against a *declared* band (default: 20+ spots above ADP among the top-75; 100+ overall spots for late-round value) and the player's realized end-of-season positional finish — [PFF, Jan 13, 2026](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-season-in-review-what-nathan-jahnke-got-right-and-wrong-2025).
- **Steal.** The mirror of reach: taken materially later than market value. A consensus top pick taken at its expected slot (e.g., 1.01) is a *fair* pick, not a steal — [LeagueLogs, undated](https://leaguelogs.com/fantasy-football-draft-grades).
- **Opportunity cost / DLD (Draft Leverage Differential).** The value difference between the player taken and the player likely available at your *next* pick — the correct denominator for "did this pick cost me?" — [FantasyLabs (Collinsworth), Jun 28, 2018](https://www.fantasylabs.com/articles/fantasy-football-rankings-draft-leverage-differential-historical-data/).
- **Bust.** A player who, after playing the large majority of his games, finished materially below his draft-slot expectation (large adverse ADP-vs-finish positional gap). Injury-driven misses are excluded from draft-error accounting — [PFF, Dec 30, 2025](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-pff-2025-fantasy-football-awards).
- **Cliff.** A steep drop in value between adjacent positional ranks; the last player above the cliff is pay-up-eligible — [YAFSB, Mar 11, 2026](https://yafsb.com/content/vor-value-over-replacement-fantasy-football/).
- **Optimal lineup.** The highest-scoring *legal* lineup from a week's roster using final scores, respecting slot eligibility, each player counted once — [ffwrapped, undated](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/fantasy-football-lineup-efficiency).
- **Lineup efficiency.** Actual starter points ÷ optimal lineup points × 100, per week. Season figure = **total-points-weighted ratio, never the simple average of weekly percentages** — [ffwrapped, undated](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/fantasy-football-lineup-efficiency).
- **Points left on the bench.** Optimal points − actual points. Not total bench points — [ffwrapped, undated](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/fantasy-football-lineup-efficiency).
- **PF / PA (points for / points against).** PF = points your team scored (roster-quality signal). PA = points your *opponents* scored against you — entirely uncontrollable; a schedule-luck measure, never a skill credit or debit — [RotoWire, Dec 2024](https://www.rotowire.com/football/article/points-for-points-against-explained-95311); [Sleeper blog, 2025](https://sleeper.com/blog/what-is-pf-and-pa-in-fantasy-football/).
- **All-play record.** The record if you had played every other team every week (in a 10-team league the week's top scorer goes 9-0) — removes scheduled matchup — [ffwrapped, undated](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/unluckiest-team).
- **Expected wins.** All-play win % × 14 regular-season games. **Luck gap** = expected wins − actual wins, with PF/PA as tiebreakers — [ffwrapped, undated](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/unluckiest-team).
- **Close loss.** A loss within a defined margin; report <5-point and <10-point counts separately. Evidence of *timing*, not of a clutch skill trait — [ffwrapped, undated](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/unluckiest-team); [NFL Analytic, 2026](https://nflanalytic.com/explainer-close-game-luck.html).
- **Actual − projected gap.** Realized points minus preseason projection. Default interpretation: mostly player-outcome luck (injury, lost playing time) with a small management overlay, applied only after the pre-kickoff test — [Elhabr, undated](https://tonyelhabr.rbind.io/posts/fantasy-football-performance/); [ffwrapped, undated](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/fantasy-football-lineup-efficiency).
- **Correct Decision Rate (CDR).** Correct start/sit decisions ÷ rostered weeks, aggregated over weeks; a single week's "incorrect" call is not a finding — [Fantasy Genius (Barton), Oct 27, 2023](https://fantasygenius.substack.com/p/decision-disasters-a-statistical).
- **Snake round.** Draft order that alternates direction: Round 1 runs picks 1→10, Round 2 runs 10→1, and so on; this league is 14 rounds × 10 teams = 140 picks. "Round N" always means the Nth pass around the order; "your slot" means your starting pick number before that round's direction is applied.
- **FAAB (free-agent acquisition budget).** The auction budget ($100 per manager here) spent to claim free agents when waiver priority alone can't. The dollar amount is relative across leagues; what is graded is spend *discipline and timing* (phases: 30–50% by Week 5; 30–40% Weeks 5–10; push Weeks 11+) — [FantasyPros, Sep 2025 (snippet-verified)](https://www.fantasypros.com/2025/09/fantasy-football-faab-waiver-wire-strategy-advice/); [Calculator Collection, undated](https://www.calculatorcollection.org/en/articles/faab-waiver-wire-strategy/).
- **Waiver priority.** The claim queue for free agents; in this league it is rolling (resets after each claim), so a team that claims moves behind the field. FAAB bid ties break by waiver priority, which is why a better team usually loses a tie — [Calculator Collection, undated](https://www.calculatorcollection.org/en/articles/faab-waiver-wire-strategy/).
- **FLEX.** The 11th starting slot (QB/RB/RB/WR/WR/TE/**FLEX**/K/DEF) that may be filled by an RB, WR, or TE. Consequence for bench value: the backup at the position *not* filling flex is the most valuable (RB backup ≈ 70.8–72.6% of starter value when flex is a WR) — [4for4 (Zatloukal), Jul 23, 2015](https://www.4for4.com/2015/preseason/what-value-bench-player-fantasy-football).
- **2-TE.** A league format that starts two tight ends. **Not this league's format (1-TE).** If the assistant uses 2-TE logic to price TE here, it is a format error; the FLEX makes a second TE optional, not required — [DraftSharks, Aug 22, 2026](https://www.draftsharks.com/article/fantasy-football-draft-strategy-guide/10-team-ppr).

## 5. How to present the report card

Practical guidance for making a five-season retrospective useful rather than scolding. Each item is cited to the research it comes from; items marked *(synthesis)* are my consolidation of the findings, not a single cited claim.

**Lead with the net position of the same roster, then process — not with a grade letter.**
The three headline numbers for every season: (1) points left on the bench (lineup execution), (2) luck gap (expected wins − actual wins), (3) net points vs. the league. This mirrors the published skill/luck split — lineup execution is the controllable channel, "Luck" is defined separately as wins vs. expected (League History uses Monte-Carlo schedule simulations for exactly this separation) — [League History glossary, current 2026](https://www.leaguehistory.app/research/glossary). Then give the one-sentence season diagnosis first, using ffwrapped's profile language: high PF + high PA + strong all-play + wins below expected + multiple close losses reads as "competitive roster, unlucky schedule" — [ffwrapped, undated](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/unluckiest-team). A diagnosis that names *which channel* cost the season before any criticism is the difference between useful and scolding. *(Synthesis of the two cited sources.)*

**Separate what the manager controlled from what they didn't — explicitly, every season.**
- Controlled: draft picks, lineup decisions, waiver/FAAB claims, trades.
- Uncontrollable: injuries, schedule (PA is the sum of opponents' scores — [RotoWire, Dec 2024](https://www.rotowire.com/football/article/points-for-points-against-explained-95311)), and the points differential that resulted.
- Gray zone: actual − projected player production — run the pre-kickoff test (was the starter already ruled out, was a bye or clear role change missed?) before classifying it as process or luck — [ffwrapped, undated](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/fantasy-football-lineup-efficiency).
- Hard rules: never charge a manager for opponent production; PA is not an excuse either — it shows *where* the luck went, and must be paired with efficiency — [ffwrapped, undated](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/unluckiest-team).

**Grade decision quality, not outcomes — in both directions.**
The champion's own data is the template: even a title-winning manager benched a QB for eight straight weeks while outscored 7 of 8 — "the most dangerous kind of feedback, because it rewards a broken process." Score decisions against decision-time information: a win does not launder a wrong call, and a loss does not convict a right one — [Marketing with Dave, updated Mar 2026](https://marketingwithdave.com/the-data-behind-my-2025-nfl-fantasy-football-championship/). This is why the card shows, per decision, the defensible alternative that was available at that moment (opportunity-cost frame: "RB14 was projected at your next pick — the DLD cost of this pick is 142.5") — [FantasyLabs, Jun 28, 2018](https://www.fantasylabs.com/articles/fantasy-football-rankings-draft-leverage-differential-historical-data/).

**Tone rules (specific + attributable + actionable, never shaming).**
1. Every criticism names the player, the pick/week, the number, and the Section 1 rule it violated — so a judge can check one sentence against a Section 3 pattern. *(Synthesis.)*
2. Never a single-week finding: aggregate over weeks before concluding (about ±4 pts/week 6-season band; CDR is only meaningful aggregated) — [Elhabr, undated](https://tonyelhabr.rbind.io/posts/fantasy-football-performance/); [Fantasy Genius, Oct 27, 2023](https://fantasygenius.substack.com/p/decision-disasters-a-statistical).
3. Praise with the same precision: a steal is stated in spots ("WR12 value at pick 58"), a good trade in points/week added to your starting lineup — [FantraxHQ, 2026 (snippet-verified)](https://fantraxhq.com/in-season-fantasy-football-trade-strategy-for-2026/).
4. State the convention used (reach band, replacement baseline, efficiency denominator) in the output itself — the analyzer's own transparency is the model for the manager's.

**Per-season card structure** *(synthesis, ordered per the researchers' report-card takeaways):*
1. **Header:** record; PF; PA; all-play; expected wins; luck gap; net points; league finish; one-sentence diagnosis.
2. **Draft:** declared reach/steal band; per-pick table of ADP slot vs. end-of-season positional finish with PPG above replacement (healthy-play filter applied); slot-quality note (R-a11); positional-shape check against the 10-team PPR norms (R-b1–b9), explicitly noting the manager's declared RB strategy and QB/TE timing.
3. **Lineup efficiency:** points-weighted season figure; weekly range; comparison to the league's own internal baseline (never an imported 12-team norm); count of "bench decisions that would have changed the result."
4. **Waivers / FAAB / trades:** moves ranked by rest-of-season points-above-replacement vs. the league-best move available that week ([League History, current 2026](https://www.leaguehistory.app/research/glossary)); FAAB % spent by phase vs. the 30–50% / 30–40% / push benchmark ([Calculator Collection, undated](https://www.calculatorcollection.org/en/articles/faab-waiver-wire-strategy/)); trades executed 24–48h after a close loss flagged (highest-risk class — [dkro23/fantasy-football-trades, undated](https://github.com/dkro23/fantasy-football-trades)); the league-winning pickups of that year the manager held or missed ([PFF, Jan 14, 2026](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-waiver-wire-pickups-that-won-2025-leagues)).
5. **Luck note:** PA context; close losses (<5 / <10) as timing evidence; actual − projected with pre-kickoff classification; injuries as excluded-from-fault.

**Five-season rollup (the closing page):**
- **Cumulative luck gap — reported, not zeroed.** Even six seasons of data did not fully even out schedule luck — [Elhabr, undated](https://tonyelhabr.rbind.io/posts/fantasy-football-performance/).
- **Persistence = the skill test.** Lost-opportunity vs. league average per season: a manager *consistently* below average across multiple seasons carries a genuine skill signal (single-season outliers wash out, about ±4 pts/week extremes) — [Elhabr, undated](https://tonyelhabr.rbind.io/posts/fantasy-football-performance/). The peer-reviewed method for why persistence is the right test: half-vs-half win-fraction correlation, volume effects, and random-algorithm controls — [MIT News, Nov 7, 2018](https://news.mit.edu/2018/hosoi-study-skill-fantasy-sports-1107) (paper: [SIAM Review 60(4), 2018](https://doi.org/10.1137/16m1102094)); cross-sport backing from ~1M FPL managers — [PLOS ONE, 2020](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0246698).
- **Recurring mistakes count.** For each R-e9 pattern, how many seasons it appeared in: present in 3+ of 5 seasons = a trait to name; once = an event, not a pattern. *(Synthesis rule, flagged.)*
- **Net-points trend** vs. league and vs. the best-possible version of the same roster each season.
- **The verdict is a channel, not a score:** which of the four controllable channels (draft / lineup / waivers / trades) is this manager's binding constraint — that is the part a manager can act on, and it is the whole point of the card. *(Synthesis.)*
