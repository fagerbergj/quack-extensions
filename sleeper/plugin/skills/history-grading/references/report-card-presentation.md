# How to present the report card

Source: `research-r8-history-grading-v2.md` §5.

## Lead with net position, then process, not a grade letter

The three headline numbers for every season: (1) points left on the
bench (lineup execution), (2) luck gap (expected wins minus actual
wins), (3) net points vs. the league. Give the one-sentence season
diagnosis first, naming which channel cost the season - a diagnosis that
names the channel before any criticism is the difference between useful
and scolding ([ffwrapped](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/unluckiest-team)).

## Separate controlled from uncontrolled, every season

- Controlled: draft picks, lineup decisions, waiver/FAAB claims, trades.
- Uncontrollable: injuries, schedule (PA is the sum of opponents'
  scores), and the resulting points differential.
- Gray zone: actual minus projected player production - run the
  pre-kickoff test (`luck-vs-skill.md`) before classifying it as process
  or luck.
- Never charge a manager for opponent production; PA is not an excuse
  either - pair it with efficiency.

## Grade decision quality, not outcomes

A win does not launder a wrong call, and a loss does not convict a right
one. The champion's own data is the template: even a title-winning
manager benched a QB for eight straight weeks while outscored 7 of 8 -
"the most dangerous kind of feedback, because it rewards a broken
process" ([Marketing with Dave, updated Mar 2026](https://marketingwithdave.com/the-data-behind-my-2025-nfl-fantasy-football-championship/)).
Show, per decision, the defensible alternative available at that moment.

## Tone rules

1. Every criticism names the player, the pick/week, the number, and the
   rule it violated.
2. Never a single-week finding - aggregate over weeks before concluding.
3. Praise with the same precision a criticism gets: a steal is stated in
   spots, a good trade in points/week added to the starting lineup.
4. State the convention used (reach band, replacement baseline,
   efficiency denominator) in the output itself.

## Per-season card structure

1. Header: record; PF; PA; all-play; expected wins; luck gap; net
   points; league finish; one-sentence diagnosis.
2. Draft: declared reach/steal band; per-pick ADP-vs-finish table with
   PPG above replacement; slot-quality note; positional-shape check.
3. Lineup efficiency: points-weighted season figure; weekly range;
   comparison to the league's own history, never an imported norm.
4. Luck note: PA context; close losses (<5/<10) as timing evidence;
   actual minus projected with pre-kickoff classification; injuries
   excluded from fault.

## Five-season rollup

- Cumulative luck gap, reported, not zeroed.
- Persistence as the skill test - a manager consistently below average
  across seasons carries a genuine signal.
- Net-points trend vs. league and vs. the best-possible version of the
  same roster each season.
- The verdict is a channel, not a score: name which controllable channel
  (draft / lineup / waivers / trades) is the manager's binding
  constraint - that is what they can act on.
