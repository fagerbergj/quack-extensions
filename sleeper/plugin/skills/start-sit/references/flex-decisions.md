# FLEX decisions (single FLEX, RB/WR/TE, full PPR)

Source: `research-r1-startsit.md` §1.10.

## The mechanical difference

A fixed slot asks "is this player better than my best bench player at the
same position?" The FLEX asks "who among *all* remaining RB/WR/TE
candidates has the highest expected points?" Fill the five dedicated slots
(QB, 2×RB, 2×WR, TE) first, then rank the remaining flex-eligible players —
usually RB3, WR3, sometimes TE2 — against each other, not against a
positional leader elsewhere in the league.

## Format shifts the pool toward receivers

Full PPR data ([4for4, Aug 12, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/how-win-flex-position-your-fantasy-football-league); 2024-2025):

- Full PPR: 51 WRs vs. 42 RBs averaged ≥8.0 PPR points/game in 2025 (62 vs.
  41 in 2024). WR3/WR4 carries more upside than RB3/RB4 in this scoring
  format.
- Override: if the RB candidate has a significantly more favorable matchup
  than the WR candidates, start the RB anyway — format is a prior, not a
  rule.

## Tie-break rules

- **Projection gap <3 points:** stop comparing means, compare floors. Prefer
  the higher floor when you need to lock in points; prefer the higher
  ceiling when you're a clear underdog and need variance.
- **Projection gap ≥3 points with a usage-driven explanation:** follow the
  usage/projection gap.
- **Usage as tiebreaker:** a receiver at ~28% target share on a pass-heavy
  offense beats one coming off a big game on ~12% target share; a
  target-starved receiver rarely delivers a startable week regardless of
  matchup.
- **TE in the FLEX** is almost always a volume play — a 20%+ target share
  functioning as a de facto WR2. Starting a TE for touchdown upside alone,
  without that usage base, is a lower-probability decision. No published
  PPR-specific numeric TE-flex threshold exists; say so if the case turns on
  one.

## Stakes

A missed FLEX call typically costs a few points; a missed availability call
costs the player's entire floor. Weight FLEX decisions as lower-stakes than
availability calls, and never let FLEX optimization delay resolving an
injury designation elsewhere in the lineup.
