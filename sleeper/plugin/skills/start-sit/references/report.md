# R1 Research Report: Weekly Start/Sit Decisions — Decision Rules, Numeric Thresholds, and Source Conflicts

**Scope and league context.** This report covers topic R1 — weekly lineup start/sit decisions — for a 10-team redraft, full-PPR Sleeper league: single FLEX (RB/WR/TE), lineup QB/RB/RB/WR/WR/TE/FLEX/K/DEF + 5 bench, 100 FAAB rolling waivers, 6-of-10 playoff field from week 15. Sources span 2007–2026; the current season is 2026, Week 2 (as of 2026-09-18), with 2024 and 2025 as the historical comparison window. Every source is dated at first mention and again in the source list at the end. Where **no published numeric threshold exists**, that absence is stated explicitly as a finding — not filled with guesses. Content-farm/secondary sources with unverified numbers are flagged wherever they appear.

---

## 1. Decision rules and numeric thresholds

### 1.1 Projections, floor, and ceiling

- **What a projection is.** A projection is the probability-weighted mean of a production distribution, not a promise of outcome; the mean of the distribution dominates, with variance/correlation secondary ([Fantasy Math, Nathan Braun, 2026](https://nathanbraun.com/fantasymath/)). The dominant aggregator does not run its own NFL model: FantasyPros projections are aggregated from sources "such as CBS, numberFire and STATS" ([FantasyPros support, "Where do player projections come from?", undated help page](https://support.fantasypros.com/hc/en-us/articles/115001319147-Where-do-player-projections-come-from); its 2025 Week 1 tables label the output "Consensus of 5 Sources" — [FantasyPros Week 1 QB Projections, Sep 8, 2025](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/projections/qb.php?max-yes=true&min-yes=true&week=1)). Its ECR system converts each expert's rank into weighted rank points, and its published in-season accuracy method snapshots boards at Thursday-night kickoff and 1 p.m. ET Sunday — the same timing a lineup lock implies ([FantasyPros in-season accuracy methodology FAQ, undated](https://www.fantasypros.com/about/faq/football-inseason-accuracy-methodology/)).
- **Working definitions (flagged editorial):** floor ≈ 10th–20th percentile outcome; ceiling ≈ 85th–90th percentile ([Fantasy Projection Lab, undated](https://fantasyprojectionlab.com/floor-and-ceiling-projections) — no stated data behind these percentiles).
- **The PPR floor mechanism.** In PPR scoring an RB carry is worth **0.59 expected fantasy points vs. 1.57 per target** — a RB's floor is set by rush volume, his ceiling by targets, and game script moves that split ([PFF, "Metrics that Matter: Fantasy options most dependent on gamescript," Scott Barrett, Jul 5, 2017, 2016 data](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-metrics-that-matter-fantasy-options-most-dependent-on-gamescript)).
- **Published variance metrics (the closest thing to floor/ceiling thresholds):**
  - **ESPN Consistency Rating (CR)** = weekly standard deviation ÷ weekly PPR average (lower = more consistent), plus a Start% statistic, computed through Week 1 of 2026 (2024–2026 = 37 games of data) ([ESPN, Tristan H. Cockcroft, "Fantasy football consistency ratings," Sep 16, 2026](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/49954186/fantasy-football-rankings-consistency-ratings-start-sit-value)). Borderline rule this supports: compare (projection − CR·k) across candidates; lower-CR player wins the close call. ESPN publishes the metric, **not a numeric k**.
  - **FantasyPros 2026 draft rankings publish a STD column** (standard deviation of draft rank across experts) — e.g., Jahmyr Gibbs 0.8 vs. Puka Nacua 1.7 ([FantasyPros 2026 Draft Rankings, STD view, accessed 2026 season](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/rankings/?scoring=STD&type=draft)).
  - FantasyPros' methodology framing treats the projection as the 50th percentile and makes variance an explicit ranking input ([FantasyPros, "Baking Consistency Into Player Rankings," 2020](https://www.fantasypros.com/2020/03/baking-consistency-into-player-draft-rankings-2020-fantasy-baseball/) — baseball edition, same framework).
- **Sourced switch rule:** use a variance statistic (ESPN CR or a computed weekly std dev) plus the projection gap, and require the gap to exceed the variance before switching starters (constructed from the ESPN 2026 and FantasyPros STD metrics above).
- **Explicit finding — no published threshold:** no primary source publishes a "sit if projection < X" or "floor below Y → sit" rule. The frequently-cited bands ("floor–ceiling gap <12 pts = safe, >20 pts = boom/bust") come from content-farm sites with no stated data ([Fantasy Projection Lab, undated](https://fantasyprojectionlab.com/floor-and-ceiling-projections)) — treat as unverified editorial.
- **2026 in-season workflow reference:** ESPN's weekly Playbook (Mike Clay, **Sep 17, 2026**, Week 2) pairs projections with lineup locks and WR/CB Shadow Reports — a live primary example of projection + matchup context co-determining the call ([ESPN Fantasy Playbook Week 2 2026](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/49948423/fantasy-playbook-projections-lineup-locks-2026-week-2-shadow-reports)).

### 1.2 Injury designations (Questionable / Doubtful / Out) → probability of playing

- **Current official definitions contain no percentages** (NFL.com, Competition Committee revision, **Aug 21, 2016**): Questionable = "uncertain as to whether the player will play"; Doubtful = "unlikely the player will participate"; Out = "will not play"; Limited Participation = "less than 100 percent of a player's normal repetitions"; Full Participation = "100 percent" ([NFL.com revision notice](https://www.nfl.com/news/competition-committee-approves-revisions-to-injury-report-0ap3000000688693)). The old numeric definitions (Q ≈ 50% likelihood; D ≈ 75% likelihood of *not* playing) were retired in 2016 because teams weren't honoring them, and "Probable" was eliminated because ~95% of Probable players played ([ESPN, Kevin Seifert, Aug 21–22, 2016](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/17361111/nfl-streamlines-injured-players-game-status-removes-probable-designation); [NBC Sports account of the Competition Committee decision, 2016](https://www.nbcsports.com/nfl/profootballtalk/rumor-mill/news/nfl-drops-probable-from-injury-report-redefines-questionable-and-doubtful)); the policy says any player with "any question" of availability "should be listed as 'questionable.'" An ESPN medical analyst contemporaneously argued "uncertain" can legitimately mean "we think he's got a good chance but can't be two-days-out certain," letting relatively healthy players ride the report ([ESPN, Stephania Bell, 2016, print archive](https://www.espn.com/blog/stephania-bell/insider/print?id=3416&imagesPrint=off)). The live 2026 report (Week 2) shows exactly three practice states and three game states — no Probable remains ([NFL.com Injury Report, live, accessed 09/18/2026](https://www.nfl.com/injuries/)). Definitions of the three states and the 2026 filing schedule are also documented at [BettingNews, Sept 2, 2026](https://www.bettingnews.com/nfl/guides/injury-reports/).
- **Published play rates — final-report designation → suiting up:**

| Source (date) | Sample | Q → played | D → played |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Footballguys, Adam Hutchison (pub. 09/13/2025)](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2025-chance-to-play-questionable-vs-doubtful) | QB/RB/WR/TE, 2017–2024, 2,000+ injuries | **70.7%** | **6.9%** |
| [Footballguys (pub. 09/01/2024)](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2024-injury-index-chance-to-play-questionable-vs-doubtful) | QB/RB/WR/TE, 2017–2023, 2,000+ | **71%** | **5.9%** |
| [Footballguys (pub. 08/30/2023)](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2023-footballguys-injury-report) | QB/RB/WR/TE, 2017–2022, 1,500+ | **71.5%** | **4.9%** |
| [Banged Up Bills, PFR-based (07/12/2025)](https://bangedupbills.com/2025/07/12/how-do-the-buffalo-bills-compare-against-the-nfl-utilizing-the-questionable-designation/) | All positions, 2017–2024 | **68.26%** (peak 73.89% 2021; low 64.84% 2019) | **1.25%** (no team had a Doubtful player suit up in 2024; only 10 teams had even one in 8 years) |
| [San Diego Union-Tribune (09/08/2017)](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/2017/09/08/different-teams-different-nfl-injury-report-designations/) | 2016 season, all 32 teams | **78%** (55% in 2015, when Probable existed) | **4%** (8 of 207) |

  **Conflict flag:** Doubtful is **6.9%** (Footballguys, skill positions only) vs. **1.25%** (Banged Up Bills, all positions) for the same 2017–2024 window — same period, opposite magnitudes (the league-wide figure is diluted by defensive players who are frequently designated Doubtful and occasionally play). Questionable ranges 68–78% depending on sample. Both agree directionally: Doubtful ≈ "will not play," and the pre-2017 "Doubtful = 25% chance" textbook number is **wrong for the modern era**. Pre-2016 context: post-change Questionable players played ~73% in 2016 vs. ~60–65% pre-change ([Zach Binney, 2016](https://zachbinney.com/blog_posts/Eliminating-probable.html)); a 2006–07-era table (USA Today, **2007**, via [archive](http://usatoday30.usatoday.com/sports/football/nfl/2007-11-22-injury-report-cover_N.htm)) is historical only and was superseded by the 2016 policy change.
- **Team is a first-order variable — league averages are not.** 2016 Questionable play rates ran Washington 92% vs. Tampa Bay 11% vs. Tennessee 33% ([Union-Tribune, 09/08/2017](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/2017/09/08/different-teams-different-nfl-injury-report-designations/)); 2024 rates ran Tampa Bay 94.83% and Indianapolis 88.24% vs. Pittsburgh 22.22%, Denver 41.67%, Buffalo 44.9% ([Banged Up Bills, 07/12/2025](https://bangedupbills.com/2025/07/12/how-do-the-buffalo-bills-compare-against-the-nfl-utilizing-the-questionable-designation/)); 2015 team rates spanned 31–85% ([Zach Binney, 2016](https://zachbinney.com/blog_posts/Eliminating-probable.html)).
- Even the most-cited source caveats its own number: the 70.7% "does not necessarily reflect" the official definition "as some NFL teams do not use the Questionable tag correctly" — it is a league average, not a player- or team-specific rate ([Footballguys, 09/13/2025](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2025-chance-to-play-questionable-vs-doubtful)).
- **No current official NFL play-rate table exists** for Q/D/Out; the NFL's only published figure is the ~95% Probable-removal justification ([ESPN, Aug 21–22, 2016](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/17361111/nfl-streamlines-injured-players-game-status-removes-probable-designation)). Every figure above is third-party.
- **Platform mapping (this league is on Sleeper):** Sleeper "mirrors the official NFL injury designations as closely as possible"; its "Out" is a **game-week status** that clears on the NFL week rollover (early Wednesday ET) unless re-issued, and byes carry designations forward ([Sleeper Support, "Injury Statuses and IR Eligibility," updated Jun 4, 2026](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3570017-injury-statuses-and-ir-eligibility)). No "day-to-day" category exists on the platform — that term is coach-speak, not a platform field. No published official mapping table from NFL designation → platform status exists; the practical mapping is 1:1 mirroring.

### 1.3 Practice-report combinations (DNP / LP / FP, Wednesday → Friday)

- **Codes:** DNP = did not participate; LP = limited participation (<100% of normal reps); FP = full participation (100% of normal reps) ([NFL.com, Aug 21, 2016](https://www.nfl.com/news/competition-committee-approves-revisions-to-injury-report-0ap3000000688693); [BettingNews, Sept 2, 2026](https://www.bettingnews.com/nfl/guides/injury-reports/)).
- **Open published table — final (Friday) practice status → chance to suit up** ([Footballguys, pub. 09/01/2024](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2024-injury-index-chance-to-play-practice-participation); every NFL team's weekly practice report 2017–2023, 2,000+ injuries):

| Final Friday practice | All injuries | Concussion | Knee | Hamstring |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FP | **86%** | 85% | 92% | 73% |
| LP | **71%** | 57% | 81% | 62% |
| DNP | **29%** | 29% | 28% | 20% |

  The 2025 update of this table (data through 2024) **exists but is paywalled** ([Footballguys, pub. 09/01/2025](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2025-practice-participation-and-chance-to-play)) — the 2024-edition numbers are the open proxy; treat them as one season stale.
- **Pattern rules for Wednesday→Thursday→Friday sequences** ("DNP-DNP-DNP = Out in all but name"; "DNP-LP-LP or LP-DNP-LP = Questionable territory"; "DNP-DNP-LP = lean sit"; "FP all week → start with confidence; LP-LP-LP → start with mild concern") appear only in secondary guides ([Fantasy Injury Report Authority, 2026](https://fantasyinjuryreportauthority.com/dnp-limited-full-practice-reports/); [Fantasy Start/Sit, undated](https://fantasystartsit.com/injury-reports-and-start-sit)) — **editorial heuristics, not data**. No league-wide play rate is published for any W/T/F sequence. (One research node rated that domain low-trust SEO content with unverifiable attributions; another used it as accessible guidance — see the source-trust conflict in §2.7.)
- One quasi-data usage-share point: "strong majority of WRs with a FP → LP → FP practice report achieve >80% of their avg production" ([FantasyPros Week 3 2025 injury notes, 09/2025](https://www.fantasypros.com/2025/09/fantasy-football-injury-updates-predictions-for-week-3/)) — a usage number, not an availability number.
- **Wednesday → Friday information gain:** Friday is the most predictive single data point — it is both the final practice report and the final game-status filing deadline (schedule in §1.8). FantasyPros' "Are They Playing?" model reports ~80–90% overall accuracy and **error dropping ~19% from Wednesday to Friday** (Brier-score table) ([FantasyPros blog, 2025](https://blog.fantasypros.com/are-they-playing-predict-injury-outcomes-with-confidence/); [product upgrade post, 09/2025](https://www.fantasypros.com/2025/09/know-whos-playing-and-whos-not-are-they-playing-just-got-a-major-upgrade/)) — vendor-reported, but the only published quantification of the Wed→Fri accuracy gain found.
- **"Plays" ≠ "plays at full snap share"** — three published anchors:
  1. The LP definition itself is the snap-share mechanism: a healthy player can be LP all week (load management), and a player can suit up while LP with a hidden cap ([NFL.com, Aug 21, 2016](https://www.nfl.com/news/competition-committee-approves-revisions-to-injury-report-0ap3000000688693)).
  2. Published production-dip data for players who *do* play back: RB high-ankle sprain ≈ **−18.4 fantasy points first week back, ~−19–21 points over the following 3 games**; WR inverted ankle ≈ −15.9 first week; QB concussion ≈ +0.5 (no dip) ([Footballguys, 08/30/2023](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2023-footballguys-injury-report); 2017–2022 data).
  3. The inactive list says nothing about volume — a player off the inactives can still play a cut-down count (secondary guides only, unverified attribution).
- **Explicit finding — no published statistic exists** for P(≥80% snap share | active + LP Friday). That gap should be stated, not filled.

### 1.4 Matchup

- **Quantified magnitude** ([The Fantasy Footballers, "Making the Most of Matchups," Aug 3, 2021](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/articles/the-fantasy-football-mythbusters-making-the-most-of-matchups/); 2005–2020 nflfastR, half-PPR): per one-spot improvement in opponent defensive rank, expected points change by **−0.07 (QB), −0.13 (RB), −0.09 (WR), 0.00 (TE, statistically insignificant)**. The gap between the #1 and #32 ranked run defenses is **~4.2 fantasy PPG across all RBs on an offense** (5–7 PPG for non-top-5 teams); top-5 RB offenses are "matchup-proof" (only 3.6 PPG difference top-5 vs. bottom-5 defenses). Crucially, **offense rank predicts ~3× better than defense rank** (same source).
- **The Athletic** runs weekly matchup rankings built on **APA (Adjusted Points Allowed)** — points allowed adjusted for opponent quality — with the explicit published caveat: *"Use these to make tough calls with players close in start/sit choices. Does **not** mean you bench a stud with a tough APA for a flier with a good matchup"* ([Jake Ciely, Dec 24, 2024, 2024 season Week 17](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/5744918/2024/12/24/fantasy-football-matchup-rankings-strength-of-schedule/), retrieved via Wayback Machine snapshot of Jan 8, 2025). Rest-of-season positional schedule strength is published weekly ([The Athletic, Oct 31, 2024](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/5883564/2024/10/31/rest-of-season-schedule-strength-the-case-to-trade-lamar-jackson-derrick-henry/)).
- **ESPN Shadow Reports** track which CB each WR actually lines up against, used to justify weekly WR upgrades/downgrades ([Week 4, 2025 edition](https://www.espn.com.sg/fantasy/football/insider/story/_/id/41420187/fantasy-football-wide-receiver-cornerback-matchups-upgrades-nfl-week-4); [Week 2, 2026 edition, Sep 17, 2026](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/49948423/fantasy-playbook-projections-lineup-locks-2026-week-2-shadow-reports)).
- **Fantasy Life** publishes a public Defense-vs-Position (DvP) table — fPPG allowed plus a "Fantasy Boost" vs. league average per position ([DvP tool, undated](https://www.fantasylife.com/tools/fantasy-football-rb-dvp)) — and uses the boost in live decision writing: in the 2025–26 playoffs (Jan 2026), Broncos RB RJ Harvey got an xFinity Boost of **+2.0** vs. the Bills while the opposing Broncos matchup was **−2.3** ([Fantasy Life DFS Divisional Round plays, Jan 2026](https://www.fantasylife.com/articles/dfs/nfl-dfs-divisional-round-plays-jaxon-smith-njigba-colston-lovela)). PFF runs a published weekly matchup chart on its own efficiency data ([PFF, Week 1 of 2026, Nathan Jahnke, Sep 16, 2026](https://www.pff.com/)).
- **Reliability constraint:** opponent-adjusted points allowed is unreliable early in a season and can be gamed by schedule; opponent adjustment (APA, DvP boosts) is the standard-practice fix, but **no primary source publishes a stable "minimum games before trust" number** (the circulating "8–10 games" figure comes from the flagged content-farm network).
- **Sourced practical rule:** apply a ±1–2 rank or ~2–4-point adjustment **only** when the DvP/APA signal is opponent-adjusted, season-stable (multiple weeks/seasons), and the two candidates are within one standard deviation of each other.

### 1.5 Game script (favorite/underdog, low/high total)

- **Pre-game script predictability** ([The Fantasy Footballers, "Flip the (Game) Script," Jul 15, 2021](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/articles/the-fantasy-football-mythbusters-flip-the-game-script/); all regular-season games 2000–2020): the favored team wins **66%** of the time; teams favored **10+ points win 85%**; the pre-game total correlates **.32** with actual combined score and the spread **.42** with final margin.
- **Win rates by spread size:** a 7+ point favorite actually wins **79%**; a 1–3 point favorite wins just **51%** ([Fantasy Points, Graham Barfield, Aug 27, 2020](https://legacy.fantasypoints.com/nfl/articles/2020/how-game-script-affects-fantasy); 2019 season data).
- **Mechanism, quantified:** teams passed **66% of plays trailing vs. 51% leading** ([PFF, Jul 5, 2017](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-metrics-that-matter-fantasy-options-most-dependent-on-gamescript); 2016 data). Running backs are the *only* position whose average fantasy production rises when the team leads; QBs/WRs/TEs all benefit from trailing (same source).
- **RB workhorse numbers:** Derrick Henry was **−12.8 fantasy PPG in losses vs. wins, with ~7 more carries per game in wins** — the clearest single-player gamescript effect published ([SI via WKKY mirror, Michael Fabiano, Aug 10, 2021](https://wkky.com/2021-08-10/game-script-impact-on-fantasy-football-production-running-backs/); 2020 data). Conversely, pass-catching RBs get *better* when trailing (Kamara +7.9, McKissic +6.3 in the same study).
- **The counter-position, in full:** the same TFF 2021 study found that as the spread widens, players on the favorite outscore their underdog counterparts at every position — WRs on favorites average **~42 more yards** than WRs on 7+ underdogs; the "underdog WR garbage-time upside" narrative is a small *share* gain that loses to the smaller *pie*. Their verdict: **points (team-level score projection) dominate game-script narrative** — pick the higher-scoring team's players rather than playing the script ([TFF, Jul 15, 2021](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/articles/the-fantasy-football-mythbusters-flip-the-game-script/)).
- **DFS-side 2026 still teaches the classic playbook:** shootout = total 48+ & spread ≤3; grind = total <41; favorite-RB/underdog-QB stacks ([DFS Only game-script guide, May 2026](https://onlydfs.com/blog/nfl-dfs-game-script-vegas.html)). Both camps remain active in 2026 content: the 2021 multi-position dataset favors "favorite-side volume," while the single-workhorse RB data favors the classic rule (see §2.4).
- **2025 league context (for scaling):** 2025 league averages were **32.1 pass / 26.8 rush per game, 45.6% run rate** ([netwins analysis, undated substack, 2025 season data](https://netwins.substack.com/p/the-nfls-most-misunderstood-debate)); pass rates settled just over 60% and 2025 had the fewest plays per team since 2000 ([Fantasy Life 2025 Year in Review, Dec 2025](https://www.fantasylife.com/articles/fantasy/nfl-2025-year-in-review-christian-mccaffrey-proves-rbs-still-matter)). Interactive per-player gamescript splits for current data: [League Station Game Script Profiler, updated Sep 17, 2026](https://www.leaguestation.com/analytics/game-script).
- **Explicit findings — no published thresholds:** (1) no 2024/2025-season peer-reviewed game-script study exists — the quantitative RB-script literature peaks at 2016–2020 data; (2) no primary source publishes a "favorite by N → RB floor +X" table. The usable sourced numbers are the win-probability rates (66% / 79% / 85%) and the pass-rate split (66% trailing vs. 51% leading).

### 1.6 Weather

- **Player-level multipliers** ([PFF, "Quantifying weather's impact on fantasy performance," Scott Spratt, May 7, 2018](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-quantifying-weathers-impact-on-fantasy-performance); 2017 season, NOAA station data):

| Condition | QB Comp% | QB YPA | RB YPC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light rain | −2.3% | −0.32 | +0.19 |
| Moderate rain | −3.4% | −0.33 | +0.04 |
| Snow | −4.1% | −0.07 | +0.089 |
| 30–49°F | −1.8% | −0.17 | +0.07 |
| <30°F | −3.1% | −0.19 | +0.26 |
| Wind 5–10 mph | −0.7% | −0.13 | — |
| Wind 10+ mph | −1.8% | −0.30 | — |

  (Pair-level: catch% −2.3 to −2.4, yards/target −0.40 in light rain.)
- **EPA level:** "As wind speed increases, passing plays become less effective while rushing plays remain roughly constant. As temperature increases, passing becomes more effective, while rushing gets worse" ([Wharton Sports Analytics, Parks et al., 2022 Fall Edition](https://wsb.wharton.upenn.edu/2022-fall-edition-volume1/); [full PDF](https://wsb.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2022_Football_Parks_Weather.pdf); 2014–2021 nflfastR).
- **Modern re-tests:**
  - Fantasy Life (Chris Allen, **published Aug 16, 2024, updated Jun 23, 2025**; 2018–2022): only **48 of 1,311 games (3.7%)** exceeded 20 mph wind; pass rate and total plays show **no visible decline until >20 mph**; from a ≤10 mph baseline to 30 mph, an average **−2.8 percentage points in PROE** per passer; wind *direction* (crosswind) matters more than raw speed ([Does Wind Matter in Fantasy Football?](https://www.fantasylife.com/articles/best-ball/does-wind-matter-in-fantasy-football)).
  - Fantasy Life **2026 edition** (Aug 30, 2026; 11-year sample, nflverse + Open-Meteo/Meteostat): QB **YPA craters 17.9% at ≥25 mph**, but **air yards (intent) fall only 6.9%**; neutral-script **pass rate falls 6.2% only past 25 mph**; heavy/extreme precipitation = ~3% of all games ([The Effects of Weather on Fantasy Football 2026](https://www.fantasylife.com/articles/fantasy/the-effects-of-weather-on-fantasy-football-2026)).
- **The null result (must be reported):** The Fantasy Footballers (Sam DiSorbo, **Mar 8, 2024**) fit ML models of weather → fantasy points by position and found **no meaningful correlation** in raw points ([Does Weather Impact Fantasy Football Performance?](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/analysis/does-weather-impact-fantasy-football-performance/)).
- **Consensus rule supported across the multiplier sources:** do not adjust any player for winds <20–25 mph, temperature >30°F, or light rain; at extreme conditions (≥25 mph sustained crosswind, heavy precipitation, sub-30°F) trim QB/WR *efficiency* (not volume) by the multipliers above and lean on rush volume — but **do not bench a stud** (Fantasy Life 2026: "Start your studs").
- **Explicit findings:** no primary source publishes an exact temperature or wind threshold below which sitting a pass-first QB/WR is *justified* — the 10/20/25-mph and 30/49°F bands above are the only sourced cutoffs. The "wind >20 mph → −18% passing, heavy rain → −14%" figures circulating in 2026 content-farm guides ([Fantasy Hype, undated](https://fantasyhype.com/insights/start-sit-guide-2026)) match no primary dataset retrieved.

### 1.7 Vegas totals as a start/sit input

- **What it measures:** the oddsmakers' combined-score forecast. Sourced predictability: **.32 correlation** with actual combined score (2000–2020) ([TFF, Jul 15, 2021](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/articles/the-fantasy-football-mythbusters-flip-the-game-script/)).
- **Standard technique:** split the total into **implied team totals = (total ± spread) / 2** and use each team's implied total as a per-offense volume/scoring proxy (worked example in [Fantasy Start/Sit Vegas guide, undated](https://fantasystartsit.com/vegas-lines-and-game-totals/) — **flag: content-farm source; the (total±spread)/2 arithmetic is standard and verifiable, but the site's specific bands — implied >27 favorable, <22 caution, total <40 → sit borderline pass-catchers — are unverified editorial**).
- **Game-archetype thresholds in circulation (flag as unverified/DFS-sourced):** shootout = total 48+ & spread ≤3; blowout = 44–50 & spread 7+; grind = total <41 ([DFS Only, May 2026](https://onlydfs.com/blog/nfl-dfs-game-script-vegas.html)); "50+ total ≈ +2.5 PPR per skill player vs. 40 total" ([Fantasy Hype, undated](https://fantasyhype.com/insights/start-sit-guide-2026)). **No primary or academic source publishes a total-points cutoff for switching RB↔WR priority — a genuine gap.**
- **2025 baseline for calibration:** scoring plateaued at ~23 fantasy PPG, pass rate ~60%, fewest plays per team since 2000 ([Fantasy Life 2025 Year in Review, Dec 2025](https://www.fantasylife.com/articles/fantasy/nfl-2025-year-in-review-christian-mccaffrey-proves-rbs-still-matter)); Fantasy Life's own playoff writing treats a **48.5 total as "the highest total of the week"** worth explicitly citing as a tailwind ([Fantasy Life DFS plays, Jan 2026](https://www.fantasylife.com/articles/dfs/nfl-dfs-divisional-round-plays-jaxon-smith-njigba-colston-lovela)).
- **The double-counting objection:** because the total already embeds weather, injuries, and offensive quality, using it *and* a matchup rank *and* a weather adjustment simultaneously is a common over-adjustment error (framed this way on [Fantasy Projection Lab's matchup-adjustments page, undated](https://fantasyprojectionlab.com/matchup-adjustments-in-fantasy-projections/), flagged; the more defensible bound is The Athletic's explicit instruction to use APA for *tough, close* calls only, which caps total/matchup adjustments from stacking — [The Athletic, Dec 24, 2024](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/5744918/2024/12/24/fantasy-football-matchup-rankings-strength-of-schedule/)).

### 1.8 Kickoff timing and Sleeper locks (Thursday / London / Monday)

**League context:** this league is on Sleeper, whose default is **per-game locking** — a player's slot locks **when that player's game begins**; locked slots cannot be changed ([Sleeper Player Auto-Sub announcement, official feed](https://sleeper.com/message/170000000000000000/1129162286456852480/1129162872032104448); [Sleeper "Game Pick" docs, Mar 2024](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/4701537-game-pick-details)). The auto-sub feature swaps in a manager-designated backup if the starter "is inactive at kickoff" (same Sleeper feed; [Player AutoSubs, 2025](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/9731991-how-does-player-autosubs-work)). ESPN documents the identical two-option model for cross-platform reference — "Individually at Scheduled Game Time" (default) vs. "Lock at First Game of the Week" ([ESPN Fan Support, undated](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000088011-Lineup-and-Roster-Lock-Times); [ESPN Fan Support, undated](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003938712-Lineup-Lock-Times)). **Sleeper does not publish a commissioner-selectable lineup-lock option comparable to ESPN's documented settings**; one aggregator claims commissioners can switch to weekly locks on Sleeper but this could not be verified in Sleeper's own docs — treat as unverified and do not assume the league locks at the week's first game ([whatisesports.xyz, undated](https://whatisesports.xyz/roster-lock-timing/)).

**The official information calendar differs by game day — the load-bearing fact.** Under the NFL Personnel (Injury) Report Policy, quoted verbatim in the official 2026-27 league date sheet ([Chargers.com, 2026](https://www.chargers.com/news/national-football-league-important-dates-2026-2027)) and mirrored in the [PFWA NFL calendar (2025-26)](https://www.profootballwriters.org/nfl-calendar/) and the [NFL's 2024-25 important dates (2024)](https://www.nfl.com/news/2024-2025-national-football-league-important-dates):

| Game day | Practice reports due (4 p.m. ET = 1 p.m. PT each day) | Final Game Status Report due |
|---|---|---|
| Sunday (incl. London) | Wed, Thu, Fri | **Friday, 4 p.m. ET (1 p.m. PT)** |
| Monday | Thu, Fri, Sat | Saturday, 4 p.m. ET (1 p.m. PT) |
| Thursday | Mon, Tue, **Wed** | **Wednesday, 4 p.m. ET (1 p.m. PT)** |

The policy also requires: "An update must be reported if there is any change in a player's condition after the initial Game Status Report is filed" ([Chargers, 2026](https://www.chargers.com/news/national-football-league-important-dates-2026-2027)). Inactive lists are submitted **~90 minutes before each kickoff** ([WagerBird explainer, 2026](https://wagerbird.com/learn/nfl-injuries-and-inactives); [NFL.com on the late-replacement rule, 2022](https://www.nfl.com/news/new-rule-to-allow-teams-to-replace-late-covid-19-inactives)).

All times in this section are US Eastern (ET) unless marked; convert them to the user's zone with `current_date`'s UTC offset before comparing with the current time (1:00 PM ET is 12:00 PM CT and 17:00 UTC during daylight time).

**Consequences per game day:**

- **Thursday:** there is **no Thursday, Friday, or Saturday official report at all** for that player — the final report is Wednesday 4 p.m. ET (1 p.m. PT), so the decision must be made by **Wednesday 4:00 PM ET**, after which the only channel is the 90-minute inactive list. Multiple 2026 writeups call TNF "the least informed decision of the week" and the "TNF trap" ([Fantasy Start/Sit primetime guide, 2026](https://fantasystartsit.com/primetime-game-start-sit-impact/); [Fantasy Injury Report Authority, 2026](https://fantasyinjuryreportauthority.com/dnp-limited-full-practice-reports/)). The **game-time decision loophole** compounds this: the NFL has confirmed a team is *not* required to downgrade a Questionable the moment it decides he won't play — the player can be ruled out only on the 90-minute list ([NBC Sports/ProFootballTalk, 2024](https://www.nbcsports.com/nfl/profootballtalk/rumor-mill/news/game-time-decision-loophole-helps-teams-avoid-having-to-downgrade-players-from-questionable); [Yahoo Sports, 2024](https://sports.yahoo.com/game-time-decision-loophole-helps-230439611.html)). A Thursday "Questionable" is a genuine coin-flip-class risk that cannot be hedged after Wednesday. A Thursday player ruled out 90 minutes before Thursday kickoff is already locked — the owner cannot react at all, and the player scores zero ([Fantasy Injury Report Authority, 2026](https://fantasyinjuryreportauthority.com/late-injury-news-fantasy-lineup-decisions/)).
- **Monday:** the latest, most complete information window — practice reports through **Saturday** and the game-status report due **Saturday** ([PFWA, 2025-26](https://www.profootballwriters.org/nfl-calendar/)). Guidance: keep the late-game slot flexible as Sunday-morning injury news lands, and set the lineup early "only if you're confident" ([Waiver Wizard, 2026](https://fantasywaiverwizard.com/learn/start-sit-decisions)).
- **London:** mechanically a regular Sunday game for reporting — same Wed/Thu/Fri cadence ([NFL.com Broncos–Jets London preview, 2025 Week 6](https://www.nfl.com/news/broncos-vs-jets-three-must-know-storylines-london-game-week-6)) — with the logistical note that the team practices in the UK all week ([Denver Post, Oct 10, 2025](https://www.denverpost.com/2025-10-10/broncos-move-practice-england/) documents the Broncos moving their London-game Friday practice to Ware). **No published evidence exists that London reports are systematically more or less conservative than home reports — a gap; no rule should be invented.** The practical London effect is timing: the early-ET kickoff (typically 9:30 AM ET) locks the player *before* most US managers are awake and before last-minute inactive news lands.
- **Worked timing** (computed from the report calendar above): for a **Sunday 1:00 PM ET game**, the Friday final report (4 p.m. ET) arrives ~45 hours before lock — the binding decision window is Sunday 11:30 AM–1:00 PM ET (inactives) ([NFL dates, 2026](https://www.chargers.com/news/national-football-league-important-dates-2026-2027)); the final report precedes kickoff by ~52 hours for a Sunday 8:20 PM ET game and ~28 hours for a Thursday 8:15 PM ET game. TNF writeups quote ~43 and ~19 hours for the same contrast ([Fantasy Start/Sit primetime guide, 2026](https://fantasystartsit.com/primetime-game-start-sit-impact/)); those figures do not match the calendar.
- **Waiver interaction (this league has rolling waivers, 100 FAAB):** "if a player's game begins on Thursday night, they will lock at kickoff and remain on waivers until your selected waiver clear day" ([Sleeper, "Waivers for Regular Season & Playoffs," current](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3978868-waivers-for-regular-season-playoffs)) — no waiver/FAAB relief exists before the lock. A commissioner can enable "Bench Lock," which makes a waiver claim fail if the player being dropped has already played ([Sleeper Support, Nov 2020](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3473234-why-was-someone-able-to-drop-their-starter-after-they-have-played)).
- **Explicit finding — no published threshold:** no source quantifies "how much to discount a TNF Questionable versus a Sunday Questionable" — every source states the information gap qualitatively.

### 1.9 When benching a star is justified

- **Injury-uncertainty (strongest published case).** TheBigLead's Week 1, 2026 start/sit piece [benches Patrick Mahomes](https://www.thebiglead.com/fantasy-football-week-1-start-em-sit-em-bench-patrick-mahomes-ii/) (Aug 2026, 2026 season) — a first-round, elite player — citing a torn ACL/LCL with zero preseason snaps plus a "stingier unit against quarterbacks" defense (matchup ranked via FantasyPros half-PPR points allowed). Worked rule: **return from major knee surgery + top-tier opposing defense = sit even a stud.** The same piece states the general policy: "you should only be sitting someone truly borderline" — sits are justified when the *star's* week is borderline, not when the matchup is merely bad.
- **Usage/role collapse with explicit numeric triggers** ([Fantasy Start/Sit, "Overvaluing Name Recognition," 2026](https://fantasystartsit.com/overvaluing-name-recognition/)): (a) snap percentage below **60% for 3+ consecutive games** → the name carries zero weight; (b) receiver target share below **12% over a 4-game window** → name premium removed; (c) a lesser-known player holding a clear structural matchup edge overrides reputation. The same network's [start/sit decision framework (2026)](https://fantasystartsit.com/start-sit-decision-framework/) orders the factors: **availability first** ("anyone carrying a designation gets resolved first, and if he cannot be resolved before lock, he is not the player you build the lineup around"), then role/volume, then game script, then matchup last.
- **Floor-over-ceiling when you're a favorite** ([Waiver Wizard, 2026](https://fantasywaiverwizard.com/learn/start-sit-decisions)): "If you're a heavy favorite, play the player with a high floor… If you're a significant underdog, play the ceiling." This directly justifies sitting a boom-or-bust star in must-win weeks — material in *this* league because 6 of 10 teams make the playoffs from week 15, so favorite/underdog status is live context in the final stretch. The same source softens the whole category: "the expected value difference between the 10th-ranked and 15th-ranked player at a position in a given week is usually small."
- **Probabilistic framing** ([Fantasy Math, Nathan Braun, 2026](https://nathanbraun.com/fantasymath/)): "the guy who's ranked higher (gets the most points on average) isn't necessarily the guy who maximizes the probability of winning" — EV-maximizing can sit a star when his variance profile is unfavorable in a tight matchup; the mean of the distribution dominates.
- **Designation-based rule** ([MFL Fantasy lineup tips, 2026](https://mflfantasyfootball.com/lineup-tips)): "About 70-80% of Questionable players actually play. If you don't have a clearly better backup option, start them. If you do, sit them." (The higher, data-backed base rates are in §1.2.)
- **Counter-position ("always start your studs"):** [FFTeamNames 2026 decision guide](https://www.ffteamnames.com/beginner-guide/who-to-start-fantasy-football) (2026): "Rule #1: Start Your Studs — If you drafted someone in the first 3 rounds, they play unless they're injured or on bye. Don't overthink it." It lists "Sitting studs for matchups" as a named mistake. [MFL Fantasy](https://mflfantasyfootball.com/lineup-tips) (2026) agrees with quantitative justification: "An elite RB against the best run defense in the MFL still outscores most bench RBs against the worst," and names "Overthinking your locks… The matchup almost never matters enough to bench an elite player" as a common mistake.
- **Synthesis:** experts do *not* disagree about *which categories* justify sitting a star — (i) unresolved/likely-missing availability, (ii) quantified role collapse (snap share / target share), (iii) return from a severe injury with limited snaps. The disagreement is over **matchup-only sits of a healthy stud** (see §2.3), with the caveat that even the "sit" school concedes most matchup sits are net-negative over a season.

### 1.10 FLEX (single FLEX, RB/WR/TE, full PPR — this league's format)

- **The mechanical difference:** a fixed slot (e.g., WR2) is answered by "is this WR better than my best benched WR?" The FLEX is answered by "who among **all remaining RB/WR/TE candidates** has the highest expected points?" — the core mechanism is "projected output versus lineup opportunity cost: a player is worth starting when their expected point total exceeds the expected point total of the **alternative**" ([Fantasy Strategy Guide, 2026](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/start-sit-decisions)). The error is named explicitly: "Your flex has four, and they play different positions, which is why it produces more agonizing than every other slot… the flex is not a fourth-best-player slot, it is a variance dial" ([Waiver Wizard, 2026](https://fantasywaiverwizard.com/learn/start-sit-decisions)).
- **"Best RB in the league" is the wrong frame** because the FLEX candidate competes against WR3/TE2 *on your roster*, not against the positional leader: "Most people default to 'start my best RB or WR not already in.' That's fine but lazy. Better: identify your 3 candidates (usually RB3, WR3, maybe TE2), compare matchups, compare projected workload" ([MFL Fantasy, 2026](https://mflfantasyfootball.com/lineup-tips)).
- **The correct ordering for a single FLEX is two-stage:** (1) fill the dedicated slots first — QB1, the two RBs, the two WRs, the TE — then (2) rank *all* remaining flex-eligible players by projected points adjusted for floor/matchup/usage ([Fantasy Start/Sit flex guide, 2026](https://fantasystartsit.com/flex-spot-start-sit/)); the candidate pool changes weekly, and PPR tilts the flex toward receivers.
- **Format matters more than the slot** ([4for4, Mike Barner, Aug 12, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/how-win-flex-position-your-fantasy-football-league); 2024–2025 data) — the clearest published quantification:
  - **Standard scoring:** 24 RBs averaged ≥10.0 fantasy points in 2024; only 10 WRs did.
  - **Half PPR:** 11 RBs vs. 6 WRs at ≥14.0 in 2025.
  - **Full PPR (this league's format):** **51 WRs vs. 42 RBs** averaged ≥8.0 PPR points in 2025, and **62 vs. 41** in 2024. "Once full-PPR scoring comes into play, WR3s and WR4s have the potential for significantly more upside than RB3s and RB4s."
  - **Matchup override:** "If your third running back has a significantly more favorable matchup than your third or fourth wide receiver, then start them in your flex spot, even if it is half-PPR or full-PPR scoring."
- **TE in the flex** ([Fantasy Start/Sit, 2026](https://fantasystartsit.com/flex-position-start-sit-decisions/)): a TE flexed outside the dedicated TE slot is "almost always a volume play — a player with a 20%+ target share who functions as a de facto WR2"; starting a TE in the flex "purely for touchdown upside, without a strong usage base, is a lower-probability decision." (Full PPR softens the TE penalty, but **no published PPR-specific numeric TE-flex threshold exists — flag the gap**.)
- **Tie-breakers and published numeric edges:**
  - **Projection-gap rule:** when two flex candidates are within **~3 points**, stop comparing means and compare floors — "a player with a 10-point floor and 16-point ceiling is the correct flex play in a week where a win requires beating a high-scoring opponent; a 7-point floor / 22-point ceiling is the choice when you need variance" ([Fantasy Start/Sit, 2026](https://fantasystartsit.com/flex-position-start-sit-decisions/)); same boundary in [Fantasy Strategy Guide, 2026](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/start-sit-decisions): "projection differential less than 3 points? lean toward the higher floor; greater than 3 points with a usage-driven explanation? follow the usage."
  - **Usage as tiebreaker:** a receiver with a **28% target share** on a pass-heavy offense beats one who scored big last week on 12% ([Fantasy Start/Sit, 2026](https://fantasystartsit.com/flex-spot-start-sit/)); full-time players score roughly **double** part-timers at the same position, and a target-starved receiver "almost never delivers a startable week however soft the coverage" ([Waiver Wizard, 2026](https://fantasywaiverwizard.com/learn/start-sit-decisions)).
  - **PPR-specific safe plays:** pass-catching RBs and slot WRs with guaranteed catch floors over boom-or-bust deep threats ([MFL Fantasy, 2026](https://mflfantasyfootball.com/lineup-tips)).
  - **Worked single-flex example:** RB3 with a 15-carry workload vs. a slot receiver with an 8-catch projection — in PPR the receiver's floor matches or exceeds the RB's; the decision then goes to matchup and Vegas total ([Fantasy Start/Sit, 2026](https://fantasystartsit.com/flex-spot-start-sit/)).
  - **Decision cost:** a *missed* flex decision typically costs only a few points, while a *missed* availability decision costs the player's entire floor — treat flex calls as low-stakes relative to availability calls ([Waiver Wizard, 2026](https://fantasywaiverwizard.com/learn/start-sit-decisions)).
  - Slot definition for reference: [Yahoo/Athlon beginner flex guide, 2025-26](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/beginner-guide-fantasy-football-flex-225631343.html).
- **Single-FLEX structural notes:** there is no published literature distinguishing single-FLEX from double-FLEX decision rules; all the frameworks above describe the standard 2-RB/2-WR/1-TE + 1-flex shape, which is exactly this league's shape plus K/DEF. With only **5 bench spots**, the "alternative" in the opportunity-cost comparison is frequently the *worst* bench player or a zero, which mechanically makes the flex's floor the binding constraint in close weeks (the 6-of-10 playoff field makes "close weeks" the norm for 60% of teams).

## 2. Where experts disagree

**2.1 Is matchup adjustment real and quantifiable?**

- **Position A — real and quantifiable.** Regression magnitudes of −0.07 to −0.13 points per defensive rank spot (QB/RB/WR), ~4.2 fantasy PPG best-vs-worst run defense for RBs, with TE at zero; weekly APA rankings, DvP "Fantasy Boost" tables, and WR/CB Shadow Reports are all actively maintained by major outlets ([The Fantasy Footballers, Aug 3, 2021](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/articles/the-fantasy-football-mythbusters-making-the-most-of-matchups/); [The Athletic, Dec 24, 2024](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/5744918/2024/12/24/fantasy-football-matchup-rankings-strength-of-schedule/); [Fantasy Life DvP tool, undated](https://www.fantasylife.com/tools/fantasy-football-rb-dvp); [ESPN Shadow Report Week 2 2026, Sep 17, 2026](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/49948423/fantasy-playbook-projections-lineup-locks-2026-week-2-shadow-reports)).
- **Position B — small, positional, and often over-applied.** The *same* TFF study is the counter-evidence: TE coefficient statistically zero, WR defense effect weak, max effect ~4 PPG across an entire backfield, and **offense rank predicts ~3× better than defense rank** ([TFF, Aug 3, 2021](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/articles/the-fantasy-football-mythbusters-making-the-most-of-matchups/)). A Medium article ("Why WR/CB Matchups Don't Matter for Fantasy Football") argues for discarding ~99% of coverage-matchup advice — **unverified: the page is behind an anti-bot wall; treat as a pointer to the contrarian camp, not a citation.** Even proponents restrict the effect to *borderline* calls: The Athletic's published caveat says APA rankings "do[] not" mean "you bench a stud with a tough APA for a flier with a good matchup" ([The Athletic, Dec 24, 2024](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/5744918/2024/12/24/fantasy-football-matchup-rankings-strength-of-schedule/)).
- **Net:** magnitude is real but bounded (0 to ~4 fantasy PPG, best vs. worst defense, by position); no side-pick is defensible from the sources, and even Position A concedes the "never bench a stud" limit.

**2.2 The Friday-Questionable star: sit now or wait until the lock?**

- **Position A — start him (EV maximizers):** at a ~70% historical final-report play rate ([Footballguys, 09/13/2025](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2025-chance-to-play-questionable-vs-doubtful)), a star's probability-weighted points usually exceed a safe benchmate's floor; head-to-head weekly formats reward expected value, and the regret of sitting a 70%-likely starter who plays is worse than the 0-point floor risk (the tradeoff is framed explicitly in [Fantasy Injury Report Authority, 2026 — flagged secondary](https://fantasyinjuryreportauthority.com/late-injury-news-fantasy-lineup-decisions/)).
- **Position B — sit / prepare the sub (floor-firsters):** the designation is team-specific (2024 Questionable play rates: Tampa Bay 94.83% vs. Pittsburgh 22.22% — [Banged Up Bills, 07/12/2025](https://bangedupbills.com/2025/07/12/how-do-the-buffalo-bills-compare-against-the-nfl-utilizing-the-questionable-designation/)); the *variance* of reduced snap share makes the floor worse than the model suggests even when "an 80% version would have been better than none"; use Sleeper auto-sub so the downside auto-covers without a last-minute panic ([Sleeper official feed](https://sleeper.com/message/170000000000000000/1129162286456852480/1129162872032104448)). Footballguys' own 2025 piece notes the 70.7% "does not necessarily reflect" the official definition because "some NFL teams do not use the Questionable tag correctly" — even the most-cited number is league-average, not player-specific.
- **Position C — intermediate (in the guides, unvalidated by data):** start the Q player only if Friday practice was FP or the trend is improving (DNP→LP→FP), otherwise sit ([Fantasy Injury Report Authority, 2026 — flagged secondary](https://fantasyinjuryreportauthority.com/dnp-limited-full-practice-reports/)). No published dataset validates the pattern-level rule (see §1.3 gap).
- **Complicating fact for this league:** under Sleeper per-game locking, a Sunday player's slot locks at kickoff, after the 90-minute inactive list — so "waiting until the lock" is only available as information, not as an action window; the actionable window ends at inactives ([Sleeper official feed](https://sleeper.com/message/170000000000000000/1129162286456852480/1129162872032104448); [WagerBird, 2026](https://wagerbird.com/learn/nfl-injuries-and-inactives)).

**2.3 "Always start your stars" vs. the floor-based / sit-your-stud school**

- **Position A — always start studs.** "Rule #1: Start Your Studs — If you drafted someone in the first 3 rounds, they play unless they're injured or on bye. Don't overthink it"; "Sitting studs for matchups" is a named mistake ([FFTeamNames, 2026](https://www.ffteamnames.com/beginner-guide/who-to-start-fantasy-football)). "An elite RB against the best run defense in the MFL still outscores most bench RBs against the worst"; "The matchup almost never matters enough to bench an elite player" ([MFL Fantasy, 2026](https://mflfantasyfootball.com/lineup-tips)).
- **Position B — sit when the star's own week is borderline or the format rewards floor.** Worked example: benching first-round Mahomes in Week 1 2026 on a post-ACL/LCL return plus an elite QB defense, with the stated policy "you should only be sitting someone truly borderline" ([TheBigLead, Aug 2026](https://www.thebiglead.com/fantasy-football-week-1-start-em-sit-em-bench-patrick-mahomes-ii/)). Heavy favorite → play the high floor, significant underdog → play the ceiling ([Waiver Wizard, 2026](https://fantasywaiverwizard.com/learn/start-sit-decisions)). Quantified role-collapse triggers remove the name premium entirely: snap % <60% for 3+ games, or target share <12% over 4 games ([Fantasy Start/Sit, 2026](https://fantasystartsit.com/overvaluing-name-recognition/)). Probabilistic grounding: the higher-ranked (mean) player "isn't necessarily the guy who maximizes the probability of winning" ([Fantasy Math, 2026](https://nathanbraun.com/fantasymath/)).
- **Shared concession:** even the floor-based school says start/sit swaps "are often less consequential than managers believe" — the EV gap between the 10th- and 15th-ranked player at a position in a week is usually small ([Waiver Wizard, 2026](https://fantasywaiverwizard.com/learn/start-sit-decisions)) — and the "sit" school concedes most *matchup-only* sits are net-negative over a season.

**2.4 Game script: the classic favorite-RB/underdog-QB rule vs. "points dominate"**

- **Position A — play the classic script.** RB workhorse data: Derrick Henry −12.8 fantasy PPG in losses vs. wins with ~7 more carries in wins; pass-catching RBs get *better* when trailing (Kamara +7.9, McKissic +6.3) ([SI via WKKY, Aug 10, 2021](https://wkky.com/2021-08-10/game-script-impact-on-fantasy-football-production-running-backs/); 2020 data); teams passed 66% of plays trailing vs. 51% leading ([PFF, 2017](https://www.pff.com/news/fantasy-football-metrics-that-matter-fantasy-options-most-dependent-on-gamescript)). 2026 DFS content still teaches the favorite-RB/underdog-QB stack ([DFS Only, May 2026](https://onlydfs.com/blog/nfl-dfs-game-script-vegas.html)).
- **Position B — team score projection beats script narrative.** As the spread widens, favorite-side players outscore underdog-side counterparts *at every position* (favorite WRs gain ~42 yards vs. 7+ underdogs); the underdog garbage-time *share* gain loses to the smaller *pie*; verdict: pick the higher-scoring team's players ([TFF, Jul 15, 2021](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/articles/the-fantasy-football-mythbusters-flip-the-game-script/)).
- **Status:** the 2021 multi-position dataset favors "favorite-side volume" while the single-workhorse RB data favors the classic rule; no newer multi-position study was found, so both remain active positions in 2026 content.
