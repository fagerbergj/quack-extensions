*Compiled 2026-09-18 for the author of an AI fantasy-football assistant serving a 10-team redraft, full-PPR Sleeper league (rolling waivers, 100 FAAB, 6 playoff teams, QB/RB/RB/WR/WR/TE/FLEX/K/DEF + 5 bench). ## 1. Decision rules and numeric thresholds

### 1.1 Preview and recap structure: the consensus skeleton

The dominant published format (ESPN, FantasyPros, The Athletic, RotoWire, Sharp Football Analysis) is **game-by-game, one uniform block per matchup**, in roughly this order:

1. Headline/lede carrying the single most important story or stat of the week
2. Standings/records snapshot
3. Head-to-head matchup blocks — the load-bearing unit
4. Projected scores / win probabilities / O-U
5. Start/sit or "lineup lock" calls, one per matchup
6. Injury watch + waiver/FAAB section (standing separate columns)
7. "What's next" — one upcoming matchup to watch

Evidence for each element:

- **ESPN "The Playbook"** (Mike Clay, Sep 17, 2026, [full text](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/49948423/fantasy-playbook-projections-lineups-locks-2026-week-2-shadow-reports)) is the cleanest template: for every Sunday game (16 games in the 2026 Week 2 edition) it repeats a fixed block — **projected score → "Lineup locks" name list → "Fantasy scoop" paragraph anchored to usage numbers (snap %, route share, target share — e.g., "Kincaid played on 77% of offensive snaps… ran a route on 71% of the team's passing plays") → O/U + win probability** (e.g., "56.2 (highest)… Bills 56% (14th highest)"). The column states its target format up front ("centered on 12-team PPR leagues with relatively standard scoring and lineup settings") and calls itself "easily digestible fantasy advice."
- **The Athletic** weekly "cheat sheet" (Sep 4, 2025, [snippet-only — direct fetch 403](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/6483817/2025/09/04/fantasy-football-cheat-sheet-nfl-week-1-injuries/)) is organized into five named sections — **Injury news, Weather report, Featured games, Waiver wire, Drops** — with a live-update loop on Sunday inactives. The Athletic's [customizable cheat-sheet generator](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/7332863/2026/08/21/fantasy-football-2026-customizable-cheat-sheet-rankings-projections/) (Aug 21, 2026, snippet-only) lets users "input your league rules and settings" to get rankings "unique to your league" — direct evidence that league-format specificity is a first-class structural requirement.
- **FantasyPros** runs two complementary weekly products: **The Primer** (Derek Brown, [Sep 25, 2025 edition](https://www.fantasypros.com/2025/09/the-primer-week-4-edition-2025-fantasy-football/), ~15 games per week; [Sep 17, 2026 edition](https://www.fantasypros.com/2026/09/the-primer-week-2-edition-2026-fantasy-football/); both snippet-only) and **Quick Grades** ([Sep 21, 2025](https://www.fantasypros.com/2025/09/fantasy-football-week-3-rankings-grades-start-sit-advice-2025/), snippet-only — "consensus rankings and projections combined with The Primer's matchup analysis to assign letter grades for every fantasy-relevant player, then added concise notes").
- **RotoWire** uses a numbered-takeaways format — "10 Things Fantasy Managers Need to Know" ([Week 2, 2026](https://www.rotowire.com/football/article/week-2-fantasy-football-10-things-fantasy-managers-need-to-know-134866), snippet-only) — plus a **daily-updated waiver pickups column** that explicitly covers "both shallow and deep leagues each week" ([Week 1 edition](https://www.rotowire.com/football/article/fantasy-football-waiver-wire-pickups-for-week-1-132805); [Week 2 edition](https://www.rotowire.com/football/article/fantasy-football-waiver-wire-pickups-for-week-2-95907); both snippet-only; "I'll update this article each day reacting to any pesky injuries").
- **Rich Hribar's Worksheet** (Sharp Football Analysis, Sep 17, 2026, [full text](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/fantasy/nfl-week-1-preview-fantasy-football-worksheet-rich-hribar-2025/), 13th season of the series) is the depth pole: every NFL game previewed with league-wide trend stats (2.18 points per drive; turnover rate 11.9% vs 8.7% prior; 13-personnel share 8.1%; 12 players over 30 PPR points in Week 1), per-game tables, paired weekly player rankings, updated all week for injuries.

**Structure rules:**
- **Rule S1 — One uniform block per matchup.** The matchup block is the unit of both previews and recaps; keep the internal field order identical week to week (observed across all five outlets above; see the dated links in this subsection).
- **Rule S2 — Declare league-format assumptions up front.** ESPN states its "12-team PPR… standard scoring" premise in the lede; the equivalent for this assistant is to state "10 teams, full PPR, rolling waivers, 100 FAAB, 6 playoff spots" whenever the format affects a recommendation (ESPN Playbook, Sep 17, 2026; The Athletic generator, Aug 21, 2026).
- **Rule S3 — Stand waiver/FAAB advice as a recurring, separately-dated column, updated for injuries**, and split it by league depth (RotoWire, 2026 Week 1–2, both retrieved via search, 2026-09-18).

### 1.2 Length norms

No fantasy outlet publishes word-count targets — that's a **gap** (the figures below are general sports-recap guidance and a league-chat guide, respectively):

- **League-chat recaps: a few hundred words.** ffwrapped's weekly recap guide (Kevin Tian, undated; retrieved 2026-09-18, [full text](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/fantasy-football-weekly-recap)): "For a league chat, I'd aim for **a few hundred words**. Include each matchup score, then spend most of the space on the two or three things people will care about. An email newsletter can go longer." The filled-in worked example (6-team league) is **~250 words across 7 sections: THE STORY → FINAL SCORES → A FEW AWARDS → A LITTLE SCHEDULE LUCK → WHERE THAT LEAVES US → NEXT WEEK → one league-voice line.**
- **Professional recaps: 800–1,000 words**, filed in 30–45 minutes; wire services file 700–900 (regular season) to 1,000–1,400 (playoffs); a published 8-paragraph template totals **650–810 words** (River, "Sports Game Recap Template: 1,000 Words in 30 Minutes," 2026, [full text](https://rivereditor.com/blogs/write-sports-game-recap-1000-words)).

**Length/structure rules:**
- **Rule L1** — League-chat recap ≈ **200–500 words**, every matchup score included, depth concentrated in 2–3 stories (ffwrapped, undated, retrieved 2026-09-18).
- **Rule L2** — Long-form preview: one uniform ~150–250-word block per game across the slate (observed, ESPN Playbook, Sep 17, 2026), or 800–1,000 words for a single-game deep story (River, 2026).
- **Rule L3** — **Story first, scores as a table**, never a prose sequence: "Always by importance (inverted pyramid)… Chronological recaps bury the story" (River, 2026); ffwrapped leads its template with THE STORY (undated, retrieved 2026-09-18).
- **Rule L4** — The lede must carry **score + hero + significance in 40–60 words**, because "73% of readers only read the first paragraph" (River, 2026).
- **Rule L5** — Include a **"what this means for standings"** element early — element #2 of the River (2026) template and "WHERE THAT LEAVES US" in the ffwrapped (undated) league format.

### 1.3 What readers value (rules for content, not just shape)

- **Rule C1 — Omissions beat filler; three sharp observations beat six filled-in sections.** ffwrapped (undated, retrieved 2026-09-18): "I'd much rather read **three good observations than six sections filled out just because the template had them**"; the detail readers want is "the detail you wouldn't get from looking at the standings. Someone scored the second-most points and lost."
- **Rule C2 — Forward calls rest on usage, not last week's points.** ESPN's scoops consistently lead with snap/route/target shares rather than fantasy points (Kincaid 77% of snaps; "clear No. 2 target" call on Coker — Sep 17, 2026). Footballguys' weekly overview (Jeff Haseley, Sep 15, 2026, [full text](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2026-fantasy-overview-week02)) codifies a **10-factor verification checklist** before believing any breakout: snap counts, depth chart, coach comments, injury-opened doors, matchup quality, **garbage-time share**, one-or-two-big-play dependence, red-zone targets, **target share %**, TD-dependence.
- **Rule C3 — Numbers in service of one stated story, not a stat dump.** Sports Media Field Guide (Nov 4, 2014, [full text](https://sportsfieldguide.org/2014/11/04/breaking-down-game-elements-to-create-a-compelling-recap/); originally 2011): a recap needs "storyline, context (the nut graph: who, where, when, score), quotes from BOTH sides, key plays, and key statistics — **particularly those that help propel the main storyline**"; "Readers' eyes will glaze if you offer too many statistics, especially if these numbers are packed together. Look for stats that might not emerge in a box score."
- **Rule C4 — Claims must be concrete and checkable.** Fantasy Footballers "MythBusters" (Samuel DiSorbo, Aug 31, 2026, [full text](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/analysis/the-fantasy-mythbusters-is-week-1-a-performance-indicator-for-the-rest-of-the-season-fantasy-football/)): 2,424 player-seasons 2018–2025; Week 1 explains ~40% of rest-of-season variance (r = 0.64); "trust the direction, discount the magnitude" (25+ point openers average 16.1 PPG after; sub-5 openers 5.4). Hribar quantifies the Week 1 scoring spike *and* immediately hedges the sample: "Do not run wild with anything we just experienced in Week 1" (Sep 17, 2026).
- **Rule C5 — League voice and arguability.** ffwrapped (undated, retrieved 2026-09-18): "A joke lands better when the score underneath is right"; "Make it sound like your league… Mention the rivalry when it matters." The Gaming Juice on AI fantasy content (Brad Evans, bylined May 16, 2026, [full text](https://www.thegamingjuice.com/2025/06/12/artificial-intelligence-content-fantasy-football)): the fatal failure mode is bloodless content where "tossing haymakers at a soulless bot doesn't hit the same."

### 1.4 Playoff odds playbook — the no-simulation (closed-form) method

Inputs per team: record W–L after week *t*, points for (PF), points against (PA), remaining schedule.

**Step 1 — Pythagorean expectation.** Win% = PF^x / (PF^x + PA^x); multiply by games played for expected wins.
- **The NFL exponent is 2.37** — Daryl Morey's fit at STATS Inc., used by Football Outsiders from 2003 and in Bill Barnwell's published work: ["The luckiest and unluckiest NFL teams of 2016 (so far)"](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/17998672/raiders-bills-opposite-ends-luck-spectrum-far-2016-nfl-season) (Nov 8, 2016) and ["NFL teams most likely to improve — and decline — in 2017"](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/20199657/nfl-teams-likely-improve-decline-2017-season-bill-barnwell-pythagorean-expectation) (Aug 1, 2017). Worked examples: Grantland, "The NFL's Cloudy Crystal Ball" (2012, [full text](https://grantland.com/features/breaking-best-nfl-stats/)): 212^2.37/(212^2.37 + 338^2.37) = 0.248 → 4.0 expected wins for a 7-9 team; NFL Analytics explainer (Jun 16, 2026, [full text](https://nflanalytic.com/explainer-pythagorean-wins.html)): PF 400 / PA 340 → ~0.59 Pythagorean win% → ~10 expected wins in 17 games. Peer-adjacent confirmation that 2.37 is the optimal NFL exponent: Frontiers in Sports and Active Living (Sep 12, 2025, [full text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2025.1638446/full); NBA's is 13.91). A student-journal empirical test of the formula in the NFL: Brady & Sommers, "Does Bill James's Pythagorean Formula Apply to Football?", *Journal of Student Research* 10(4) (2021, [DOI record](https://doi.org/10.47611/jsr.v10i4.1372) — citation record retrieved, no finding attributed). The formula's baseball origin (x = 2) is documented in Baseball Prospectus (2011, [full text](https://www.baseballprospectus.com/news/article/342/revisiting-the-pythagorean-theorem-putting-bill-james-pythagorean-theorem-to-the-test/)); related predictive work: SABR journal article, [full text](https://sabr.org/journal/article/a-new-formula-to-predict-a-teams-winning-percentage/) (undated).
- **The fantasy exponent — a gap with one named anchor.** No published calibration of x for fantasy scoring exists beyond **Daniel Ludwinski, "Fantasy Playoff Probability" (Nov 16, 2017, [full text](https://dludwinski.com/blog/2017/11/16/fantasy-playoff-probability/))**, who used a **Pythagorean exponent of 6**, "similar to what Yahoo uses in their projections." Directional logic: fantasy weekly totals (80–150+ points) are far larger than NFL's (~50), so x must exceed 2.37. Defensible operating rule: **fit x on the league's own completed season; until then use ≈ 4–6** and cite Ludwinski's 6 as the only dated, named-expert anchor.

**Step 2 — Regression to .500 (published fractions).**
- NFL year-over-year: regressing Year N wins on Year N-1 (2003–2014) gives `Wins = 5.51 + 0.31 × prior wins` (R² = 0.094) — a team earns credit for **~31% of its prior wins above the mean** (Chase Stuart, Football Perspective, Mar 25, 2015, [full text](https://www.footballperspective.com/regression-to-the-mean-and-team-wins/)).
- Real-wins regression slope **0.3265** (~1/3 credit) vs Pythagorean-wins slope **0.3995** (40%) — Pythagorean needs less regression because luck is already removed (Stuart, "Pythagenpat Records in 2013," Feb 11, 2014, [full text](https://www.footballperspective.com/pythagenpat-records-in-2013/)).
- Baseball midseason form: at 46 of 162 games, "you can figure out the rest-of-season win% by using anything from **10 to 12 games** as the regression amount" (Tom Tango, The Book, ~2005, [full text](https://insidethebook.com/ee/index.php/site/comments/forecasting_the_end_of_season_records_after_46_games)); the Bayesian restatement: **.060 SD of talent ≈ 69 games added** (Tango, May 2006, [full text](https://insidethebook.com/ee/index.php/site/comments/bayes_and_regression_again)).
- **Gap:** no published NFL *midseason* or fantasy calibration of regression to .500 exists. Operating rule: use the **≈1/3 of wins above average** NFL year-over-year anchor, scaled to a 14-game season, rising toward 1 as games accumulate — and flag it in output as an assumption.
- The implied shrinkage language: with true-ability r = 0.75, "your best guess of a team's true strength should regress its actual record **one quarter of the way back to the .500 mean**" (Advanced Football Analytics, Nov 9, 2010, [full text](http://www.advancedfootballanalytics.com/2010/11/randomness-of-win-loss-records.html) — 42% of an NFL season record is sample error, r² = 0.58).

**Step 3 — Projected final record, with schedule folded in.**
- Per remaining game vs opponent *j*, estimate p from the point-margin of your PPG vs theirs: Pythagorean form (each side's projected points for/against) or the no-Pythagorean alternative, **logistic on margin: p = 1/(1 + 10^(−m/17.8))** — the inverse-normal-CDF form NFL game margin uses with σ ≈ 13.5–14 points (Datafield sports-betting textbook, ch. 7, 2024–2025, [full text](https://datafield.dev/sports-betting-textbook/part-02/chapter-07/); FiveThirtyEight's NFL Elo used the same normal-inverse structure for football win probabilities, 2012, [archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20171004105151/https:/fivethirtyeight.com/features/introducing-nfl-elo-ratings/)). For fantasy, scale the 17.8 divisor up to the league's observed weekly-score SD.
- **Schedule strength comes in game-by-game, automatically** — every opponent's current scoring appears in their PA — which is why the method folds it in per game rather than as a separate multiplier (synthesis of the power-rating structures used inside published simulators: The Athletic's EPR, "a prediction of how many points a team would score above an average team on a neutral field," glossary of Oct 29, 2020, [full text](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/1997663/2020/10/29/nfl-analytics-glossary-dvoa-epa-qbr/); Washington Post's "power rating… based on point differential and strength of schedule," [2024–25 interactive](https://www.washingtonpost.com/sports/interactive/2024/nfl-playoff-odds-standings-predictions-2024-25/)).
- **E(W) = W_so_far + Σ p_i over remaining games.** A simple defensible rest-of-season W% hybrid: `0.5 × (regressed current W%) + 0.5 × (Pythagorean W%)`, with Pythagorean presented as primary — it predicts better (correlation with next-year wins 0.36 vs 0.32 for real wins; in a multiple regression actual wins were not significant once Pythagorean wins were included — Stuart, Feb 11, 2014).

**Step 4 — The "playoff line."** Compute E(W) for all 10 teams; **the 6th-largest projected win total is the playoff line**; teams above it are "projected in." This mirrors how real-league tools count finishes: ffwrapped's 5,000-iteration calculator (undated; retrieved 2026-09-18, [full text](https://ffwrapped.com/fantasy-football-playoff-odds-calculator)), My Fantasy Analyzer's 10,000-iteration Sleeper tool (undated; retrieved 2026-09-18, [full text](https://myfantasyanalyzer.com/playoff-odds/)), and [PlayoffComputer](https://playoffcomputer.appspot.com/?code=ctpeb63uc579kr67v9vthdhdwrem99es) (undated) for imported leagues. Report the **gap to the line** ("0.4 wins clear"), not just in/out.

**Step 5 — Probability without a simulator.** Treat remaining games as independent Bernoulli with the p_i above:
- `σ = sqrt(Σ p_i(1 − p_i))` — for a 4–5 game finish, σ ≈ 1.1–1.4 (the 17-game NFL analog is σ ≈ 1.8–2.0; NFL Analytics, 2026, [full text](https://nflanalytic.com/explainer-2026-win-totals.html)).
- `P(make playoffs) ≈ Φ((line_6 − E(W) + 0.5) / σ)` (standard normal CDF, continuity correction). The binomial/normal approximation on win counts is standard with the caveat that it ignores schedule heterogeneity (Datafield ch. 7, 2024–2025).
- **Sanity anchor:** Ludwinski (Nov 16, 2017), on a comparable 10-team structure, produced **59.5% / 59.0%** for two 5-5 teams fighting over two spots — coin-flip odds at the cutoff; the assistant's output should reproduce this.
- **Cross-check base rates** (closest published analog is 12-team/6-playoff; **a 10-team/6-playoff table does not exist — gap**): 3-0 → 84.8%, 2-1 → 63.8%, 1-2 → 36.2%, 0-3 → 15.5%, 0-4 → 8.7%; 7-5 is the "magic ticket" (~80%+) (Guru Fantasy World, Sep 23, 2023, [full text](https://gurufantasyworld.com/understanding-playoff-probabilities/), republishing Laura Pressman / Automated Insights). The "Expected extra wins = remaining games × expected win probability" pattern is also visible in a low-authority SEO calculator (CodingAce, undated; pattern-verified only, [link](https://codingace.net/general/fantasy_football_playoff_scenario.html)) and a worked static logit model (Logit P = 0.660 + 0.304·SOS + 0.747·Pythagorean PX; Code and Football, Jan 9, 2025, [full text](https://codeandfootball.wordpress.com/2025/01/09/2024-playoff-season-worksheet-first-round-odds-formula-plus-srs/)).
- **Worked mini-example (illustrative, from the feeder):** Week 9 of 14, 6 in. Team A: 6-3, PF 990 / PA 910 (110.0 / 101.1 PPG); Team B: 5-4, PF 930 / PA 890 (103.3 / 98.9 PPG). Team A Pythagorean (x = 4, fantasy-scale) ≈ 0.587 vs .667 record → regress: .500 + (1/3)(.167) + 0.5(0.587 − .500) ≈ .605. Team B Pyth ≈ .618, blended ≈ .601. Project the remaining 5 games per opponent PPG: A → E(W) = 11.3 (σ ≈ 1.2), B → 11.0. If the 6th-best projection league-wide is 10.9, A's P ≈ Φ(0.33) ≈ 63%, B's ≈ 54%.

### 1.5 The lightweight Monte Carlo approach (the standard "lightweight simulation")

- **Every published "playoff odds" product simulates** — this is the industry default, not a niche. NFL side: The Athletic simulated the remainder **1,000,000 times** with EPRs plus home-field and rest adjustments (Dec 29, 2020, [full text](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/2166725/2020/12/29/nfl-playoffs-predictions-odds/)); the 2025 model ran **100,000** (Feb 9, 2025, [full text](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/5698572/2025/02/09/nfl-playoffs-projections-odds/)); the Distributed Power Rating model post describes 100,000 sims with deliberately added uncertainty because early ratings "are not likely to hold up by week 18" (Sep 8, 2021, [mirror](https://aglitz.s3.uk.perf.cloud.ovh.net/distributed-power-rating-how-i-built-the-athletic-s-nfl-predictions-model-using-team-strengths-an.html)). The Athletic's site 403s on direct fetch — treat iteration counts as The-Athletic-published, via indexed page text and the 2021 mirror. Washington Post runs **25,000 simulations per update** ([2024–25 interactive](https://www.washingtonpost.com/sports/interactive/2024/nfl-playoff-odds-standings-predictions-2024-25/); [2025–26 interactive](https://www.washingtonpost.com/sports/interactive/2025/nfl-playoff-odds-standings-predictions-2025-26/)). ESPN's MLB odds use 10,000 Monte Carlo simulations (2024–25 season, [full text](https://www.espn.com/mlb/story/_/id/40571201/mlb-2024-second-half-preview-rankings-playoff-odds-all-30-teams)). Baseball Prospectus' canonical pre-simulator-era explanation of the machinery — Pythagorean-style expected winning percentage, regressed to the mean, paired via log5 — (Tommy Bennett, "Expanded Horizons: Understanding Post-season Odds," circa 2006, page undated; [full text](https://www.baseballprospectus.com/news/article/11881/expanded-horizons-understanding-post-season-odds/)).
- **Real fantasy leagues use the same pattern at far smaller counts:** My Fantasy Analyzer **10,000** sims of a Sleeper league's actual rosters/schedule (undated; retrieved 2026-09-18); ffwrapped **5,000** with "weekly scoring variation" (undated; retrieved 2026-09-18; [methodology post](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/fantasy-football-playoff-odds)); Smash Accept "thousands" (undated, [full text](https://www.smashacceptff.com/fantasy-season-simulator)); Clinch 30,000 (NFL, [full text](https://clinchsports.app/how-it-works)). A pedagogical spreadsheet version draws each team's weekly score as a normal with mean = season-average PPG and σ = the std of recent weekly scores (19.6 in the example; SpreadsheetSolving, 2017–2021, [full text](https://spreadsheetsolving.com/fantasy_football_monte_carlo/)). Ludwinski (Nov 16, 2017) brute-forced all **262,144** win/loss combinations of three weeks for a 10-team league rather than approximating.
- **Why simulation beats closed-form:** the 6th-place cutoff is a *rank* of 10 *correlated* win counts (teams' outcomes are linked through their mutual games); the normal approximation ignores that correlation.
- **Rules:**
  - **Rule M1** — A 10-team league makes ~**1,000–5,000 sims** of the remaining weeks (per-game p_i from §1.4) trivially cheap (~15 remaining matchups × 5,000 passes) — the published fantasy norm (ffwrapped 5,000; MFA 10,000; both undated, retrieved 2026-09-18).
  - **Rule M2** — **Any "playoff odds" figure must be traceable to one of three methods**: Pythagorean projection, a base-rate table, or a named simulation — never a vibes number (synthesis of ffwrapped's odds post, undated, retrieved 2026-09-18; Guru Fantasy World, Sep 23, 2023; Gretch, Aug 9, 2023, below).
  - **Rule M3** — **State the method in one line whenever a percentage is published**, and never treat a high (or displayed 0%/100%) probability as a confirmed playoff berth — "Even a displayed 0% or 100% [playoff probability] is a simulation estimate" (ffwrapped, undated, retrieved 2026-09-18).

### 1.6 Luck indicators and when the sample is too small to use them

**Pythagorean overshoot/undershoot (expressed in wins, always with the number):**
- Unit of measure is wins: "Pythagorean expectation: 5.9 wins" vs the actual record; Barnwell's gap example — "a gap of 3.3 wins… fourth-largest since 1989" (Aug 1, 2017). The translation to record language: "playing like a 6-10 team" (Barnwell, Nov 8, 2016).
- **Regression thresholds (population):** the 10 largest overperformance gaps 1989–2015 → **7 of 10 declined, none improved, average drop 3.4 wins**; teams 2–3 wins above Pythagorean expectation declined by an average of **3 wins** the following season (Barnwell, Aug 1, 2017). Counterpoint: betting on early-season Pythagorean over/underachievers (defined as a ≥25-point gap in Pythagorean win%) hit only **~52% since 1999** — the regression is a population tendency, not an individual guarantee (Andrew Healy, "Betting Bad: A Look at Early-Season Pythagorean Outliers," Sep 2014, [full text](https://www.footballperspective.com/betting-bad-a-look-at-early-season-pythagorean-outliers/)).
- Close-game losses → improvement (Barnwell, Aug 1, 2017): 29 teams who lost 12+ one-win games over a two-year stretch went **123-113 (.521)** in close games the next year and improved by an average **2.2 wins**; 35 teams ≤.250 in close games over two years returned to **.504** and improved by **3.4 wins**. Snippet-verified (article body did not render): VSiN's annual series reports "44 teams [since the era] had at least 8 close wins… only six improved the next season" ([2025 edition](https://vsin.com/nfl/studying-2025-nfl-quality-wins-losses-to-predict-2026-results/)) and "Since 1993, 46 teams have lost 8+ games in 'close loss' format… 39 improved, with a combined average improvement of 3.97 wins" ([2024 edition](https://vsin.com/nfl/studying-2024-nfl-quality-wins-losses-to-predict-2025-success/)).
- **Sample floor:** regression framing "assumes rough continuity" — a new QB/coach breaks the link (NFL Analytics, Jun 16, 2026, "Caveats and Limits").

**Close losses / one-score records:**
- Base rates: across **7,276 games 1999–2025, 50.7% of NFL games end within one score (≤8 points** — the 8-point cutoff is the maximum one-possession comeback: TD + 2PT); 1–3-point games are 23.3%; the share rose from 49.9% (1999–2004) to 52.7% (2017–2025) (NFL Analytics, "One-Score Games," Jul 4, 2026, [full text](https://nflanalytic.com/explainer-one-score-games.html)).
- **Core dataset finding (6,967 games 1999–2025, 829 franchise season-pairs, nflverse data, bootstrap CIs):** a team's one-score win% correlates only **+0.08 (±0.04)** with its own one-score record a year later, while **point margin persists at +0.40 (±0.03)**; the 140 team-seasons that won 70%+ of close games (avg .798) came back at **.515**, the 138 that won ≤30% at **.462**; the 2024 Chiefs went **11-0 in one-score games, then 1-9 in 2025** with a *positive* scoring margin; controlling for point margin, close-game record adds nothing (coefficient −0.02, t = −0.7, vs margin's t = +10.0) — "a receipt for luck already spent" (NFL Analytics, "One-Score Records Are Luck: The Year-After Test," Aug 9, 2026, [full text](https://nflanalytic.com/explainer-close-game-luck.html)). Caveat from the same page: "Zero correlation is not zero skill… Whatever close-game skill exists is too small to see across 27 seasons."
- **Sample floor:** on a per-team basis the NFL averages ~8 close games per season, so "a 3-game 0-3 close record is indistinguishable from skill" (NFL Analytics, Aug 9, 2026, "diluted below detection by eight-game samples").
- Classic companion stat: "Record in games decided by seven points or fewer: 8-1 (.889)" (Barnwell, Aug 1, 2017); teams ≥75% in 6+ such games went 50.7% the next year (Grantland, 2012).
- League-chat "luck gap" method (ffwrapped, "Schedule Luck," undated; retrieved 2026-09-18, [full text](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/unluckiest-team)): **all-play record** (each week, count how many of the other 9 teams you would have outscored) → **expected wins** = all-play win% × games played → **luck gap** = expected wins − actual wins, explained with **points against** and **close losses, counted at <5 and <10 points**. Rhetorical rule: "'You missed the playoffs because of three losses by a combined 8.6 points' is the kind of detail that starts a league chat argument" — concrete small numbers beat "you were unlucky by 2.4 expected wins."
- Single-game luck ceilings (keep in mind before making confident regression claims): only 48% of team records differed from a pure-luck binomial → "in 52% of NFL games, luck is the deciding factor" (Advanced Football Analytics, Jul 31, 2007, [full text](http://www.advancedfootballanalytics.com/2007/08/luck-and-nfl-outcomes.html)); a synthesized skill/luck fit at **52.5% luck** implies "**76% correct would be the theoretical ceiling**" for game prediction (Aug 2007, [full text](http://www.advancedfootballanalytics.com/2007/08/luck-and-nfl-outcomes-3.html)). The best-team-wins study (Lopez, Matthews & Baumer, *Annals of Applied Statistics* 12(4), 2018, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/18-aoas1165); [arXiv:1701.05976, Jan 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.05976); abstract only retrievable): the NFL has the largest talent dispersion of the four leagues — NFL *seasons* are skill-driven, NFL *single games* are still coin-flip-ish.
- **Week-1 floor:** winning any single game predicts the rest of the season at r ≈ +0.2–0.3 (r² = 3.7%, 861 openers 1999–2025) — "It's evidence, not destiny" (NFL Analytics, "Week 1 Signal," live page, [full text](https://nflanalytic.com/explainer-week-1-signal.html)); "a receiver who posts 3 catches for 28 yards in Week 1 has produced exactly one data point — not a trend," with preseason projections weighted ~60–70% vs in-season data ~30–40% through Week 3 (Fantasy Start/Sit, "Early Season Start/Sit Decisions," undated, [full text](https://fantasystartsit.com/early-season-start-sit)).

**Points against (PA) as a luck indicator:**
- No published number cleanly splits "PA variance = X% luck vs Y% skill" — **gap**. The field's answer is an *adjustment pipeline*: opponent adjustment, garbage-time downweighting, rolling windows.
- Raw points-allowed is opponent-schedule dependent: "A defense that has faced 4 consecutive pass-heavy offenses will accumulate fantasy points allowed at an inflated rate… the schedule effect does not represent true defensive weakness"; completion-%-over-expectation needs **~200 targets** before year-to-year correlation is meaningful, and rate stats from "fewer than roughly 100 independent observations" are "a candidate for meaningful regression" (Matchup Analytics, live 2026 page, [full text](https://matchupanalytics.com/regression-to-the-mean-in-matchup-analytics)).
- DVOA's luck-cleaning steps: red-zone performance regressed to team baseline, **turnovers downweighted to 75%**, blowout/garbage-time plays downweighted (Aaron Schatz, "Introducing DVOA v8.0," Sep 5, 2023, [full text](https://ftnfantasy.com/nfl/introducing-dvoa-v8-0)); predictive validation: DVOA-to-future-wins correlation 0.419 (2013–2022). The honest counterpoint: a third-party glossary still lists "Doesn't Account for Luck and Variance" as a DVOA limitation ([betmana, undated](https://betmana.co.uk/glossary/dvoa/)) — DVOA reduces, not eliminates, luck.
- Fantasy-specific: **raw fantasy points-allowed correlates < 0.10 (often < 0.06) with next week's output at RB/WR/TE; points-for runs 0.15–0.20**; a combined cross-validated model reaches ~0.35, near Vegas-line territory (0.36) (Subvertadown, May 22, 2022, updated Sep 10, 2026, [full text](https://subvertadown.com/article/-fantasy-points-allowed-too-weak-for-making-lineup-decisions)). Usable only after opponent adjustment, only as a small modifier.
- The opponent adjustment in practice: **schedule-adjusted fantasy points allowed (aFPA) uses a rolling 10-week, opponent-adjusted window** (4for4 support explainer, modified Sep 9, 2023, [full text](https://support.4for4.com/support/solutions/articles/19000054611-what-is-schedule-adjusted-fantasy-points-allowed-/)). Quantified payoff on 2013 data (C.D. Carter, 4for4, Mar 4, 2014, [full text](https://www.4for4.com/2014/preseason/learning-love-schedule-adjusted-fantasy-points-allowed-afpa)): the five worst teams in aFPA vs. QB allowed top-12 QB performances in precisely **half** of their collective games (the Vikings alone in **75%**; New England ranked only 11th in raw points allowed yet made the adjusted worst-five), while the best five allowed them in just **23.7%**. The standard public weekly tables live at [FantasyPros](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/points-allowed.php) and [RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/football/defense-vs-pos.php) (neither rendered text through fetch — data-location pointers only, no numbers attributed).
- In the Pythagorean formula, PA enters *nonlinearly* — a single blowout-inflated allowance moves expected record far less than the raw number suggests; known distortion: "A couple of garbage-time-inflated routs can pad point differential without reflecting true strength" (NFL Analytics, Jun 16, 2026).

**Usage over outcomes for forward calls (the anti-luck rule for player advice):**
- **Sample-size boundaries:** **<3 games: "Almost any rate stat should be treated as noise"; 3–6 games: directional only, "regression weight should still dominate"; 7+ games: "beginning to carry meaningful predictive weight"** (volume metrics like target share stabilize before efficiency metrics; "Touchdowns are the single most volatile fantasy scoring element") (Fantasy Projection Lab, undated, [regression guide](https://fantasyprojectionlab.com/regression-to-mean-in-fantasy/) and [waiver guide](https://fantasyprojectionlab.com/using-projections-for-waiver-wire-decisions/)).
- **Opportunity ≠ ownership:** in a 10-year, 30-player study, eventual league-winner breakouts averaged **19.2 PPR points** in Week 1 vs **19.1** for busts — points don't separate them; whether the job is *his* does (Malcolm Brown 2020: 21 touches but Akers also had 15 → bust; Puka Nacua 2023: 15 targets with Kupp out ≥4 weeks → winner) (Must Be Sunday, Sep 8, 2026, [full text](https://mustbesunday.com/2026/09/08/stop-chasing-week-1-points/)).
- **Role change vs hot streak:** "a receiver whose target share climbs from 14% to 23% following a team injury is experiencing a role change, not a hot streak" (Matchup Analytics, live 2026 page).
- **Don't call a player "due":** "Prior extreme games do not increase the probability of a bounce-back; they are simply evidence of variance. Regression toward the mean is a population statement, not a predictive statement about any individual player's next game" (Matchup Analytics).
- **Weekly "bad game or bad sign" template:** ESPN Fantasy's standing column (Matt Bowen, Sep 17, 2026, [full text](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/49957386/fantasy-football-rankings-value-bad-game-bad-sign) — "It's easy to overreact to Week 1") with companion "Facts vs. Feelings" (Liz Loza, Sep 16, 2026, [full text](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/49956322/facts-vs-feelings-fantasy-advice-week-2)); a single underperformance week is variance to regress against, not evidence of decline.
- **Accountable regression columns exist and are graded:** Football Guys "Regression Alert" (Sep 17, 2026, [full text](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2026-regression-alert-week02)) ranks all players on a metric, predicts the bottom group outscores the top going forward, and **publishes a tracked hit rate (51-17, 75% over eight seasons)** — the template for regression claims an assistant can make *and be graded on*. Note NBC Sports fantasy's deliberate user-facing avoidance of the word "luck" ('luck' is "so crass and unsophisticated") in ["Regression Files"](https://www.nbcsports.com/fantasy/football/news/regression-files-week-14-dont-give-up-on-rome-odunze) — prefer "variance" / "regression" in output.
- Also on the books but no finding attributed: Koopmeiners, "A Comparison of the Autocorrelation and Variance of NFL Team Strengths Over Time," *J. Quantitative Analysis in Sports* 8(3) (2012) — publisher page returned HTTP 202, no body, on 2026-09-18 ([link](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/1559-0410.1422/html)); search-abstract describes NFL competitive balance post-1994.

## 2. Where experts disagree

**(a) The Pythagorean exponent — constant vs floating, NFL vs fantasy.**
Position A: static **2.37** — Barnwell/ESPN ([Nov 8, 2016](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/17998672/raiders-bills-opposite-ends-luck-spectrum-far-2016-nfl-season); [Aug 1, 2017](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/20199657/nfl-teams-likely-improve-decline-2017-season-bill-barnwell-pythagorean-expectation)), Football Outsiders' standard since 2003, reconfirmed as optimal for the NFL in a 2025 peer-reviewed study ([Frontiers, Sep 12, 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2025.1638446/full)).
Position B: floating / refit — **2.57** best-fit over 1990–2013 with **Pythagenpat** x = [(PF+PA)/G]^0.251 (Seahawks 2.53, Denver 2.83 in 2013) (Stuart, Feb 11, 2014); **Pythagenport** x = 1.5·log10[(PF+PA)/G], adopted by Football Outsiders in 2011 (Neil Paine, Nov 2012, [full text](https://www.footballperspective.com/data-dump-schedule-adjusted-pythagorean-ratings-1970-2012/)); a **2.67** best-fit for 2001–10 with the key improvement coming from applying the formula *per game* (correlation with actual wins 0.91 → 0.95) rather than the exponent (Advanced NFL Stats Community, Dec 2011, [full text](http://community.advancednflstats.com/2011/12/towards-better-pythagorean-should.html)).
Fantasy position: **6** (Ludwinski, Nov 16, 2017, attributed to Yahoo's undisclosed method) — the only dated, named-expert fantasy value. *Stuart's own verdict on the NFL variants: the differences are "too minor to have any practical effect." For this league: 2.37 is fine for NFL-team analogs; use the larger fantasy value (≈4–6, fit per-league) for fantasy scores and state which exponent was used.*

**(b) Regression weight to .500.**
Position A: NFL year-over-year slope **0.31** (Stuart, Mar 25, 2015) / **0.3265** for actual wins vs **0.3995** for Pythagorean wins (Stuart, Feb 11, 2014) — i.e., ~1/3, more credit if Pythagorean is the base.
Position B: baseball midseason forms — "10 to 12 games" of regression at 46 of 162 games (Tango, ~2005) and the .060-SD ≈ 69-game Bayesian prior (Tango, May 2006), which imply a different (game-count-dependent) shrinkage for a 14-game season.
*No fantasy (or NFL midseason) publication states the fraction — this is a declared gap; the assistant must use one anchor and flag it as an assumption.*

**(c) Include remaining schedule strength, or not.**
Position A: include — The Athletic's DPR and the Washington Post both build schedule strength into their power ratings (links in §1.4). Position B: don't — Stuart's Pythagorean tables "do not incorporate strength of schedule or anything other than points scored and points allowed" (Feb 11, 2014); NFL Analytics concedes raw PF/PA "ignore[s] schedule" and recommends pairing with a SOS adjustment (Jun 16, 2026). *The data-backed preference: include it, game-by-game (each opponent's current scoring enters their PA) — with more fixed pairings in a 14-game fantasy season, schedule matters more than in the NFL's balanced 17. Note in output that this is a point of view, not consensus.*

**(d) Closed-form vs simulation for "playoff odds."**
Position A: closed-form — the feeder's §1.4 method; it reproduces the *inputs* every simulator draws from and is what an assistant that can't run sims should ship. Position B: simulation owns the genre — Athletic (1,000,000 / 100,000 sims), WaPo (25,000), ESPN MLB (10,000), MFA (10,000), ffwrapped (5,000) (§1.5 links). *The closed-form camp has no big-brand proponent; treat closed-form as an approximation of the sim and say so in output (per the feeder). The more data-backed source for the probability figure itself is the named simulation; the more data-backed source for the *inputs* is the closed-form math.*

**(e) Does Pythagorean overachievement predict decline?**
Position A: yes, in aggregate — the 10 largest gaps 1989–2015 declined 7 of 10, average −3.4 wins; 2–3-win overachievers −3 wins (Barnwell, Aug 1, 2017). Position B: not at the individual/betting level — ~52% hit rate betting on early-season outliers since 1999 (Healy, Sep 2014). *Use Position A for population framing, Position B as the hedge; never present regression as an individual guarantee.*

**(f) Do close-game records predict anything?**
Position A (NFL, preferred): one-score record is noise once margin is known — r = +0.08 vs margin's +0.40 (NFL Analytics, Aug 9, 2026). Position B (college): one-score record carries real signal, r = −0.37 across 1,591 FBS team-seasons, shrinking to −0.21 controlling for prior wins (Punt and Rally, live page, [full text](https://www.puntandrally.com/backtest_regression.php)). *The NFL page is the stronger, sport-specific dataset (nflverse, 27 seasons, bootstrap CIs) — use it for NFL players and never present the college number for the NFL. Same site's turnover-margin test: r = −0.27 raw, but 0.02 once prior wins are held fixed — turnover margin is mostly a proxy for "this team was good."*

**(g) Preview/recap length: brevity vs depth.**
Position A (brevity): "a few hundred words… three good observations rather than six filled-out sections" (ffwrapped, undated, retrieved 2026-09-18); "stating the obvious is… a waste of time" (Fantasy Football for Winners, Nov 3, 2014, [full text](https://www.fantasyfootballforwinners.com/2014/11/stating-obvious-is-boring-and-worthless.html)). Position B (depth): Hribar's 13-year every-game worksheet with league-wide trend tables (Sep 17, 2026); The Athletic's five-section all-in-one cheat sheet (Sep 4, 2025, snippet-only). *Practical synthesis, stated by ffwrapped itself: "An email newsletter can go longer if your league enjoys the extra detail" — short for the league chat, deep only where a story earns it.*

**(h) How much to trust the latest week.**
Position A (act fast): 4for4's strategy guide frames the waiver wire as the top lever ("There is nothing more important in fantasy football than winning the waiver wire") and argues for acting on it, distinguishing rolling lists from FAAB (2025 preseason guide, JS-gated — snippet-verified, [link](https://www.4for4.com/2025/preseason/ultimate-guide-waiver-wire-faab-strategy-2025)). Position B (wait/verify): Fantasy Life, "Avoiding Week 1 Overreactions" (2026 preseason, snippet-only, [link](https://www.fantasylife.com/articles/redraft/avoiding-week-1-overreactions)); Must Be Sunday's "freeze your targets before Sunday can make us look smarter" (Sep 8, 2026); Footballguys' 10-factor verification (Sep 15, 2026). *The data-backed tilt is toward B for trend claims (r² = 3.7% per game, §1.6) and toward A only when a verified role change (ownership + eligibility) justifies it.*

**(i) Publishing exact probabilities at all.**
Position A (simulation standard): Baseball Prospectus' canonical odds machinery (circa 2006); ffwrapped (5,000 sims) and MFA (10,000 sims) publish their methods. Position B (skepticism): Gretch on "engagement-farming threads that GUARANTEE they've figured out **6.784 key trends** after charting 394 WRs because they read how humans are more attracted to precise numbers than rounded ones" (Aug 9, 2023, [full text](https://bengretch.substack.com/p/fantasy-analysis-keeps-getting-better)); ffwrapped's own caution that even displayed 0%/100% is an estimate (undated, retrieved 2026-09-18). *Synthesis rule: publish the number only with a one-line method and a caveat.*

**(j) ML vs Pythagorean.**
Position A: random forest/neural nets outperform the Pythagorean formula on 2003–2023 NFL data (R² 0.891) (Frontiers, Sep 12, 2025). Position B's counterweight, from the same study: points scored/allowed remained the top two SHAP features — the Pythagorean captures most of the signal with one formula. *For a lightweight assistant this settles toward keeping the formula, but the disagreement is real and recent.*

**(k) DVOA and luck.**
Position A: DVOA v8.0 actively *removes* luck — red-zone regression, turnovers downweighted to 75%, blowout plays downweighted (Schatz, Sep 5, 2023). Position B: a third-party glossary lists "Doesn't Account for Luck and Variance" as a standing DVOA limitation (betmana, undated). *Honest read per the feeder: DVOA reduces, not eliminates, luck. (Lower-authority source; noted accordingly.)*

**(l) Minor feeder conflict, resolved:** one feeder cited the Pythagorean-in-football student journal without a year; the other retrieved the DOI record (Brady & Sommers, *Journal of Student Research* 10(4), 2021) — the more complete, retrieved citation is used here, and no finding is attributed to the paper beyond its existence.

## 3. What bad advice looks like

Ten checkable failure patterns. The judge's instruction for each: **the answer fails if…**

1. **Generic filler / stating the obvious.** The answer fails if any recommendation lacks a reason plus a number the reader couldn't find by reading the headline — advice equivalent to "start Dez Bryant this week": "They're boring. They're a commodity, appearing in thousands of places… A waste of time" ([Fantasy Football for Winners, Nov 3, 2014](https://www.fantasyfootballforwinners.com/2014/11/stating-obvious-is-boring-and-worthless.html)).
2. **Unverified scores, or invented game flow.** The answer fails if any cited score, record, or margin doesn't match the actual data ("Check all the scores before calling something the toughest loss" — the "toughest loss" must be verified against *all* other teams' scores, not the opponent's), or if it describes a comeback, drive, or garbage-time score from a final score alone: "Only describe a comeback if you know it happened… If all you have is the final score, 'won by 1.5' is plenty" ([ffwrapped weekly recap guide, undated, retrieved 2026-09-18](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/fantasy-football-weekly-recap)).
3. **Claiming an illegal lineup swap.** The answer fails if "should have started X" doesn't verify X was eligible for an open slot, or omits that a winning lineup sometimes needs **two changes rather than one** in 2-RB/2-WR/TE+FLEX formats ("A bench player outscoring a starter doesn't automatically mean the manager could have made that swap. Check the lineup slots and whether the change was legal" — same ffwrapped source).
4. **Chasing last week's points.** The answer fails if any start/sit, waiver, or FAAB call rests on a single week of fantasy points without usage/role evidence: "Chasing points is real, and it's a slippery slope… you start him next week, and he delivers a total disappointment" ([Footballguys, Sep 15, 2026](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2026-fantasy-overview-week02)); "The instinct is to treat Week 1 as a revelation. The math says treat it as a data point" ([Fantasy Footballers, Aug 31, 2026](https://www.thefantasyfootballers.com/analysis/the-fantasy-mythbusters-is-week-1-a-performance-indicator-for-the-rest-of-the-season-fantasy-football/)); winner breakouts and busts scored identically in Week 1 (19.2 vs 19.1 PPR) — what separates them is the job ([Must Be Sunday, Sep 8, 2026](https://mustbesunday.com/2026/09/08/stop-chasing-week-1-points/)).
5. **Waiver/FAAB advice with no mechanics.** The answer fails if "add X" doesn't state who is injured/departing, whether the incumbent is still involved (opportunity ≠ ownership), rostered % (claimability), who has rolling-waiver claim priority, or — in a 100-FAAB league — a bid with a ceiling ("this waiver-wire column will address both shallow and deep leagues each week" — [RotoWire Week 2, 2026](https://www.rotowire.com/football/article/fantasy-football-waiver-wire-pickups-for-week-2-95907), snippet-verified; "There is nothing more important in fantasy football than winning the waiver wire" — [4for4, 2025](https://www.4for4.com/2025/preseason/ultimate-guide-waiver-wire-faab-strategy-2025), snippet-verified).
6. **Fake precision.** The answer fails if it carries more decimals than its method justifies, or emits a probability with no stated method and caveats — "engagement-farming threads that GUARANTEE they've figured out 6.784 key trends after charting 394 WRs" ([Ben Gretch, Aug 9, 2023](https://bengretch.substack.com/p/fantasy-analysis-keeps-getting-better)); "Even a displayed 0% or 100% [playoff probability] is a simulation estimate… don't treat a high probability as a confirmed playoff berth" ([ffwrapped playoff odds, undated, retrieved 2026-09-18](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/fantasy-football-playoff-odds)).
7. **Hedged, meatless, non-numeric boilerplate.** The answer fails if a recommendation has no clear bottom line, or if a hedge ships without an attached evidence number: AI content's fatal flaw is being "lifeless, meandering and often meatless… no supportive advanced data is referenced. **No bold prediction is made**… The argument is incomplete, which leaves the consumer unfulfilled" ([Gaming Juice, bylined May 16, 2026](https://www.thegamingjuice.com/2025/06/12/artificial-intelligence-content-fantasy-football)). Contrast: ESPN's hedges ("a fringe starting option") are always paired with specific usage data (Sep 17, 2026) — hedges are acceptable only when each carries an evidence number.
8. **Luck framing or trend claims on a too-small sample.** The answer fails if it labels a player/roster "lucky," "due," or "trending" from fewer than ~3 weeks without an explicit small-sample label — one game explains ~3.7% of what follows (r²; [NFL Analytics, Week 1 Signal, live page](https://nflanalytic.com/explainer-week-1-signal.html)); "<3 games: almost any rate stat is noise" ([Fantasy Projection Lab, undated](https://fantasyprojectionlab.com/regression-to-mean-in-fantasy/)); "Do not overrate one painful matchup. Every fantasy manager has one" ([ffwrapped, undated, retrieved 2026-09-18](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/unluckiest-team)); "a sea of overreactions" ([Hribar, Sep 17, 2026](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/fantasy/nfl-week-1-preview-fantasy-football-worksheet-rich-hribar-2025/)). Close-game luck framing additionally fails on fewer than ~8 close games (per-team detection floor, [NFL Analytics, Aug 9, 2026](https://nflanalytic.com/explainer-close-game-luck.html)).
9. **A playoff call with no schedule or no method.** The answer fails if any playoff line or odds figure ignores the remaining matchups (schedule strength is more important in a fixed-pairing 14-game season than in the NFL's balanced one — [NFL Analytics, Jun 16, 2026](https://nflanalytic.com/explainer-pythagorean-wins.html); Stuart, Feb 11, 2014), reports in/out without the gap to the line, or cites a probability not traceable to a Pythagorean projection, a base-rate table, or a named simulation ([Guru Fantasy World, Sep 23, 2023](https://gurufantasyworld.com/understanding-playoff-probabilities/); [Datafield ch. 7, 2024–2025](https://datafield.dev/sports-betting-textbook/part-02/chapter-07/)).
10. **A recap that dumps stats or recites standings.** The answer fails if numbers aren't in service of one stated story per section ("Readers' eyes will glaze if you offer too many statistics, especially if these numbers are packed together" — [Sports Media Field Guide, Nov 4, 2014](https://sportsfieldguide.org/2014/11/04/breaking-down-game-elements-to-create-a-compelling-recap/)), or if the week is reported as "little more than a scoreline and a handful of adjectives" with no implication — every recap must state at least one of: record movement, seed impact, or playoff-line movement ("The fun part is explaining what the week meant"; "WHERE THAT LEAVES US" is a mandatory template section — [ffwrapped, undated, retrieved 2026-09-18](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/fantasy-football-weekly-recap)).

## 4. Glossary

Canonical phrasing the assistant's prompt should standardize on:

1. **Preview** — Forward-looking, published before kickoff: game-by-game matchup blocks with projected scores and one start/sit or "lineup lock" call per matchup (structure per ESPN Playbook, Sep 17, 2026; The Athletic cheat sheet, Sep 4, 2025).
2. **Recap** — Backward-looking, published after final scores: story-first, every matchup score included, ~200–500 words for league chat with depth on 2–3 stories (ffwrapped, undated, retrieved 2026-09-18).
3. **Matchup block** — The atomic unit of both formats: one league game (or one NFL game in a preview) with score, margin, one story, and one implication; all blocks use the same field order every week.
4. **Lede** — The first paragraph; must carry score + hero + significance in 40–60 words because most readers stop there (River, 2026; nut graph = the context paragraph of who/what/when/score/why — Sports Media Field Guide, Nov 4, 2014).
5. **Lineup lock** — A must-start player for this format; the term ESPN uses for its per-game start list (ESPN Playbook, Sep 17, 2026).
6. **Usage evidence** — The *reason* layer under any start/sit or waiver call: snap share, route share, target share, red-zone targets, garbage-time share — not last week's fantasy points (ESPN, Sep 17, 2026; Footballguys 10-factor checklist, Sep 15, 2026).
7. **Pythagorean expectation** — Expected winning percentage from points: Win% = PF^x/(PF^x + PA^x), where **x (the Pythagorean exponent)** is 2.37 for NFL scoring and ≈4–6 for fantasy scoring (fit per-league; the only dated fantasy anchor is 6 — Ludwinski, Nov 16, 2017). "Pythagorean wins" = expected win% × games played; the gap between actual and Pythagorean wins is the overshoot/undershoot, always stated **in wins** (Barnwell, Aug 1, 2017; NFL Analytics, Jun 16, 2026).
8. **Regression to .500** — Shrinking a record toward 7-7 before projecting the rest of the season; weight ≈ 1/3 of wins above average (NFL year-over-year anchor: Stuart, Mar 25, 2015), flagged as an assumption since no fantasy calibration is published.
9. **Projected final record** — `E(W) = wins so far + Σ p_i` over the remaining games, where each p_i is that game's Pythagorean- or margin-based win probability against the specific opponent; the number that places a team in the playoff race.
10. **Playoff line** — In this 10-team/6-in league, the 6th-highest projected final win total league-wide; the de facto cutoff. Always report the gap to the line ("0.4 wins clear"), never a bare in/out flag (method per §1.4; the counting practice of [ffwrapped](https://ffwrapped.com/fantasy-football-playoff-odds-calculator) and [My Fantasy Analyzer](https://myfantasyanalyzer.com/playoff-odds/), both undated, retrieved 2026-09-18).
11. **Monte Carlo playoff odds** — Replaying the remaining weeks N times and counting finishes in the top 6; 5,000–10,000 is the published fantasy norm (ffwrapped 5,000; MFA 10,000; NFL products run 25,000–1,000,000 — WaPo 2024–25; Athletic Dec 29, 2020). Every such percentage in output carries a one-line method statement and is an estimate, never a clinch (ffwrapped odds post, undated, retrieved 2026-09-18).
12. **Close loss** — A loss by a small margin; working thresholds are **<5 and <10 points** for league-chat luck stories (ffwrapped, undated, retrieved 2026-09-18); in the NFL literature "one-score" = ≤8 points (NFL Analytics, Jul 4, 2026). Remember: the close-loss *record* is a "receipt for luck already spent" once point margin is known (NFL Analytics, Aug 9, 2026) — lead with the margin, not the count.
13. **Luck gap** — The league-luck framing: **all-play record** (wins against every other team's score each week, ignoring assigned matchups) → expected wins = all-play win% × games played → luck gap = expected − actual, explained with points against and close losses (ffwrapped, undated, retrieved 2026-09-18).
14. **Points for / points against (PF/PA)** — Season totals scored / allowed; inputs to Pythagorean expectation, luck stories, and the no-sim odds method. Raw fantasy points-against is weak (<0.10 predictive correlation) and usable only opponent-adjusted (Subvertadown, May 22, 2022, updated Sep 10, 2026; 4for4 aFPA rolling 10-week window, modified Sep 9, 2023).
15. **Waiver priority (rolling) / FAAB / ownership** — Claim order that resets to the bottom after each successful claim, so "add X" advice must name who currently has priority; in this league's **100-FAAB** setting, the advice is a bid with a stated ceiling, not just a name; **ownership (rostered %)** is the share of leagues rostering the player, driving realistic claimability; a target is only actionable if the incumbent's involvement is actually gone (opportunity ≠ ownership — Must Be Sunday, Sep 8, 2026; RotoWire, 2026).
16. **FLEX** — The format slot usable by any RB/WR/TE (lineup: QB/RB/RB/WR/WR/TE/FLEX/K/DEF + 5 bench); any "should have started X" claim must verify X was eligible for the FLEX (or another open slot) and name all swaps required (ffwrapped, undated, retrieved 2026-09-18).

*Note on "small sample": any claim from fewer than ~3 weeks of data must be explicitly labeled as such in output (Fantasy Projection Lab sample boundaries, undated; NFL Analytics Week 1 Signal, live page).*

### Named gaps (do not invent around these)

- No published word-count target for *fantasy-specific* previews/recaps at a named outlet; the 800–1,000-word figure is general sports-recap guidance (River, 2026) and "a few hundred words" is ffwrapped's league-chat guide (undated).
- No published base-rate playoff table for 10-team/6-playoff leagues; the 12-team/6-playoff table (Guru Fantasy World, Sep 23, 2023) is the standard proxy.
- No dated, published fantasy-football Pythagorean exponent beyond Ludwinski's single 2017 data point (x = 6) — fit per-league.
- No published midseason regression-to-.500 fraction for NFL or fantasy — the ≈1/3 NFL year-over-year anchor is a repurposed statistic.
- No peer-reviewed "points-allowed variance = X% luck vs Y% skill" number exists for the NFL or fantasy — the field's answer is the adjustment pipeline (opponent adjustment, garbage-time downweighting, rolling windows).
