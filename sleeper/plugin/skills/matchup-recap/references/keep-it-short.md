# Length and honesty norms

Source: `report.md` (R4), section 1.2 and the "what bad advice looks like"
patterns 1, 6, 7, 10, plus the "Named gaps" section.

No fantasy outlet publishes a word-count target for fantasy-specific
previews/recaps — a gap. The figures below are the report's best available
proxies: general sports-recap guidance and a fantasy league-chat guide.

## Length rules

**Rule L1** — a league-chat recap runs about **200-500 words**, every
matchup score included, depth concentrated in 2-3 stories ([ffwrapped,
undated, retrieved 2026-09-18](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/fantasy-football-weekly-recap)):
"for a league chat, I'd aim for a few hundred words. Include each matchup
score, then spend most of the space on the two or three things people will
care about."

**Rule L3** — story first, scores as a table, never a prose sequence:
"chronological recaps bury the story" ([River, 2026](https://rivereditor.com/blogs/write-sports-game-recap-1000-words)).

**Rule L4** — the lede carries score + hero + significance in **40-60
words**, because "73% of readers only read the first paragraph" (River,
2026).

**Rule L5** — include a "what this means for standings" element early.

## What bad advice looks like — write to avoid these

1. **Generic filler.** Any recommendation without a reason plus a number the
   reader couldn't already see from the headline fails — "advice equivalent
   to 'start Dez Bryant this week'... a waste of time" ([Fantasy Football
   for Winners, Nov 3, 2014](https://www.fantasyfootballforwinners.com/2014/11/stating-obvious-is-boring-and-worthless.html)).
6. **Fake precision.** More decimals than the method justifies, or a
   probability with no stated method and no caveat, fails — "engagement-
   farming threads that GUARANTEE they've figured out 6.784 key trends"
   ([Ben Gretch, Aug 9, 2023](https://bengretch.substack.com/p/fantasy-analysis-keeps-getting-better)).
7. **Hedged, meatless boilerplate.** A recommendation with no clear bottom
   line, or a hedge with no evidence number attached, fails — AI content's
   named failure mode is being "lifeless, meandering and often meatless...
   no bold prediction is made" ([The Gaming Juice, bylined May 16, 2026](https://www.thegamingjuice.com/2025/06/12/artificial-intelligence-content-fantasy-football)).
   ESPN's own hedges ("a fringe starting option") are acceptable because
   each one carries a specific usage number.
10. **Stat dumps and scorelines with no implication.** Numbers must serve
    one stated story per section — "readers' eyes will glaze if you offer
    too many statistics, especially if these numbers are packed together"
    ([Sports Media Field Guide, Nov 4, 2014](https://sportsfieldguide.org/2014/11/04/breaking-down-game-elements-to-create-a-compelling-recap/)).
    Every recap must state at least one of: record movement, seed impact,
    or playoff-line movement.

## Named gaps — do not invent around these

No published word-count target exists for fantasy-specific previews/recaps
at a named outlet; no published base-rate playoff table exists for a
10-team/6-playoff league; no dated fantasy Pythagorean exponent exists
beyond Ludwinski's single 2017 data point; no published midseason
regression-to-.500 fraction exists for NFL or fantasy; no peer-reviewed
"points-allowed variance = X% luck vs Y% skill" number exists. State the gap
rather than filling it with an invented figure.
