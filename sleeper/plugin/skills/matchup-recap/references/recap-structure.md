# Preview/recap structure

Source: `report.md` (R4), section 1.1 and 1.3.

## One uniform block per matchup

The dominant published format (ESPN, FantasyPros, The Athletic, RotoWire,
Sharp Football Analysis) is game-by-game: one repeating block per matchup,
same field order every week. [ESPN "The Playbook" (Mike Clay, Sep 17,
2026)](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/49948423/fantasy-playbook-projections-lineups-locks-2026-week-2-shadow-reports)
repeats a fixed block per game: projected score, then a "lineup locks" name
list, then a usage-anchored paragraph, then O/U and win probability. **Rule
S1** — keep the internal field order identical week to week.

## State the league format up front

ESPN states its "12-team PPR... standard scoring" premise in the lede
whenever format affects a recommendation. The equivalent here is stating "10
teams, full PPR, rolling waivers, 100 FAAB, 6 playoff spots" whenever it
matters. **Rule S2.**

## Story first, scores as a table

[River, "Sports Game Recap Template" (2026)](https://rivereditor.com/blogs/write-sports-game-recap-1000-words):
"always by importance (inverted pyramid)... chronological recaps bury the
story"; the lede must carry score + hero + significance in 40-60 words,
because "73% of readers only read the first paragraph." Include a "what this
means for standings" element early — the digest's `summary` field and the
retro's biggest-swing framing exist for exactly this.

## Omissions beat filler

[ffwrapped weekly recap guide (undated, retrieved 2026-09-18)](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/fantasy-football-weekly-recap):
"I'd much rather read three good observations than six sections filled out
just because the template had them"; the detail readers want is "the detail
you wouldn't get from looking at the standings. Someone scored the
second-most points and lost." Every recap must state at least one of:
record movement, seed impact, or playoff-line movement — "the fun part is
explaining what the week meant."

## Forward calls rest on usage, not last week's points

ESPN's scoops lead with snap/route/target share numbers, not fantasy points
("Kincaid played on 77% of offensive snaps... ran a route on 71% of the
team's passing plays" — same ESPN Playbook). [Footballguys (Jeff Haseley,
Sep 15, 2026)](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2026-fantasy-overview-week02)
codifies a 10-factor verification checklist before believing a breakout:
snap counts, depth chart, coach comments, injury-opened doors, matchup
quality, garbage-time share, one-or-two-big-play dependence, red-zone
targets, target share %, TD-dependence.

## Verify scores and lineup legality — never infer game flow

[ffwrapped (undated, retrieved 2026-09-18)](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/fantasy-football-weekly-recap):
"Only describe a comeback if you know it happened... if all you have is the
final score, 'won by 1.5' is plenty." For a retro's "should have started X"
claim, verify X was actually eligible for an open slot — "a bench player
outscoring a starter doesn't automatically mean the manager could have made
that swap. Check the lineup slots"; a winning lineup sometimes needs two
changes, not one, in a 2-RB/2-WR/TE+FLEX format.

## League voice

[ffwrapped (undated)](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/fantasy-football-weekly-recap):
"a joke lands better when the score underneath is right"; mention the
rivalry when it's real. Bloodless, hedge-only recaps read as AI filler
([The Gaming Juice, bylined May 16, 2026](https://www.thegamingjuice.com/2025/06/12/artificial-intelligence-content-fantasy-football)):
"no bold prediction is made... the argument is incomplete."
