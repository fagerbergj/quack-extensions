# On-the-clock: the 60-90 second procedure

Source: `research-r9-drafting.md` §1, "Best available on the clock".

## The procedure

1. **Anchor on tiers, not a rank order.** Identify the highest tier that
   still has an available player; within it, take the player who best
   fits roster need / bye coverage.
2. **Cliff check first.** If one position has its last tier-X player and
   another has several tier-X-or-better players, take the one about to
   disappear - the question is "who is most likely to be gone before my
   *next* pick," not "who do I take now."
3. **Reach test = VONA.** If the target will almost certainly survive to
   the next pick, his VONA is ~0 - reaching is not an edge. Reaching
   more than one tier above ADP requires a stated positional-cliff
   justification; log the reach, don't celebrate it.
4. **Fallen-player check.** A target 8+ picks past ADP: confirm the
   cause (news vs. inattention) before treating it as value. Drifting
   *ahead* of ADP with no news is narrative inflation - a reason to
   fade, not chase.
5. **Positional-run override.** A run changes the answer only once it
   has emptied the tier at that position; avoid being the end-of-run
   picker (the 0.25-ADP-spot premium).
6. **Need sets the menu; value picks the winner.** Per-position menus
   are parameterized by current roster count at that position; value is
   the compass within the menu, not the reverse.
7. **Time-box.** The full VONA calc over every candidate cannot be
   hand-done in 60 seconds - a pre-built tier/availability board is what
   makes the pick fast.

## What to say in the 60-90 seconds

(a) One primary pick, with the marginal reason in one sentence ("next-
best RB falls a tier; next-best WR does not"); (b) a fallback for the
two documented failure modes (the primary was just sniped, or the
primary's tier is safe so pivot to need); (c) one line of tier context
("he's the last WR in this tier" vs. "four comparable RBs remain"); (d)
an availability estimate if confident; (e) one line of next-seat plan;
(f) one "do not" - the specific trap on the board right now (end-of-run
grab, own-RB handcuff, same-bye QB).

## What it should never do

- Recommend a player without tier context.
- Draft from the ADP/rankings list as the primary order.
- Reach across a tier cliff for a "favorite" with no stated cliff
  justification.
- Join a positional run while its tier still has depth.
- Treat a 12-team or other-platform ADP as this league's price.
- Present stale ADP as current with no snapshot timestamp.
- Build Zero RB in this 10-team league.
- Spend a mid-late pick on a generic (healthy-starter) handcuff, or let
  bye-week aesthetics displace a tier-cliff player.
- Present a long rankings list as the primary on-the-clock output.
- Assume clock or format mechanics - read the league's actual
  `pick_timer`; it is commissioner-set and changeable mid-draft, with no
  official default.
- Force a rigid pick-by-pick plan, or positional balance, regardless of
  what the board actually shows.
