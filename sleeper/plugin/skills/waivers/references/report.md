## 1. Decision rules and numeric thresholds

### 1.1 Rolling waivers vs. FAAB — mechanics and the first decision rule

**Mechanics.**

- **Rolling priority.** Starts in inverse draft order (worst draft pick = highest priority); every successful claim drops the claiming team to the bottom of the queue while everyone else moves up one; the order does **not** reset weekly — "managers who waited the longest to make a claim keep moving up in priority." [Sleeper Support – "What types of waivers do you support?", Aug 14, 2024](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/9656662-what-types-of-waivers-do-you-support); [Sleeper Blog – "How Do Waivers Work in Fantasy Football?", Aug 31, 2024](https://sleeper.com/blog/how-do-waivers-work-in-fantasy-football/). ESPN documents the identical "Option B: Move to Last After Claim" mechanic with the initial queue as the inverse of snake-draft order ([ESPN Fan Support – "Waiver Order Overview and Free Agent Budget Tiebreakers," undated official doc, accessed 2026-09-18](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/4669787227668-Waiver-Order-Overview-and-Free-Agent-Budget-Tiebreakers)).
- **Reverse-standings / fixed order (weekly reset).** Priority resets every Monday (ESPN) or each game week (Sleeper) by current standings — worst team gets #1. Documented as "Option A: Reset Each Week" by both platforms ([Sleeper Support, Aug 14, 2024](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/9656662-what-types-of-waivers-do-you-support); [ESPN Fan Support](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/4669787227668-Waiver-Order-Overview-and-Free-Agent-Budget-Tiebreakers)). 4for4's Ryan McDowell: "A 'worst-to-first' that resets by standings every week is very common. This method sounds equitable but is actually a punishment for winning" — he prefers rolling because it forces the "is this week's guy really worth my one bullet" trade-off. [4for4 – "The Ultimate Guide to Winning the Waiver Wire in 2026," Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026)
- **FAAB (blind-bid auction).** Fixed season-long budget ($100 default on [Sleeper, Aug 14, 2024](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/9656662-what-types-of-waivers-do-you-support) and the most-common setting per [Sleeper's FAAB explainer, Aug 25, 2024](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/9657110-how-do-faab-and-waivers-work)); all bids submitted blind in a window; highest bid wins each player; the winning amount is deducted from that manager's remaining budget; claims execute in **value-ranked order** (highest bid first); **$0 bids are allowed and win ties on lower waiver rank**; the commissioner can edit budgets. [Sleeper Support, Aug 25, 2024](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/9657110-how-do-faab-and-waivers-work). ESPN's two FAB variants — *FAB (Continuous)* (all unrostered players always on waivers, no instant free-agent adds) and *FAB* (unclaimed players clear to first-come, first-served free agency) — both move a winning team to the bottom of the FAB tiebreaker. [ESPN Fan Support – "Waivers Overview," accessed 2026-09-18](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000041152-Waivers-Overview)
- **The 24-hour rule (both systems share it).** A player dropped after **>24 hours** on a roster goes to waivers; dropped after **<24 hours** becomes an immediate free agent; free-agent adds do not affect priority. On ESPN the wire processes daily around 3:00 AM ET. [ESPN Fan Support – "Claim a Player Off Waivers," accessed 2026-09-18](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000071352-Claim-a-Player-Off-Waivers). On Sleeper the commissioner sets after-game lock + waiver-clear day + drop-to-waivers duration (1 or 2 days recommended). [Sleeper Support – "Waivers for Regular Season & Playoffs," updated week of 2026-09-18](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3978868-waivers-for-regular-season-playoffs)
- **Hybrid (this league's format).** A league can run rolling priority and a $100 FAAB simultaneously for different claim classes — teams "either use FAAB or rolling waivers, or in some cases, both." [Sleeper Support](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3978868-waivers-for-regular-season-playoffs)

**Decision rules the assistant must apply:**

1. **Channel decision first.** Classify each candidate add as priority-queue or bid-pool before any sizing or target decision; the two are distinct decision problems with distinct costs. [Sleeper Support, Aug 14, 2024](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/9656662-what-types-of-waivers-do-you-support); [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026)
2. **Track two resources separately:** waiver position (where you sit in the queue) and remaining FAAB.
3. **The 24-hour rule gates every drop recommendation** — whether a dropped player hits the wire at all, and when a claim window can land. [ESPN](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000071352-Claim-a-Player-Off-Waivers); [Sleeper](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3978868-waivers-for-regular-season-playoffs)
4. **$0 bids are a legal weapon in a hybrid league:** a $0 bid wins ties on lower waiver rank — relevant when you want a player without burning dollars but still consume priority. [Sleeper, Aug 25, 2024](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/9657110-how-do-faab-and-waivers-work)
5. **Drop-timing exploit (published tactic):** "On some platforms, you can drop a bench player once his game has finished. If you've got a guy you were cutting anyway, drop him Sunday evening and grab the handcuff behind a running back playing Monday night." [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026)

### 1.2 Bid sizing

**By need — published bands** (share of *remaining* FAAB unless noted):

| Need tier | Published band | Source (date) |
|---|---|---|
| Season-changing replacement (lead RB takes over a job, WR1 out of injury, QB1 replacement) | **25–40%** of remaining FAAB | [Calculator Collection – "FAAB Waiver Wire Strategy," Emil Björk, Aug 10, 2026](https://www.calculatorcollection.org/en/articles/faab-waiver-wire-strategy/) |
| Mid-tier flex / role player / bye-week fill | **5–15%** | same |
| Lottery ticket (handcuff, deep sleeper) | **1–3%** — "small bids on multiple lottery tickets… not big bids on one" | same |
| High-value trigger: only bid **>15% of remaining budget** if (a) multi-week starter injury with a clear 15+ carry/volume path for the backup, (b) positional-scarcity player, or (c) a fantasy-playoff-stretch (Weeks 14–16) need | trigger rule | [Fantasy Strategy Guide – FAAB Bidding Strategy (undated; secondary)](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/faab-bidding-strategy) |
| Third-string RB who needs an injury to see the field | **≤15%** of budget | [4for4, McDowell, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026) |
| Depth player after your second conditional bid | "no more than a few dollars" | same |
| Defense, any week, any matchup | **never more than $2**, "no exceptions" | same; [Fantasy Upside – "How FAAB Bidding Actually Works," Jun 22, 2026](https://fantasyupside.com/learn/how-faab-bidding-works) ("$1–2 max. Always. Defenses are matchup plays"); [4for4, Rajaram, Aug 27, 2025](https://www.4for4.com/2025/preseason/ultimate-guide-waiver-wire-faab-strategy-2025) ("a dollar or two") |
| Kicker | "**a dollar or nothing, always**" | [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026) |
| Conservative bench/depth generally | "$1 or two depending on position" | [FantasySP – FAAB Guide, Sep 9, 2025](https://www.fantasysp.com/news/nfl/3055414/fantasy-football-faab-guide-managing-budget) |
| The one season-defining bid (a legitimate every-week starter) | "**40, 50, 60 percent without flinching**" — "one bid a season that breaks all of the above" | [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026) |

Position rules beyond the bands: **QB** — don't spend big unless your starter is out for the season (the position is deep); **TE** — bid only on a confirmed breakout ("red-zone usage plus double-digit targets two weeks running"), otherwise stream. [Fantasy Upside, Jun 22, 2026](https://fantasyupside.com/learn/how-faab-bidding-works)

**Pre-bid gate — the "three Ps"** for any bid over 10% of starting budget: **P**ath (real route to volume, or does it need a second injury?), **P**ermanence (rental vs. job), **P**rice (is the premium over the likely second bid sensible?). "Fail one, cut the bid number in half. Fail two, just walk away." [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026)

**Anchor to your own upgrade, not the market:** "bid what the player is worth to *your* roster over the *remaining* season, not what the market says" — the upgrade over whoever you'd otherwise start. [Calculator Collection, Aug 10, 2026](https://www.calculatorcollection.org/en/articles/faab-waiver-wire-strategy/); [Fantasy Upside, Jun 22, 2026](https://fantasyupside.com/learn/how-faab-bidding-works) — worked example: an 8 PPR/week upgrade over your current starter × 12 weeks = 96 fantasy points, "worth a big bid." Todd Zola frames the same idea as supply/demand: your true bid is "a few units higher than the next offer, but low enough to leave ample budget for the rest of the season." [Fantasy Index – "The Art of FAAB," Apr 10, 2024](https://fantasyindex.com/2024/04/10/analysis/the-art-of-faab)

**By week / season phase — published models (they conflict; see §2.3):**

| Model | Rule | Source (date) |
|---|---|---|
| Three-phase, front-loaded | Weeks 2–5: spend **30–50%** of total budget combined ("sitting on 90% of your budget past Week 5" usually means missed pickups); Weeks 5–10: **30–40%**; Weeks 11–14: the season's largest bids — at Week 11 with 60–70% remaining *in playoff contention*, "spend aggressively… the dollars you don't spend in a losing playoff game are worth exactly zero"; an unspent $80 in Week 14 "is worth nothing" | [Calculator Collection, Aug 10, 2026](https://www.calculatorcollection.org/en/articles/faab-waiver-wire-strategy/) |
| Equal quarters | ~**25% per quarter**; "Week 1 produces the most overbids by far"; save **5–10%** of budget for the fantasy playoffs | [Fantasy Upside, Jun 22, 2026](https://fantasyupside.com/learn/how-faab-bidding-works) |
| Extreme front-loading | **75–80% of the entire budget early** on a legitimate top-10 player ("FAAB money doesn't score points") | [4for4, Rajaram, Aug 27, 2025](https://www.4for4.com/2025/preseason/ultimate-guide-waiver-wire-faab-strategy-2025) |
| Mid-season concentration | "Reserve budget for mid-season — the most valuable waiver wire opportunities tend to arrive in Weeks 5–10, when rosters are depleted and the top handcuffs become starters. Managers who spent aggressively in September have nothing left." | [Scoutcast.ai – Waiver Wire Strategy, updated Jul 8, 2026](https://scoutcast.ai/blog/fantasy-football-waiver-wire-strategy/) |
| Midpoint hold | Retain **30–40%** of FAAB past the midpoint of the regular season | [Fantasy Strategy Guide – FAAB (undated; secondary)](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/faab-bidding-strategy) |
| Phase by need type (priority leagues) | Early: "chase breakout players and league-winning upside"; late: "reliable weekly production, playoff matchups, handcuffs, and injury replacements that can help win now" + stash high-value backup RBs; late-season FAAB includes "strategic reserve management" | [Fantasy Owner – Early-Season vs Late-Season Waiver Wire Strategy, 2026](https://www.fantasyowner.com/articles/early-season-vs-late-season-waiver-strategy/) |
| Anti-hoarding | "FAAB depreciates whether you use it or not… be aggressive early. If a legitimate every-week starter surfaces in Week 2 and it costs 40% to land him, spend the 40%." | [4for4, McDowell, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026) |
| No fixed ranges | "Bid 10% of your budget" isn't one-size-fits-all; split FAAB into a **Management Budget** (fixed weekly allotment for streamers/maintenance, ~$10/wk per NFBC champion Rob Silver) and a **Maintenance/Luxury Budget** for real upgrades; use *your league's own historical winning bids* as price precedent | [Fantasy Index, Zola, Apr 10, 2024](https://fantasyindex.com/2024/04/10/analysis/the-art-of-faab) |

**Auction mechanics (how the bid actually wins):**

- **Odd-number bids.** Bidders cluster on round numbers; "$11 or $26 or $51 wins a shocking number of auctions for one extra dollar." [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026); also [4for4, Aug 27, 2025](https://www.4for4.com/2025/preseason/ultimate-guide-waiver-wire-faab-strategy-2025), [Fantasy Upside, Jun 22, 2026](https://fantasyupside.com/learn/how-faab-bidding-works), [FantasySP, Sep 9, 2025](https://www.fantasysp.com/news/nfl/3055414/fantasy-football-faab-guide-managing-budget) ("Price is Right technique"). Calculator Collection's variant: add $1–$3 to intended bids ($27 vs $25) to win the waiver-priority tiebreak. [Calculator Collection, Aug 10, 2026](https://www.calculatorcollection.org/en/articles/faab-waiver-wire-strategy/). Ties otherwise fall back to waiver priority / inverse standings — where a good team *loses* ties. [Fantasy Upside](https://fantasyupside.com/learn/how-faab-bidding-works)
- **Auction-theory basis.** In a first-price sealed-bid auction with n bidders, the Bayesian–Nash equilibrium is to bid **(n−1)/n × your value** — ~**90%** of true value at 10 teams, ~83% at 6. No mainstream fantasy source publishes the formula; it is the theoretical basis for bid shading. [Wikipedia – First-price sealed-bid auction, accessed 2026-09-18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-price_sealed-bid_auction); [Wikipedia – Bid shading, accessed 2026-09-18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bid_shading)
- **Bid against a known rival's budget.** If you can see one manager's remaining FAAB, "bid just $1 more than the competitor's remaining budget to guarantee you get him": "You don't need $200. You need $61." [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026); [4for4, Aug 27, 2025](https://www.4for4.com/2025/preseason/ultimate-guide-waiver-wire-faab-strategy-2025)
- **Published calculators (treat as one data point, not established methodology):** `bid = remaining budget × base% × need × urgency`, with base% stepping from 45% (top-tier value) down to 3%, need ×1.3 for a starting hole / ×0.7 for a speculative stash, urgency scaling ~1.5 late season because "unspent FAAB expires worthless" ([FantasyVerdicts FAAB calculator, undated](https://fantasyverdicts.com/faab-calculator)); and `bid = (projectedPts − currentPlayerPts) × remainingWeeks × need% × budget / 10000` — worked example: 5-pt/week upgrade × 7 weeks × 80% need on $60 remaining ≈ **$17** ([Calculator Collection – FAAB Budget Calculator, updated Sep 2026](https://www.calculatorcollection.org/en/calculators/fantasy-sports/fantasy-waiver-wire-priority/))
- **Market clusters to calibrate against:** when an RB takes over a job, "handcuff scramble" bids cluster **$15–$35** in competitive leagues ([Fantasy Strategy Guide – FAAB, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/faab-bidding-strategy)); an injury-to-availability news event can push a backup from "near-zero to 30–40% of a manager's total budget within 24 hours" ([Fantasy Waiver Wire – FAAB Bidding System, undated; secondary](https://fantasywaiverwire.com/faab-bidding-system))

**10-team format note:** No published heuristic the researchers found is explicitly keyed to 10-team leagues. The only 10-team-specific anchors: the (n−1)/n equilibrium above (~90% shading), and that with only 9 other managers the pool of unowned backups is *thinner* — per-player reaction speed is fine, but a specific stash is more likely already pre-claimed ([NovaPredict, Jun 10, 2026](https://novapredict.com/blog/fantasy-football-handcuffs)). FantasyDutchman's data was built for 12-team rosters (top-24 RB benchmark; top-20 RB is the comparable line for a 10-team league). [FantasyDutchman, 2025](https://thefantasydutchman.com/drafting-handcuff-rbs-valuable-or-just-a-myth/)

### 1.3 Add/drop criteria and picking the drop

**Classify the claim first — four buckets** ([Fantasy Strategy Guide – Waiver Wire Strategy, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/waiver-wire-strategy); 4for4 uses the same logic — "am I renting a week, or buying a role?" [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026)):

| Bucket | Definition | Resource rule |
|---|---|---|
| Replacement starter | Backup inheriting a *permanent* job | Highest value; "should command the most resources" (25–40% band) — [Fantasy Strategy Guide](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/waiver-wire-strategy); [Calculator Collection, Aug 10, 2026](https://www.calculatorcollection.org/en/articles/faab-waiver-wire-strategy/) |
| Handcuff | Value exists only if the specific starter ahead of him is injured; floor near zero | $1–$3 / $0–$2 band — [Fantasy Strategy Guide – FAAB](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/faab-bidding-strategy) |
| Streamer | 1–2 weeks of matchup value, then dropped; "consumes waiver priority or FAAB but delivers short-term point production" | No premium; let it clear to free agency when priority cost is high |
| Lottery ticket | Real upside but a second injury or a demotion of a healthy player must occur; "a longer thesis that warrants holding through a cold week or two" | Long hold; minimum bid |

**Drop-selection procedure (published, convergent):**

1. **Model the add-minus-drop net first, not the add in isolation.** FantasyPros' own assistant tooling does exactly this: it pulls your league's available players, ranks them by "expert rankings for the next week as well as rest of season (ROS)," then "compare[s] how much your team may improve (or worsen) based on potential drops from your active roster." [FantasyPros Support – "How does the Waiver Wire Assistant work?"](https://support.fantasypros.com/hc/en-us/articles/115001363868-How-does-the-Waiver-Wire-Assistant-work)
2. **Drop on role change, not a bad week.** "Before you drop a player, ask whether the underlying role has changed. If the snap count held steady and the target share held steady, the bad game was weather or game script, not a demotion. Hold. If the snap count fell, the role has changed — that is a real signal, not noise." [Scoutcast.ai, updated Jul 8, 2026](https://scoutcast.ai/blog/fantasy-football-waiver-wire-strategy/) The same source: "streaming — dropping a player after one bad week and picking up whatever is available — feels productive. It is usually a trap." Independent usage-first rule: "We don't want to overreact to Week 1 production, and we certainly don't want to overpay for it… *usage* — snaps, routes run — is a signal." [RotoWire – Waiver Wire Week 2, Sep 2026 (snippet-only)](https://www.rotowire.com/football/article/nfl-waiver-wire-high-stakes-deep-league-adds-ffpcnffc-134625)
3. **Case-specific drops:**
   - **Dropping a streamer** — the availability gate decides: "Candidates for streaming must be rostered in no more than 40% of Yahoo! fantasy leagues" ([4for4 – Kicker Streaming Week 3, 2025 season](https://www.4for4.com/2025/w3/fantasy-football-kicker-streaming-week-3-no-need-prater-fantasy-points)); ESPN's equivalent for defenses: "available in at least 75% of ESPN leagues that week" ([ESPN – D/ST road map, 2025 edition, Aug 25, 2025](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/46050621/2025-fantasy-football-streaming-defense-d-st-strategy-matchups))
   - **Dropping a depth player** — only if underlying snap/target share is flat ([Scoutcast](https://scoutcast.ai/blog/fantasy-football-waiver-wire-strategy/)); if the alternative drop is unavoidable, dropping the player you were cutting anyway on Sunday evening to grab a Monday-night handcuff is a positive-EV move even before the game ([4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026))
   - **Dropping a bye-matched backup** — only if the *non-bye* backup already covers the position adequately; the bye-matched one is insurance for exactly the week the starter can't play. [Fantasy Strategy Guide – Bye Week Management, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/bye-week-management)
   - **Sabotage-drop counter-signal:** "When a struggling contender cuts a big name to fill a need, somebody in your league is going to add that name on reputation alone and drop somebody genuinely useful to do it. That dropped player? He's your real target." [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026)
4. **Reachability filter:** FantasyPros' weekly wire column only discusses "Players chosen based upon having early-week consensus roster percent below 50%" — a published use of ownership % as a reachability gate, not a value signal. [FantasyPros – Week 2 2026 waiver wire rankings, 2026-09 (snippet-only; page JS-blocked)](https://www.fantasypros.com/2026/09/fantasy-football-waiver-wire-rankings-pickups-week-2-2026/)

### 1.4 Handcuffs

- **Definition.** A backup whose value is *contingent*: near-zero projected points while the starter is healthy, a startable workload if the starter misses time. Almost always an RB: "RBs absorb more contact… their production is driven by opportunity more than talent. A backup wide receiver rarely inherits a full target share the same way." [NovaPredict, Jun 10, 2026](https://novapredict.com/blog/fantasy-football-handcuffs); [Fantasy Strategy Guide, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/waiver-wire-strategy)
- **Two screening questions (consensus checklist):** (1) **Is the contingent role concentrated?** — the backup inherits near-complete workload (workhorse backfield, **70–75%+ of touches**; [Fantasy Waiver Wire, undated; secondary](https://fantasywaiverwire.com/handcuff-players-waiver-wire/) cites "a true bell-cow who accounts for 75%+ of backfield touches"); (2) **Is the path clear?** — unambiguous #2, "the shortest possible chain between 'starter is out' and 'this player gets the ball'" ([NovaPredict, Jun 10, 2026](https://novapredict.com/blog/fantasy-football-handcuffs)). Qualitative tiering: rank by (a) whether the starter's body/usage makes a miss plausible (Barkley: "482 touches over the regular season and playoffs last season"), (b) whether the backup is the *clear* next man or a committee member, and (c) the quality of the offense he would inherit ("The Eagles offense, led by one of the best offensive lines in the league, will elevate Shipley's fantasy ceiling if he gets a chance"). [SB Nation – Chet Gresham, "Top 10 running back handcuffs for 2025," Aug 22, 2025](https://www.sbnation.com/fantasy/24480975/running-back-handcuffs-fantasy-football-2025-montgomery-shipley-allgeier-charbonnet-isaac-guerendo-ray-davis)
- **Data-backed base rates** ([FantasyDutchman – "Drafting Handcuff RBs: Valuable or Just a Myth?", 2025; five seasons of data, 2020–2024](https://thefantasydutchman.com/drafting-handcuff-rbs-valuable-or-just-a-myth/)):
  - **≥33% of top-24 RB1s miss 3+ games** to injury in a season — the base rate that makes handcuffs worth considering.
  - Only **23% of drafted handcuffs** (top-24 starter's backup, drafted rounds 10–15) delivered 2+ startable (10+) weeks tied to the starter being out; **59% were flat misses**; 18% gave exactly one viable week.
  - When the RB1 *is* out, handcuffs posted RB1-level points in 2023–24 (rising trend; ~30% of their weeks 10+ startable vs ~20% with the starter in).
  - **Opportunity cost:** late-round (rounds 10–15) WRs outscored late-round RBs by ~**18 fantasy points/season**; 18 of 26 late-round 200+-point players were WRs, only 8 RBs.
  - **His rules:** only handcuff injury-prone RB1s; never reach for healthy-stud handcuffs; prefer late-round WR upside otherwise; grab insurance on waivers for **$0–2** instead of a mid-round pick; recheck weekly.
- **Claim-timing rules:**
  - *Before the injury:* $0–2 stashes at most — "handcuff is a one or two dollar flier, not a real bid" ([NovaPredict, Jun 10, 2026](https://novapredict.com/blog/fantasy-football-handcuffs)); "handcuffs for players with low injury probability or weak backup track records" are minimum-bid scenarios ([Fantasy Strategy Guide – FAAB, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/faab-bidding-strategy)).
  - *After the injury:* this is a season-changing bid — the 25–40% band ([Calculator Collection, Aug 10, 2026](https://www.calculatorcollection.org/en/articles/faab-waiver-wire-strategy/)) with the $15–35 cluster benchmark ([Fantasy Strategy Guide – FAAB](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/faab-bidding-strategy)) and the worked RB math: 15 PPR/wk starter vs 7 PPR bench = 8 pts × 12 weeks = 96 fantasy points, "worth a big bid" ([Fantasy Upside, Jun 22, 2026](https://fantasyupside.com/learn/how-faab-bidding-works)).
- **10-team / short-bench logic.** "In shallow leagues with short benches, most handcuffs are not worth the spot… in 10-team leagues with a short bench, the waiver wire is deep enough to react to injuries when they happen. Handcuffs matter most in deep leagues where replacement players are scarce." [NovaPredict, Jun 10, 2026](https://novapredict.com/blog/fantasy-football-handcuffs). 4for4: "I would also not bother drafting backup quarterbacks or tight ends, **especially in smaller leagues**" — use the bench spot for high-upside skill players and mid-season streamers. [4for4, Rajaram, Aug 27, 2025](https://www.4for4.com/2025/preseason/ultimate-guide-waiver-wire-faab-strategy-2025)
- **The "dark side" variant (opponent's-starter handcuffs):** draft/claim backups to *other managers'* RB1s — a post-injury Allgeier *combined with your own Saquon* is a superteam move ([FantasySP – Chmyz, Jul 28, 2025](https://www.fantasysp.com/news/nfl/3055123/fantasy-football-draft-strategy-what-is)); in his 2025 rankings Charbonnet is the league's top handcuff (**21.9 pts/game in six games** with Walker out in 2024), and backs consolidating **70%+ of touches** are "likely to be fantasy-relevant" while ~65% may not. [FantasySP, Jul 31, 2025](https://www.fantasysp.com/news/nfl/3055148/2025-fantasy-football-running-back-handcuff)
- **Format condition:** in shallower formats (14–17 total roster spots), preemptive own-stud handcuffing is "usually not a great strategy." [FantasyPros – "Should You Handcuff?", 2025-07 (snippet-only; page JS-blocked)](https://www.fantasypros.com/2025/07/should-you-handcuff-in-fantasy-football-strategy-advice/) Full disagreement in §2.6.

### 1.5 Streaming K and DEF

**Kickers.**

- **Consensus rule: stream, and spend near zero.** "Streaming is your best bet in redraft leagues" — the kicker you draft "is of very little significance other than your possible Week 1 guy," and drafting before the final round risks missing a positional player who produced starter numbers. [4for4 – "Debunking the Randomness of Kickers," May 9, 2025](https://www.4for4.com/2025/preseason/debunking-randomness-kickers-fantasy-football). The position is the least predictable in fantasy — no position has a lower correlation between draft position and final rank, week-to-week scoring, or projection and actuals — and "a dozen or more starting kickers are sitting around on waivers at any given time." [Footballguys – "Rent-a-Kicker: Week 2," Adam Harstad, Sep 15, 2026](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2026-rent-a-kicker-week02)
- **Weekly criteria:** rank the *situation*, ignore the kicker's own talent — indoor/dome venue, high projected game total / implied team total, an offense that moves the chains and stalls in field-goal range. [Footballguys, Sep 15, 2026](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2026-rent-a-kicker-week02); SI's Week 2 2026 top-5 frames each pick on exactly these: a 51.5-point game O/U, a 29.5-point implied team total, SoFi's "controlled dome environment," a 49.5-point projected total. [SI – Week 2 Kicker Streamers, Sep 15, 2026](https://www.si.com/onsi/fantasy/nfl/fantasy-football-week-2-kicker-streamers-top-5-waiver-wire-pickups)
- **Availability gate — two published numbers, note the conflict:** only recommend Ks available in **≥50% of leagues** (Yahoo roster data) — [Footballguys, Sep 15, 2026](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2026-rent-a-kicker-week02) — vs. streaming candidates "rostered in **no more than 40%** of Yahoo! fantasy leagues" — [4for4, Week 3 2025](https://www.4for4.com/2025/w3/fantasy-football-kicker-streaming-week-3-no-need-prater-fantasy-points). An assistant should surface both rather than flatten them.
- **Cost rule:** "Defenses and kickers should cost a dollar or nothing, always." [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026). Budget math: streaming 3 players/week at $5 = $15/wk = $120 over 8 weeks — exceeds a $100 budget; streaming is only viable at honest $1–2 accounting per pickup. [Fantasy Waiver Wire, undated; secondary](https://fantasywaiverwire.com/faab-bidding-system); [FantasySP, Sep 9, 2025](https://www.fantasysp.com/news/nfl/3055414/fantasy-football-faab-guide-managing-budget)
- **Hold exceptions exist only at the edges:** only two kickers finished top-10 in each of the prior two seasons (Aubrey, Dicker) and they "seem to be the only ones worth a speculative pick"; otherwise stream; if you won't burn a waiver pick early, grab a kicker with a **late bye week** (the piece lists a Week-10-by-or-later cutoff); for the fantasy playoffs, target **dome kickers** (Aubrey, Dicker, Bates) and pivot to one when your current kicker hits his bye. [USA TODAY – "The ultimate fantasy football strategy for picking a kicker," Sep 2, 2025](https://www.usatoday.com/story/sports/fantasy/football/2025/09/02/fantasy-football-strategy-streaming-kickers/85927174007/)
- **Low-authority numerics — flagged: single undated SEO source, not corroborated by the major outlets, which use the same variables qualitatively:** stream when the rostered kicker's team has an implied total below ~19 pts, or the waiver option's team is above ~27 pts with dome/favorable weather; hold when the projected-total difference is under ~6 pts; treat 2 straight missed FGs (or 3+ in 6 weeks) as a real slump signal. [fantasystartsit.com, undated](https://fantasystartsit.com/kicker-start-sit-strategy)
- **Second kicker:** generally a wasted bench spot — "one kicker and a flexible streaming strategy is the more efficient choice"; exceptions are very deep leagues with thin waiver wires, best-ball, or custom long-FG scoring; **replace during the bye instead of stashing a second**. [todayquestions.com, undated (low authority)](https://www.todayquestions.com/should-i-have-two-kickers-in-fantasy-football/); [footballnationusa.com, undated](https://www.footballnationusa.com/post/how-many-kickers-draft-fantasy-football-2026)

**D/ST.**

- **Consensus rule: stream matchups, not talent — and the data strongly supports it.** ESPN's 2025 backtest: in 2024, the top five scoring D/STs had average ADPs of **18.2 at position (194.0 overall)** — the production leaders were being drafted around the 4th round — yet streaming the Browns' *opponent* matchups would have yielded **203 points** vs only 166 for the league-leading Broncos; a D/ST that faced the Tennessee Titans every week would have totaled **193**; even a conservative weekly "most-widely-available defense facing the best matchup" strategy ("available in at least 75% of ESPN leagues that week") totaled **146 — exceeded by only two individual D/STs in all of 2024.** [ESPN – D/ST road map, 2025 edition, Aug 25, 2025](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/46050621/2025-fantasy-football-streaming-defense-d-st-strategy-matchups) *(The researchers' bylines for this column conflict — one cites Mike Clay, the other Tristan H. Cockcroft; it is cited here by outlet and edition only.)*
- ESPN's 2026 edition gives the cleanest numeric framing: over the past three seasons, **top-five scoring D/STs averaged 9.6 fantasy points/game; the D/STs facing the five most favorable matchups averaged 10.4** — the matchup group beat the talent group by at least 1 point/game in each of the past four seasons. His recommended draft-day multi-week targets all "have been gone undrafted in at least 50% of ESPN drafts." [ESPN – D/ST road map, 2026 edition, ~Sep 2, 2026](https://www.espn.com.au/fantasy/football/story/_/id/49798496/2026-fantasy-football-d-st-road-map-early-season)
- **Ownership threshold for D/STs:** in the week-by-week rankings, **defenses rostered in fewer than 50% of ESPN leagues are the optimal streaming targets; those over 50% are effectively "to avoid."** [ESPN 2026, ~Sep 2, 2026](https://www.espn.com.au/fantasy/football/story/_/id/49798496/2026-fantasy-football-d-st-road-map-early-season). (The 2025 edition expresses the same streamable set as an availability frame — "available in at least 75% of ESPN leagues" — [ESPN 2025](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/46050621/2025-fantasy-football-streaming-defense-d-st-strategy-matchups).)
- **Cadence rule:** the NFL "changes swiftly and dramatically" within about four weeks (coaching, scheme, personnel, injuries), so the road map is **refreshed after Weeks 4, 8, and 12** — don't lock a hold on a pre-season read. [ESPN 2025, Aug 25, 2025](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/46050621/2025-fantasy-football-streaming-defense-d-st-strategy-matchups). FantasyPros runs a weekly "Let's Stream Defenses" column (Jacob Herlin; Week 4 2025 edition, [Sep 24, 2025, snippet-only](https://www.fantasypros.com/2025/09/lets-stream-defenses-week-4-fantasy-football-picks/)) with the game-theory caveat that "your league context matters" if you're the only one streaming.
- **Early-season target pattern:** stream *against* bad-offense teams — in 2024 the opponent teams Cleveland (**200 pts allowed to D/ST**), Tennessee (**186**), Las Vegas (**168**), and NY Giants (**162**) each produced more fantasy points than every actual D/ST except Denver; the five most-drafted D/STs (SF, BAL, NYJ, DAL, CLE) had "utterly unrelated" results to the top scorers (DEN 175, MIN 156, PIT 145, GB 144). [Football Absurdity – Jeff Krisko, Aug 20, 2025](https://footballabsurdity.com/2025/08/20/fantasy-football-2025-to-stream-d-st-or-not-to-stream-d-st/). Live example, Week 2 2026: Panthers D/ST at **3% owned**, Bucs at **20%**. [Football Absurdity – Waleed Ismail, Sep 17, 2026](https://footballabsurdity.com/2026/09/17/week-2-fantasy-football-defensive-streamers-its-simple-we-kill-the-bad-man/)
- **Cost rule:** "Never more than $2 on a defense, any week, any matchup, no exceptions." [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026); "Bid $1–2 max. Always." [Fantasy Upside, Jun 22, 2026](https://fantasyupside.com/learn/how-faab-bidding-works)
- **PPR note for this league:** PPR scoring adds points only for receptions — the difference between PPR and standard is exclusively at QB/RB/WR/TE; kicker and team-defense categories are identical across scoring formats, and snap counts matter for individual defensive players, not for a D/ST unit. **PPR changes nothing about K/DEF logic; the prompt should not condition K/DEF advice on PPR.** [ESPN Fan Support – Scoring Formats, undated official doc](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360003914032-Scoring-Formats)

### 1.6 Bye-week planning

- **Distribution first:** spread key starters across different bye weeks so no single week eliminates more than one irreplaceable starter; in the middle draft rounds, "the bye week becomes a meaningful tiebreaker" when player quality is roughly equal. [Fantasy Strategy Guide – Bye Week Management, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/bye-week-management)
- **Map conflicts before the season, not in-season:** "Open your league schedule and mark every week in which a starting quarterback, tight end, or defense is unavailable. Then mark overlaps at running back and wide receiver. The worst bye-week mistake is solving one position while accidentally creating a second." [SharkSnip – Bye-Week Streamer Stacks 2026, 2026 (snippet-only)](https://sharksnip.com/blog/fantasy-2026-bye-week-streamer-stack-guide)
- **Lead time:** "Target the waiver wire proactively — add streaming options **1–2 weeks before the bye hits**, not during the conflict week when roster availability is thinnest." [Fantasy Strategy Guide, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/bye-week-management)
- **Tier the response by player importance, not position:** Tier 1 (top-5 at position) bye = mandatory coverage, acquire or trade before the conflict week; Tier 2 (solid starter, not irreplaceable) = situational — use an existing same-position backup if adequate, otherwise a temporary waiver add; Tier 3 (streamer/depth) = "the bye week is essentially the same decision already being made." [Fantasy Strategy Guide](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/bye-week-management)
- **2-man slot mechanics (RB/RB, WR/WR, FLEX overlap):** a *double-stacked bye* (two rostered players sharing a bye, e.g., a stacked RB1+handcuff or a QB+TE from the same NFL team) creates a **two-slot deficit in one week that "is rarely recoverable through waiver wire adds alone"** in a short-bench format — draft-time bye distribution is the primary control; 1–2 weeks of advance waiver adds plus a post-week trade of bye-depth for a longer-term asset is the fallback. [Fantasy Strategy Guide](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/bye-week-management)
- **NFL schedule fact that shapes all of this:** every NFL team gets exactly one bye, typically Weeks 5–14, and they cluster — "six teams have a Week 12 bye, while only two are on bye in Week 9. There is going to be more waiver wire competition during Week 12 than Week 9." [Sleeper Blog, Aug 31, 2024](https://sleeper.com/blog/how-do-waivers-work-in-fantasy-football/) — directly relevant where a clustered bye week (3 of the 10 NFL teams across your league sharing a bye) produces the worst 2-man-slot crunch.
- **Short-bench (10-team, 5-bench) consequence:** "Fewer reserve spots make weekly waivers more important, lower the payoff of slow-developing stashes, and put more pressure on managers to construct a killer starting lineup on draft day. Early picks should strengthen the lineup first." [Athlon Sports – How to Win in Short-Bench Leagues (snippet-only; page 403 on fetch)](https://athlonsports.com/fantasy/fantasy-football-strategy-shallow-bench-leagues)
- **Counter-position (surface to the user; §2.8):** "The roster you draft is not the roster you will actually be managing when most bye weeks arrive. The two players you carefully put on different bye weeks in August may not even both be on your roster by the time those weeks get here… there is a real cost to… avoiding a scheduling problem for one week." [Fantasy Football Blueprint, Aug 6, 2026 (snippet-only)](https://www.fantasyfootballblueprint.com/2026-08-06/bye-weeks-dont-matter-as-you-think/)
- **K/DEF byes:** pick your stopgap K for a *late* bye week and pre-plan the swap to a dome kicker at the bye for the playoff run — the bye is "the most common streaming trigger" at K. [USA TODAY, Sep 2, 2025](https://www.usatoday.com/story/sports/fantasy/football/2025/09/02/fantasy-football-strategy-streaming-kickers/85927174007/) D/ST byes are built directly into hold/stream calls (2025: the Cardinals were "locked in until their Week 8 bye"; the Ravens "don't get easier until after their Week 7 bye") — [ESPN 2025, Aug 25, 2025](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/46050621/2025-fantasy-football-streaming-defense-d-st-strategy-matchups). In a single-DEF-slot league with no DEF bench, a bye is a hard 0 unless you've already rotated in a different defense.

### 1.7 Interpreting trending adds and ownership %

**What the numbers actually are (platform documentation, not commentary):**

- **Sleeper trending adds/drops.** Green/red arrows mean the player "has been either added or dropped by many teams across Sleeper in the last few days." [Sleeper Support – "League Legend," Jun 27, 2022](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/4584482-league-legend). The underlying data is a public API — `GET /v1/players/nfl/trending/{ADD|DROP}?lookback_hours={h}&limit={n}` — i.e., **the trending number is literally a change in roster count over a configurable lookback window (in hours, default 24h): not a forecast, a projection, or an editor's judgment.** [Sleeper API client docs – Player endpoints](https://sleeper-api-client.readthedocs.io/en/latest/endpoints/player.html); [docs.sleeper.com, undated official doc](https://docs.sleeper.com/). Magnitude check from the live embed: post-Week 1 2026, the top RB showed **+222,735 adds** in a 24-hour window. [Sleeper live trending embed, accessed 2026-09-18](https://sleeper.com/embed/players/nfl/trending/add?limit=10&lookback_hours=24); third-party dashboards expose the same data with explicit "last 48 hours" windows. [Sleeper Tiers, last updated 2026-08-27](https://sleepertiers.com/trending)
- **Ownership / roster %** is a point-in-time census: "the number of fantasy leagues in which a player is on a roster divided by the total number of fantasy leagues" — "how the public views a player" ([DNA of Sports, undated](https://www.dnaofsports.com/football/what-does-percent-rostered-mean-in-fantasy-football/)); it "measures popularity and availability — not talent, projected points or the percentage of managers who should start him" ([Football Nation USA, undated; secondary](https://www.footballnationusa.com/post/what-does-roster-percentage-mean-fantasy-football)). On major platforms it refreshes "every few hours" during NFL in-season weeks, with some updates tied to waiver-claim processing. [Fantasy Player Database, undated; secondary — the most detailed written treatment of this number's failure modes](https://fantasyplayerdatabase.com/player-ownership-percentages)
- **Known platform quirks:** users report Sleeper roster % can disagree between a team page and the same player's card, with IDP ownership figures in particular badly off. [Sleeper forum thread, undated](https://sleeper.com/topic/170000000000000002/926664911952924672). Platform trending data is *not* league-specific. [league-loom tool doc, accessed 2026-09-18](https://glama.ai/mcp/connectors/com.leagueloom/league-loom/tools/fantasy_get_trending_players)

**Published numeric benchmarks:**

|---|---|---|

| **40–60%** | "The most analytically interesting band… enough managers see upside to roster him, enough don't that he's available in real leagues" | same |

| **<50% consensus rostered** | FantasyPros' scoping filter for which wire players its column even discusses | [FantasyPros, Week 2 2026, 2026-09 (snippet-only)](https://www.fantasypros.com/2026/09/fantasy-football-waiver-wire-rankings-pickups-week-2-2026/) |

**Documented failure modes of these signals:**

1. **Recency / hype chasing** — "A player spiking from 3% owned to 45% owned in 24 hours may have already been the right add 12 hours earlier — when the tape showed a target share jump — not when the box score confirmed it." [Fantasy Strategy Guide – Waiver Wire, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/waiver-wire-strategy). Named pattern: "We are bidding on a highlight and treating it as a projection." [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026). Quantified leak: "Panic-buying after breakout games is one of the most common FAAB leaks… managers routinely spend $20–$40 on these players only to drop them three weeks later." [Fantasy Strategy Guide – FAAB, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/faab-bidding-strategy). Standing editorial countermeasures exist precisely for this: [CBS Sports – Week 1 2026 overreactions, Sep 13, 2026](https://www.cbssports.com/fantasy/football/news/week-1-fantasy-football-recap-week-2-waiver-wire/); [ESPN – "Bad game or bad sign," Sep 16, 2026](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/49957386/fantasy-football-rankings-value-bad-game-bad-sign)
2. **Monday spike = the scoreboard.** "On the Monday after Week 1 it mostly shows one thing: who scored" — sort adds into *roles* vs *one-week spikes* rather than ranking by add count. [Parlae – Week 2 waiver wire guide, 2026 (snippet-only)](https://parlae.io/fantasy/guides/week-2-waiver-wire-roles-versus-spikes). Quantified: Must Be Sunday's 10-season study (2016–2025, 30 post-Week-1 waiver targets) found eventual **league winners averaged 19.2 PPR points in Week 1 and eventual busts averaged 19.1** — indistinguishable; not one of the three league winners finished top-5 at his position in Week 1, while the loudest performances (John Ross 34.8, Nyheim Hines 27.3, Malcolm Brown 26.0) led nowhere. The separating variable is *opportunity* (touch count, target share, injury opening), not the box score — e.g., Malcolm Brown 2020 (21 touches but Akers started with 15 of his own → bust) vs Puka Nacua 2023 (15 targets, 44.1% target share, Kupp out ≥4 weeks → one of the decade's defining pickups). [Must Be Sunday, Sep 8, 2026](https://mustbesunday.com/2026/09/08/stop-chasing-week-1-points/); [Must Be Sunday, Sep 15, 2026 — its Week 2 2026 application: predict the market's most-aggressive adds (Vele, Douglas, Black) *separately* from what the role evidence says to add (Washington — the least exciting Week 1 line but 98% snap share and 29.6% target share — gets claimed first; Douglas, the box-score darling, gets benched)](https://mustbesunday.com/2026/09/15/week-2-waiver-wire-who-everyone-will-add-and-who-they-should/)
3. **Ownership is a census, not a vote.** "Ownership percentage is one of the most misread numbers in fantasy sports — treated as a confidence vote when it's actually a census." A post-waiver Monday-morning snapshot "looks dramatically different from the Sunday night one" and "reflects processed transactions, not live roster decisions"; streaming formats produce a "sawtooth" ownership pattern that spikes pre-matchup and drops post — the number is mechanically inflated right around when a manager is most tempted to act. [Fantasy Player Database, undated; secondary](https://fantasyplayerdatabase.com/player-ownership-percentages)
4. **News-event ownership jumps are transaction artifacts:** "An injury to a starter can push a backup from 4% owned to 48% owned inside 90 minutes of a beat reporter's tweet" — an ownership jump measures that *others already claimed the player*, i.e., the edge closing. [Fantasy Player Database, undated; secondary](https://fantasyplayerdatabase.com/player-ownership-percentages)
5. **Platform-population bias:** "A player sitting at 67% owned on ESPN may sit at 41% owned on a niche dynasty platform — not because managers disagree on the player's talent, but because the populations are structurally different. Casual leagues skew toward name recognition; deeper leagues skew toward current situational value." Never compare or blend ownership numbers across platforms as if they were comparable populations. [Fantasy Player Database, undated; secondary](https://fantasyplayerdatabase.com/player-ownership-percentages)
6. **Staleness:** treat ownership cautiously when the timestamp is **>48 hours old in a news-heavy week**, or "the player's situation has changed but ownership hasn't updated yet — a window where the number is technically accurate and practically wrong simultaneously." [Fantasy Player Database](https://fantasyplayerdatabase.com/player-ownership-percentages)
7. **DFS-logic transfer is a category error:** "In season-long formats, contrarian ownership has no intrinsic value; what matters is whether the player scores points." [Fantasy Player Database](https://fantasyplayerdatabase.com/player-ownership-percentages)
8. **Sabotage-drop distortion:** a trending-DROP spike can be a *wrong* value signal when a struggling contender cuts a big name — the drop count spikes because a roster clogger is being cut, not because the dropped player was bad. [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026)
9. **Thin samples / extreme environments:** pre-season reads are limited to four weeks ("we tend to have a far greater feel for a defense's true talent level through four weeks than at its onset" — [ESPN 2025](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/46050621/2025-fantasy-football-streaming-defense-d-st-strategy-matchups)); extreme single-game environments (New Orleans' 56-pass overtime Week 1 game) "should not be projected forward" even when the underlying role is real. [Must Be Sunday, Sep 15, 2026](https://mustbesunday.com/2026/09/15/week-2-waiver-wire-who-everyone-will-add-and-who-they-should/)

**The architecture consensus for the assistant:** crowd data (trending adds, ownership %) supplies *scarcity / bidding context*; an independent value model (role, usage, projection) supplies *value* — "add/drop counts come from Sleeper… we join them to our own values; the trending numbers are theirs." [FantasyVerdicts – Live Add and Drop Trends, live page accessed 2026-09-18](https://fantasyverdicts.com/trending); [sleeper-mcp trending tool description, undated](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/bealmot/sleeper-mcp/tools/trending) — "a crowd signal, not an analytical one — tells you who is being claimed, which is often as useful for knowing what you'll have to bid against."

## 2. Where experts disagree

Each disagreement is named with both positions and both sources. These are structural disagreements — the assistant should surface both to the user, not flatten them.

**2.1 Aggressive vs. patient on rolling waivers (the most contested question).**

- *Aggressive ("sniping is dead"):* in a priority league, burn priority on real value when it surfaces; the discipline lives in pre-set rules, not in waiting — "FAAB depreciates whether you use it or not… be aggressive early. If a legitimate every-week starter surfaces in Week 2 and it costs 40% to land him, spend the 40%." The "Sunday Night Shop" tactic (buying next week's target in this week's window) is an explicit anti-sniping play: "The price is only high because all twelve of you showed up at once." [4for4, McDowell, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026)
- *Patient (priority as ammo):* "Using waiver priority early in the season for a medium-upside player means entering Week 8 at the back of the queue when a true RB1 vacancy opens. Holding priority carries an opportunity cost measured in missed medium-upside adds, but it builds optionality for catastrophic need." Its checklist tells priority-league managers to "confirm waiver position before committing. If the need is a streamer and the priority cost is high, evaluate whether free-agent alternatives exist post-processing" — i.e., deliberately let streamers clear to free agency. [Fantasy Strategy Guide – Waiver Wire Strategy, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/waiver-wire-strategy)
- **Point of agreement (the safest rule to hard-code):** never spend full premium on a *streamer* — both camps agree.

**2.2 FAAB vs. rolling as league format.**

- *FAAB converts:* rolling structurally starves everyone except the top priority — "other managers have very little chance at the best waiver target unless the top priority decides to pass" — FAAB recommended for competitive leagues. [FFwrapped Blog – FAAB vs Rolling Waivers, undated; secondary](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/faab-vs-rolling-waivers). Stronger still: "I'll die on this hill: every league should run FAAB, because it removes the two unrelated variables from the waiver process, which are your record and your daily work schedule." [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026)
- *Rolling is fine:* FFwrapped concedes rolling is "perfectly fine" for casual leagues. [FFwrapped](https://blog.ffwrapped.com/posts/faab-vs-rolling-waivers) Practical output for this user's hybrid league: the channel-decision rule (§1.1).

**2.3 When to spend FAAB (timing) — four incompatible published stances, plus a fifth that rejects the framing:**

- *Aggressive early:* 75–80% of the entire budget early on a legitimate top-10 player [4for4, Rajaram, Aug 27, 2025](https://www.4for4.com/2025/preseason/ultimate-guide-waiver-wire-faab-strategy-2025); 40% in Week 2 for a legitimate starter [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026); 30–50% of budget by Week 5, with 90% remaining past Week 5 = missed pickups [Calculator Collection, Aug 10, 2026](https://www.calculatorcollection.org/en/articles/faab-waiver-wire-strategy/)
- *Mid-season concentration:* "the most valuable waiver wire opportunities tend to arrive in Weeks 5–10… Managers who spent aggressively in September have nothing left." [Scoutcast.ai, updated Jul 8, 2026](https://scoutcast.ai/blog/fantasy-football-waiver-wire-strategy/)
- *Midpoint hold:* retain 30–40% past the season midpoint. [Fantasy Strategy Guide – FAAB, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/faab-bidding-strategy)
- *Small reserve:* spend ~25% per quarter, save only 5–10% for the playoffs. [Fantasy Upside, Jun 22, 2026](https://fantasyupside.com/learn/how-faab-bidding-works)
- *Reject fixed ranges:* "bid 10% of your budget" isn't one-size-fits-all — split into a weekly Management Budget and a Maintenance/Luxury Budget, and use the league's own historical winning bids as the price precedent. [Fantasy Index, Zola, Apr 10, 2024](https://fantasyindex.com/2024/04/10/analysis/the-art-of-faab) The 4for4 2025 and 2026 guides also note the spend-it-all end-state ("unspent FAAB expires worthless"), while Scoutcast and Fantasy Strategy Guide are the clearest counterweight. The disagreement is real; the assistant should pick based on (a) league channel and (b) week of season, and state which stance it is applying.

**2.4 Kicker hold vs. stream.**

- *Stream:* "streaming is your best bet in redraft leagues" [4for4, May 9, 2025](https://www.4for4.com/2025/preseason/debunking-randomness-kickers-fantasy-football); rank situations, ignore kicker talent [Footballguys, Sep 15, 2026](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2026-rent-a-kicker-week02)
- *Conditional hold:* Subvertadown's simulation analysis (originally May 22, 2022, **updated Sept 10, 2026**) is the quantitative key: "streaming can be at least as viable as holding (on average) depending on what your league-mates do and the quality of the kicker ranking source"; "being able to say you held the top kicker is mostly hindsight and luck"; and the critical caveat — **"If everyone's doing it, then holding a top kicker can be better."** Under a mixed 50/50 league assumption, matchup-streaming beats top-scorer-chasing on average; against uniformly top-scorer-chasing league-mates, being the sole streamer wins by a wide margin. So the expert position is *conditional*: stream wins in an uncoordinated league, hold (a high-volume/dome kicker) wins in a fully informed one. [Subvertadown – "Analysis of Holding vs. Streaming Kickers"](https://subvertadown.com/article/analysis-of-holding-vs-streaming-kickers)
- *Named hold exceptions:* only Aubrey and Dicker finished top-10 in each of the prior two seasons and "seem to be the only ones worth a speculative pick." [USA TODAY, Sep 2, 2025](https://www.usatoday.com/story/sports/fantasy/football/2025/09/02/fantasy-football-strategy-streaming-kickers/85927174007/)

**2.5 DEF hold vs. stream.**

- *Stream:* the 2025 backtest and 9.6 vs 10.4 pts/week data above (matchup group beats talent group four straight seasons) [ESPN 2025](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/46050621/2025-fantasy-football-streaming-defense-d-st-strategy-matchups); [ESPN 2026](https://www.espn.com.au/fantasy/football/story/_/id/49798496/2026-fantasy-football-d-st-road-map-early-season); stream against bad offenses [Football Absurdity, Aug 20, 2025](https://footballabsurdity.com/2025/08/20/fantasy-football-2025-to-stream-d-st-or-not-to-stream-d-st/)
- *Hold (schedule-elite units):* ESPN's own road maps concede holds are real — the 2025 Texans were the #1 drafted D/ST *and* the #1 scorer yet had the roughest September (Bills, Bengals, Cowboys), an explicit caution for knockout formats where one bad month is fatal [ESPN 2026, ~Sep 2, 2026](https://www.espn.com.au/fantasy/football/story/_/id/49798496/2026-fantasy-football-d-st-road-map-early-season); the 2025 Ravens were a "borderline chance" of a locked-in D/ST despite a brutal September because their long-term positional schedule was the season's best [ESPN 2025, Aug 25, 2025](https://www.espn.com/fantasy/football/story/_/id/46050621/2025-fantasy-football-streaming-defense-d-st-strategy-matchups). For this league (6 teams make playoffs from Week 15): streaming maximizes weekly points, but an elite D/ST that keeps scoring in Weeks 15–17 can be a hold worth its premium — the experts frame this as a *format* decision, not a talent decision.

**2.6 Preemptive own-starter handcuffing (draft time).**

- *Position A — against:* [FantasyDutchman, 2025](https://thefantasydutchman.com/drafting-handcuff-rbs-valuable-or-just-a-myth/) (23% payoff rate; "for most fantasy team builds, your bench spots are better used for standalone upside"); [FantasySP – Chmyz, Jul 28, 2025](https://www.fantasysp.com/news/nfl/3055123/fantasy-football-draft-strategy-what-is) (own-stud handcuffing "is, at its core, a risk-minimization strategy… a mistake… handcuffing your early-round backs is drafting scared"); [4for4, Rajaram, Aug 27, 2025](https://www.4for4.com/2025/preseason/ultimate-guide-waiver-wire-faab-strategy-2025) ("not bother drafting backup quarterbacks or tight ends, especially in smaller leagues"); [FantasyPros, 2025-07 (snippet-only)](https://www.fantasypros.com/2025/07/should-you-handcuff-in-fantasy-football-strategy-advice/) (in 14–17-spot formats "usually not a great strategy")
- *Position B — for (with conditions):* the same Chmyz article's "dark side" — draft backups to *other managers'* RB1s [FantasySP, Jul 28, 2025](https://www.fantasysp.com/news/nfl/3055123/fantasy-football-draft-strategy-what-is); [FantasyLabs – Sean Koerner, Aug 31, 2025](https://www.fantasylabs.com/articles/2025-running-back-handcuff-rankings-fantasy-football-expert-reveals-rb-upside-ratings-sleepers/) (late-round contingent RBs are his main upside source, priced in a "Starter Out" column); [NovaPredict, Jun 10, 2026](https://novapredict.com/blog/fantasy-football-handcuffs) (own-stud handcuffing "is usually worth it when the backup clears the two questions," but draft *other teams'* handcuffs "like a lottery ticket: last few rounds only")
- *Non-RB handcuffing:* near-unimal that it's inoptimal in redraft — backup WRs rarely inherit full target share, backup TEs are thin, backup QBs "burn a hole in your bench for 17 weeks." Exception: deep rosters/dynasty where a bench slot is cheap. [FantasySP, Jul 28, 2025](https://www.fantasysp.com/news/nfl/3055123/fantasy-football-draft-strategy-what-is); [NovaPredict, Jun 10, 2026](https://novapredict.com/blog/fantasy-football-handcuffs)

**2.7 Trending adds: value signal or scarcity signal.**

- *Value-signal use:* rejected — Must Be Sunday's 10-year study (winners 19.2 vs busts 19.1 Week 1 PPR), [Sep 8, 2026](https://mustbesunday.com/2026/09/08/stop-chasing-week-1-points/); RotoWire's "don't overreact… usage is a signal" [Sep 2026](https://www.rotowire.com/football/article/nfl-waiver-wire-high-stakes-deep-league-adds-ffpcnffc-134625); CBS's overreaction column [Sep 13, 2026](https://www.cbssports.com/fantasy/football/news/week-1-fantasy-football-recap-week-2-waiver-wire/); Monday adds ≈ "who scored" [parlae.io, 2026](https://parlae.io/fantasy/guides/week-2-waiver-wire-roles-versus-spikes)
- *Scarcity/bidding-context use:* endorsed — "a crowd signal… as useful for knowing what you will have to bid against" [sleeper-mcp tool description](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/bealmot/sleeper-mcp/tools/trending); the join-crowd-data-to-own-values architecture [FantasyVerdicts, accessed 2026-09-18](https://fantasyverdicts.com/trending). The published resolution is not either/or: use trending/ownership for *availability and bidding context*, decide value on role/usage.

**2.8 How much weight to give bye weeks.**

- *Distribution-first:* spread byes, tier responses, double-stacked byes rarely recoverable [Fantasy Strategy Guide, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/bye-week-management); pre-map overlaps [SharkSnip, 2026 (snippet-only)](https://sharksnip.com/blog/fantasy-2026-bye-week-streamer-stack-guide)
- *Don't over-weight:* "The roster you draft is not the roster you will actually be managing when most bye weeks arrive… there is a real cost to… avoiding a scheduling problem for one week." [Fantasy Football Blueprint, Aug 6, 2026 (snippet-only)](https://www.fantasyfootballblueprint.com/2026-08-06/bye-weeks-dont-matter-as-you-think/) Present both; the tiebreaker is draft round (middle rounds, where quality is roughly equal, byes matter most — [Fantasy Strategy Guide](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/bye-week-management)).

**2.9 Fixed percentage bands vs. no fixed bands; theory vs. practice.**

- *Published fixed bands* (25–40% / 5–15% / 1–3% of remaining FAAB; 30–40% past midpoint) come from [Calculator Collection, Aug 10, 2026](https://www.calculatorcollection.org/en/articles/faab-waiver-wire-strategy/) and [Fantasy Strategy Guide, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/faab-bidding-strategy) — the researchers note the exact bands originate from SEO-style secondary sites and are consistent with, but not independently re-derivable from, the named analysts, who give *fewer* hard numbers.
- *Rejection of fixed bands:* Zola — "bid 10% of your budget" isn't one-size-fits-all; use the league's own historical winning bids. [Fantasy Index, Apr 10, 2024](https://fantasyindex.com/2024/04/10/analysis/the-art-of-faab)
- *Theory vs. practice:* the (n−1)/n equilibrium (~90% of value at 10 teams) is the theoretical basis for bid shading, but no mainstream fantasy source publishes the formula — published practice (odd-number bids, +$1–$3, bid-$1-over-rival) is heuristic. [Wikipedia – First-price sealed-bid auction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-price_sealed-bid_auction); [Wikipedia – Bid shading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bid_shading)

## 3. What bad advice looks like

Ten concrete, checkable failure patterns a judge can test an assistant answer against:

1. **No channel/resource attribution.** The advice says "claim X" or "bid $Y" without stating which channel (rolling priority queue vs. FAAB bid pool) the move goes through in this hybrid league, and which resource it consumes (waiver position vs. remaining dollars). *Check: does every recommended action name its channel and its resource cost?* [Sleeper Support, Aug 14, 2024](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/9656662-what-types-of-waivers-do-you-support); [Sleeper, updated week of 2026-09-18](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3978868-waivers-for-regular-season-playoffs); [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026)
2. **Bid math against the initial $100 or held static.** Sizing as a share of *starting* budget, or giving one dollar figure that doesn't change with remaining budget and weeks left (e.g., "bid $20" in Week 14 with $25 left = 80% of what remains). *Check: is every bid figure expressed against remaining FAAB and weeks remaining?* [Calculator Collection, Aug 10, 2026](https://www.calculatorcollection.org/en/articles/faab-waiver-wire-strategy/); [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026)
3. **Undifferentiated bid bands — or a premium on a streamer.** One bid band for a season-changing starter, a mid-tier flex, and a lottery ticket; or spending premium resources (a top-of-queue claim or >~10% of FAAB) on a 1–2-week matchup play. *Check: does the advice split need tiers using the published 25–40% / 5–15% / 1–3% shape, and hold K/DEF to "$1 or nothing" / "$2 max"?* [Calculator Collection, Aug 10, 2026](https://www.calculatorcollection.org/en/articles/faab-waiver-wire-strategy/); [Fantasy Strategy Guide – FAAB, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/faab-bidding-strategy); [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026)
4. **Trending adds or an ownership spike treated as evidence of value.** Citing "12,000 adds" or "45% rostered" as a reason the player is good, rather than as a signal the market has *already moved* and the edge is closing. *Check: is any crowd metric framed as scarcity/bidding context, with the value case made on role/usage evidence?* [Fantasy Strategy Guide – Waiver Wire, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/waiver-wire-strategy); [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026); [Must Be Sunday, Sep 8, 2026](https://mustbesunday.com/2026/09/08/stop-chasing-week-1-points/)
5. **Ownership/trending numbers cited bare, or blended across platforms.** Reporting a % or add count without the platform, timestamp, and lookback window — or mixing ESPN and Sleeper numbers as if comparable (67% on one platform ≠ 41% on another). *Check: does every number carry platform + timestamp + window, and are cross-platform comparisons flagged?* [Sleeper API docs](https://sleeper-api-client.readthedocs.io/en/latest/endpoints/player.html); [Fantasy Player Database, undated; secondary](https://fantasyplayerdatabase.com/player-ownership-percentages)
6. **A drop based on last week's box score instead of role change.** Recommending to cut a player after one bad week without checking whether snap share / target share changed. *Check: does the drop rationale cite usage or injury data, and does it apply the four anti-recency questions before a big bid?* [Scoutcast.ai, updated Jul 8, 2026](https://scoutcast.ai/blog/fantasy-football-waiver-wire-strategy/); [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026)
7. **Waiver-mechanics blind.** Ignoring the 24-hour rule (advising a drop that will clear to *immediate* free agency — grabbable by any opponent without a claim — when the user needs the waiver period to land a player), ignoring the daily processing window (ESPN ~3 AM ET), or recommending a claim without verifying reachability: the player is on the wire, under the ownership reachability line (a ~93%-rostered player is "almost nobody can add"), and not already on the user's own roster. *Check: does the advice verify 24h status, processing timing, and availability — including that the team doesn't already own the target?* [ESPN, accessed 2026-09-18](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000071352-Claim-a-Player-Off-Waivers); [Sleeper, updated week of 2026-09-18](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3978868-waivers-for-regular-season-playoffs); [Must Be Sunday, Sep 15, 2026](https://mustbesunday.com/2026/09/15/week-2-waiver-wire-who-everyone-will-add-and-who-they-should/)
8. **Bye-week advice in the wrong week, or the wrong shape.** Recommending the bye fill *during* the conflict week instead of 1–2 weeks ahead; treating a double-stacked bye (two same-team players sharing a bye) as a one-slot problem recoverable with a single waiver add. *Check: does the advice lead the bye by 1–2 weeks, and flag 2-slot deficits as a draft/trade problem first?* [Fantasy Strategy Guide – Bye Week, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/bye-week-management); [Sleeper Blog, Aug 31, 2024](https://sleeper.com/blog/how-do-waivers-work-in-fantasy-football/)
9. **Blanket handcuff advice with no need context.** "Draft/claim a handcuff" without stating the starter's injury risk, the backup's depth-chart clarity (70–75%+ touches), bench depth, and league size — or blanket "never handcuff" that ignores the data-backed case for injury-prone RB1s and opponent-starter stashes. *Check: does the advice answer the two screening questions and the 10-team/short-bench caveat (23% payoff rate; wire deep enough to react)?* [FantasyDutchman, 2025](https://thefantasydutchman.com/drafting-handcuff-rbs-valuable-or-just-a-myth/); [NovaPredict, Jun 10, 2026](https://novapredict.com/blog/fantasy-football-handcuffs); [FantasySP, Jul 28, 2025](https://www.fantasysp.com/news/nfl/3055123/fantasy-football-draft-strategy-what-is)
10. **Format mismatch.** Applying 12-team, deep-roster, or 2QB/Superflex guidance to this 10-team single-QB, single-K, single-DEF, 5-bench PPR league — e.g., a positional-scarcity QB bid band meant for 2QB leagues, a second kicker as a default stash, a premium DEF bid justified by deep-league logic, or conditioning K/DEF advice on PPR when PPR doesn't change K/DEF scoring at all. *Check: is every rule keyed to the user's exact format, and are 12-team-benchmarked numbers (FantasyDutchman's top-24) relabeled as such?* [Fantasy Upside, Jun 22, 2026](https://fantasyupside.com/learn/how-faab-bidding-works); [FantasyDutchman, 2025](https://thefantasydutchman.com/drafting-handcuff-rbs-valuable-or-just-a-myth/); [ESPN – Scoring Formats](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360003914032-Scoring-Formats)

## 4. Glossary — terms the prompt should use consistently

| Term | Working definition (precise usage) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Rolling-priority waiver** | Queue system where each *successful claim* drops the claiming team to the bottom and everyone else moves up one; no weekly reset. Normalize "rolling priority," "continuous waivers." | [Sleeper, Aug 14, 2024](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/9656662-what-types-of-waivers-do-you-support) |
| **Reverse-standings waiver** | Priority resets each game week by current standings; worst team gets #1. Normalize "worst-to-first," "standings reset." | [ESPN Fan Support](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/4669787227668-Waiver-Order-Overview-and-Free-Agent-Budget-Tiebreakers) |
| **FAAB / remaining FAAB** | Free Agent Acquisition Budget — fixed season-long blind-bid pool (default $100); the winning bid is deducted from the *remaining* budget, which is the denominator for all sizing. | [Sleeper, Aug 25, 2024](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/9657110-how-do-faab-and-waivers-work) |
| **Channel decision** | The first classification of any candidate add: does this claim go through the priority queue (costs position) or the bid pool (costs dollars)? | [Sleeper, updated week of 2026-09-18](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3978868-waivers-for-regular-season-playoffs) |
| **Claim order / waiver position** | Where a team sits in the priority queue; consumed in order and reset to the bottom on a successful claim. | [Sleeper, Aug 14, 2024](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/9656662-what-types-of-waivers-do-you-support) |
| **Claim** | A submitted intention to add a specific player during the waiver window; if it succeeds, costs waiver position (rolling) or a bid amount (FAAB). | [Sleeper, Aug 14, 2024](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/9656662-what-types-of-waivers-do-you-support) |
| **Waiver window** | The period after that week's games conclude in which claims/bids are submitted before the platform processes them (ESPN processes daily ~3 AM ET). | [ESPN, accessed 2026-09-18](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000071352-Claim-a-Player-Off-Waivers) |
| **24-hour rule** | Dropped after <24h on a roster → immediate free agent; dropped after >24h → waiver wire; free-agent adds cost no priority. | [ESPN, accessed 2026-09-18](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000071352-Claim-a-Player-Off-Waivers); [Sleeper, updated week of 2026-09-18](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3978868-waivers-for-regular-season-playoffs) |
| **Free-agent add** | Adding a player who has cleared waivers (or never triggered the 24h rule); costs no waiver priority or FAAB. | [ESPN, accessed 2026-09-18](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000071352-Claim-a-Player-Off-Waivers) |
| **Replacement starter** | A waiver target whose starter ahead of him is out for a meaningful period and who inherits a *permanent* role — the highest-value waiver category. | [Fantasy Strategy Guide, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/waiver-wire-strategy) |
| **Handcuff** | A backup whose value is almost entirely conditional on a specific starter ahead of him being injured; pre-injury worth $0–2, post-injury a 25–40% bid. | [Fantasy Strategy Guide, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/waiver-wire-strategy); [NovaPredict, Jun 10, 2026](https://novapredict.com/blog/fantasy-football-handcuffs) |
| **Streamer** | A player added for 1–2 weeks of matchup-driven value, then dropped; should never consume premium waiver resources. | [Fantasy Strategy Guide, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/waiver-wire-strategy) |
| **Lottery ticket** | A player with real upside who needs a second injury or a demotion of a healthy player to become relevant; holds through cold weeks, minimum bid. | [Fantasy Strategy Guide, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/waiver-wire-strategy) |
| **Snipe / sniping** | Holding priority (or FAAB) to grab a specific player the moment he hits the wire; 4for4's 2026 position is that "sniping is dead" in competitive leagues. | [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026) |
| **Sunday Night Shop** | 4for4's named tactic: buying next week's target in *this* week's window or free-agency gap, before the market converges on it. | [4for4, Aug 28, 2026](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/ultimate-guide-winning-waiver-wire-2026) |
| **Ownership % / rostered %** | Share of leagues *on one specific platform* carrying a player at a point in time; a census of popularity/availability, never comparable across platforms without caveat; always cite platform + timestamp. | [Fantasy Player Database, undated; secondary](https://fantasyplayerdatabase.com/player-ownership-percentages) |
| **Trending adds** | Sleeper's raw add count across all Sleeper NFL leagues over a configurable lookback window (default 24h); a claim-velocity/crowd signal, not a projection. | [Sleeper Support, Jun 27, 2022](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/4584482-league-legend); [Sleeper API docs](https://sleeper-api-client.readthedocs.io/en/latest/endpoints/player.html) |
| **The 50% line** | The de facto availability threshold used operationally by Footballguys (K), ESPN (D/ST), and Must Be Sunday: below it a player is addable in most leagues; above it, the edge is largely gone. | [Footballguys, Sep 15, 2026](https://www.footballguys.com/article/2026-rent-a-kicker-week02); [ESPN 2026, ~Sep 2, 2026](https://www.espn.com.au/fantasy/football/story/_/id/49798496/2026-fantasy-football-d-st-road-map-early-season) |
| **Lag** | Strong role evidence (target share, snap share, injury opening) at low ownership — the buy-signal test for low-ownership players. | [Fantasy Player Database, undated; secondary](https://fantasyplayerdatabase.com/player-ownership-percentages) |
| **One-week spike** | An add/ownership surge driven by a single box score rather than a role change; the primary recency failure mode. | [Parlae, 2026 (snippet-only)](https://parlae.io/fantasy/guides/week-2-waiver-wire-roles-versus-spikes); [Must Be Sunday, Sep 8, 2026](https://mustbesunday.com/2026/09/08/stop-chasing-week-1-points/) |
| **Bye week** | The one week per NFL season in which an NFL team does not play; typically Weeks 5–14, with documented clustering (six teams on bye in Week 12 vs two in Week 9). | [Sleeper Blog, Aug 31, 2024](https://sleeper.com/blog/how-do-waivers-work-in-fantasy-football/) |
| **Double-stacked bye / 2-man slot** | Two rostered players (typically same NFL team) sharing a bye, creating a two-slot deficit in one week that is rarely recoverable via waiver adds in a 5-bench format. | [Fantasy Strategy Guide – Bye Week, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/bye-week-management) |
| **FLEX** | The lineup slot accepting any offensive position (RB/WR/TE); in this 10-team league it overlaps the second RB and WR slots, which is why bye overlap matters most at RB and WR. *(League spec; overlap mechanic per the 2-man-slot guidance above.)* | [Fantasy Strategy Guide – Bye Week, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/bye-week-management) |
| **Stretch run** | The fantasy-playoff stretch (Weeks 14–16 for this league, which sends 6 teams to playoffs from Week 15) — one of the three named triggers for a high-value (>15% of remaining FAAB) bid. | [Fantasy Strategy Guide – FAAB, undated; secondary](https://fantasystrategyguide.com/faab-bidding-strategy); [Calculator Collection, Aug 10, 2026](https://www.calculatorcollection.org/en/articles/faab-waiver-wire-strategy/) |
